# Pemphigus vulgaris vs bullous pemphigoid, and mycophenolate

## In one line
Pemphigus attacks the glue **between** keratinocytes (desmosomes), so the blister is inside the epidermis, flaccid and in the mouth; pemphigoid attacks the anchor **beneath** them (hemidesmosomes), so the blister is under the epidermis, tense and itchy in older adults.

## Core mechanism
1. Both are type II hypersensitivity: IgG autoantibodies bind a structural protein. Your notes list both under type II, and put pemphigus vulgaris in the "cellular dysfunction (non-cytotoxic)" group, where antibodies change how the target works instead of killing the cell (El-Husseiny Microbiology, pp. 453, 461).
2. **Pemphigus vulgaris:** IgG against desmoglein 3 (sometimes desmoglein 1), the cadherins that hold keratinocytes together. Cells separate (acantholysis) and blisters form in the lower epidermis. [MSD Manual: Pemphigus Vulgaris](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/dermatologic-disorders/bullous-diseases/pemphigus-vulgaris)
3. **Bullous pemphigoid:** IgG against hemidesmosome proteins BP230 (BPAg1) and BP180 (BPAg2), which anchor basal cells to the basement membrane. The whole epidermis lifts off (subepidermal split, in the lamina lucida). Your notes: the blisters are subepidermal and **nonacantholytic**, the oral mucosa is spared, and the disease is less severe than pemphigus vulgaris (El-Husseiny MSK, p. 178). [MSD Manual: Bullous Pemphigoid](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/dermatologic-disorders/bullous-diseases/bullous-pemphigoid)

## Compare and contrast
| Feature | Pemphigus vulgaris | Bullous pemphigoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Target | Desmoglein 3 (± 1), desmosomes | BP180, BP230, hemidesmosomes |
| Split | Within the epidermis (lower layers), acantholysis | Below the epidermis (lamina lucida) |
| Blisters | Flaccid, rupture easily, leave raw erosions | Tense, on trunk and flexures |
| Nikolsky sign | Positive (stroking the skin ruptures the thin bullae) | Negative |
| Asboe-Hansen sign | Positive (pressing on top spreads the bulla sideways) | — |
| Mouth | Often first (oral lesions precede skin) | Spared (your notes) or rare (MSD) |
| Age | Middle-aged | Usually over 60 |
| First symptom | Painful oral erosions | Itching (pruritus) |
| Direct immunofluorescence | IgG surrounding keratinocytes in a "fish net pattern" (El-Husseiny MSK, p. 177) | Linear IgG and complement along the basement membrane |
| Biopsy extras | Split just above the basal layer (suprabasal); basal cells stay attached to the basement membrane by hemidesmosomes, giving a "tombstone" appearance (El-Husseiny MSK, p. 177) | Many eosinophils and neutrophils |
| Drug triggers | — | Furosemide, spironolactone, omeprazole, PD-1/PD-L1 antibodies, DPP-4 inhibitors (gliptins, e.g. sitagliptin), others |

Sources: [MSD Manual: Pemphigus Vulgaris](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/dermatologic-disorders/bullous-diseases/pemphigus-vulgaris), [MSD Manual: Bullous Pemphigoid](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/dermatologic-disorders/bullous-diseases/bullous-pemphigoid); El-Husseiny MSK, pp. 176-178

## Mycophenolate mofetil
- **Mechanism:** its active form, mycophenolic acid, inhibits inosine monophosphate dehydrogenase (IMPDH), blocking the **de novo** pathway of guanosine nucleotide synthesis. Your notes: it "reversibly inhibits IMP dehydrogenase, preventing purine synthesis of B and T cells" (El-Husseiny Microbiology, p. 483); ribavirin shares the same target (El-Husseiny Biochemistry, p. 229). [FDA label: CellCept](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2015/050722s033%2C050723s032%2C050758s030%2C050759s038lbl.pdf)
- **Why lymphocytes:** T and B cells depend on de novo purine synthesis to proliferate; other cells can use salvage pathways (stated in the 2015 label; your notes say the drug targets purine synthesis "of B and T cells", El-Husseiny Microbiology, p. 483). [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2015/050722s033%2C050723s032%2C050758s030%2C050759s038lbl.pdf)
- **Use (labeled):** preventing rejection of kidney, heart and liver transplants in adults and children 3 months and older, in combination with other immunosuppressants. [DailyMed: CellCept label, rev. 7/2026](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=37241e87-4af4-4dc3-a1aa-ea6f20d8dc40); [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2015/050722s033%2C050723s032%2C050758s030%2C050759s038lbl.pdf)
- **Use (your notes):** lupus nephritis (El-Husseiny Microbiology, p. 483). **Other source:** lupus nephritis is not on the US label; it is an off-label use that is standard teaching and common on exams. Know both: transplant rejection prevention is the labeled use, lupus nephritis is the classic exam use.
- **Adverse effects:** diarrhea, vomiting, leukopenia, sepsis and opportunistic infections. Boxed warning: embryofetal toxicity (first-trimester loss, malformations), malignancy (lymphoma) and serious infections. Your notes add pancytopenia, hypertension and hyperglycemia, note it is less nephrotoxic and neurotoxic than the calcineurin inhibitors, and link it to **invasive CMV infection** (El-Husseiny Microbiology, p. 483). [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2015/050722s033%2C050723s032%2C050758s030%2C050759s038lbl.pdf)

## Clinical vignette
A 78-year-old man has weeks of intense itching followed by large, tense blisters on his abdomen and inner thighs. His mouth is spared. Rubbing nearby skin does not shear it. What will direct immunofluorescence show?

**Answer:** linear IgG and complement along the basement membrane zone (bullous pemphigoid).
**Why:** antibodies against hemidesmosomes (BP180/BP230) split the skin below the epidermis, so blisters are tense and Nikolsky is negative. [MSD Manual: Bullous Pemphigoid](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/dermatologic-disorders/bullous-diseases/bullous-pemphigoid)
**Trap:** choosing a cell-surface (intercellular) pattern, which is pemphigus.

## Common traps
- **D**esmosome = **D**esmoglein = pemphigus. **H**emidesmosome = deeper = pemphigoid.
- Flaccid blisters and mouth erosions = pemphigus; tense blisters, itch and old age = pemphigoid.
- Pemphigus = **acantholytic** (keratinocytes come apart); pemphigoid = **nonacantholytic** (El-Husseiny MSK, pp. 177-178).
- Mycophenolate blocks the **de novo** purine pathway (IMPDH); azathioprine is a prodrug of 6-mercaptopurine that blocks an earlier step (PRPP amidotransferase); its breakdown needs xanthine oxidase, so allopurinol raises its toxicity (El-Husseiny Biochemistry, p. 229; Microbiology, p. 483).
- Mycophenolate is teratogenic: think about pregnancy before prescribing.

## Practice questions
1. A 45-year-old woman has painful mouth erosions for 2 months, then flaccid skin blisters that slough with gentle pressure. Which protein is targeted?
   - **Answer:** desmoglein 3.
   - **Why:** pemphigus vulgaris; IgG against desmoglein 3 breaks keratinocyte adhesion. [MSD Manual](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/dermatologic-disorders/bullous-diseases/pemphigus-vulgaris)
   - **Trap:** BP180 is the pemphigoid target.
2. A biopsy shows a subepidermal blister full of eosinophils. Most likely diagnosis?
   - **Answer:** bullous pemphigoid.
   - **Why:** subepidermal split with many eosinophils and neutrophils. [MSD Manual](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/dermatologic-disorders/bullous-diseases/bullous-pemphigoid)
   - **Trap:** pemphigus splits within the epidermis.
3. Why does mycophenolate suppress lymphocytes more than other cells?
   - **Answer:** lymphocytes rely on the de novo purine pathway that IMPDH controls; other cells can salvage purines.
   - **Why:** mycophenolic acid blocks IMPDH. [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2015/050722s033%2C050723s032%2C050758s030%2C050759s038lbl.pdf)
   - **Trap:** choosing calcineurin inhibition (cyclosporine, tacrolimus).
4. A kidney transplant recipient on mycophenolate has persistent diarrhea and a white count of 2,500/µL. Explanation?
   - **Answer:** expected mycophenolate adverse effects (GI toxicity and leukopenia).
   - **Why:** diarrhea and leukopenia are among the principal adverse reactions. [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2015/050722s033%2C050723s032%2C050758s030%2C050759s038lbl.pdf)
   - **Trap:** blaming nephrotoxicity, which is the calcineurin inhibitors' signature; your notes say mycophenolate is "less nephrotoxic" (El-Husseiny Microbiology, p. 483).
