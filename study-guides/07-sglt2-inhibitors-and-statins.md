# SGLT2 inhibitors and statins: how they protect the heart and kidney

Exam focus: USMLE Step 1 and NBME · Study material, not patient care guidance · Auditor: cleared 2026-10-27
From the question log: "How do SGLT2 inhibitors and HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors (statins) exert their cardioprotective and renoprotective effects?"
Based on your notes: El-Husseiny's Essentials of Cardiovascular (statins pp. 212-213), Essentials of Nephrology (glucose threshold p. 39) and Pharmacology High Yield (SGLT2 blockers, PDF p. 152). Each fact is also checked against StatPearls and the MSD Manual; where a different source disagrees with your notes, or newer guidance has changed, a marked note says so.

## In one line
SGLT2 inhibitors dump glucose and sodium into the urine, which unloads the heart and, through tubuloglomerular feedback, lowers glomerular pressure. Statins block cholesterol synthesis in the liver, which pulls LDL out of the blood and stabilizes plaques.

## Core mechanism: SGLT2 inhibitors (the "-gliflozins")
1. The sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) sits in the **proximal convoluted tubule** and reabsorbs most filtered glucose along with sodium. The drugs cut glucose reabsorption by about 30-60%. [StatPearls: SGLT2 Inhibitors](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK576405/)
2. **Kidney:** more sodium reaches the macula densa. Tubuloglomerular feedback reads this as "too much filtration" and constricts the **afferent** arteriole, lowering glomerular pressure, so less albuminuria and slower chronic kidney disease (CKD). [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK576405/)
3. **Heart:** sodium loss and glucose-driven osmotic diuresis lower preload; arterial vasodilation lowers afterload. [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK576405/)
4. Benefit in heart failure holds whether or not the patient has diabetes; KDIGO 2024 strongly recommends them to slow CKD (guideline recommendation as reported in StatPearls; standard teaching). [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK576405/)

5. **Your notes on the glucose threshold (El-Husseiny Nephrology, p. 39):** glucose starts spilling into the urine at a plasma level of about **200 mg/dL**, and all carriers are saturated (the transport maximum) at about **375 mg/min**. SGLT2 inhibitors make glucose appear in the urine **below 200 mg/dL**. Pregnancy does something similar: the higher GFR lowers the threshold, so glucosuria can occur at normal blood glucose.
6. **Your pharmacology notes (Pharmacology High Yield, PDF p. 152):** SGLT2 in the proximal tubule handles about 90% of glucose reabsorption; drugs include canagliflozin, dapagliflozin and empagliflozin; adverse effects listed are urinary tract infection and a risk of bladder and breast cancer.
- **Other source (cancer risk):** StatPearls says a pooled analysis of 22 trials "indicated a possible association between dapagliflozin and bladder cancer" and advises against dapagliflozin with hematuria or a history of bladder cancer; it does not mention breast cancer. [StatPearls: SGLT2 Inhibitors](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK576405/) (Your notes also list sergliflozin, which was never marketed in the US; standard teaching.)
- **Recent change:** neither of your notes lists heart failure or kidney uses. Dapagliflozin and empagliflozin now carry FDA indications for heart failure at any ejection fraction and for chronic kidney disease at risk of progression, with or without diabetes. [StatPearls: SGLT2 Inhibitors](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK576405/)

## Core mechanism: statins
1. Statins competitively inhibit HMG-CoA reductase, the rate-limiting step of cholesterol synthesis (HMG-CoA to mevalonate). [StatPearls: HMG-CoA Reductase Inhibitors](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK542212/)
2. Less cholesterol inside hepatocytes increases LDL receptor transcription, so the liver pulls more LDL and VLDL out of the blood. [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK542212/)
3. Beyond LDL lowering ("pleiotropic" effects): they keep the fibrous cap of plaques intact, reduce inflammatory cytokines and increase endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS) activity. [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK542212/)
4. **Your notes (El-Husseiny Cardiovascular, pp. 212-213):** statins (atorvastatin, rosuvastatin, "-statins") are first-line for high cholesterol. They block HMG-CoA reductase, the rate-limiting step of liver cholesterol synthesis, so LDL receptors go up and LDL is cleared; less VLDL also lowers triglycerides. They are the most effective lipid drugs for preventing cardiovascular events, and after an MI they cut both repeat MI and death, through LDL lowering **and** plaque stabilization.
5. **Side effects in your notes:** myalgia is common and does **not** raise creatine kinase (CK); myositis raises CK and usually comes with higher doses; rhabdomyolysis can cause acute kidney failure; check liver enzymes before starting. Fibrates (especially **gemfibrozil**, which slows statin clearance in the liver) and niacin raise the myopathy risk. Most statins are cleared by cytochrome P450, **except pravastatin**, so P450 inhibitors raise the risk of myopathy (El-Husseiny Cardiovascular, pp. 212-213).
6. Statins are **not** a kidney-protective drug the way SGLT2 inhibitors are; the cited review lists kidney risks (proteinuria, hematuria at high intensity) and names statins that need no dose change in CKD. [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK542212/)

## Compare and contrast
| Feature | SGLT2 inhibitors | Statins |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Target | SGLT2, proximal convoluted tubule | HMG-CoA reductase, liver |
| Main effect | Glucosuria and natriuresis | Lower LDL (also lower triglycerides, slightly higher HDL) |
| Heart benefit | Less preload and afterload; fewer HF hospitalizations and deaths | Fewer atherosclerotic events via LDL lowering and plaque stabilization |
| Kidney benefit | Afferent constriction via tubuloglomerular feedback lowers glomerular pressure and albuminuria | No direct kidney protection: in CKD statins lower cardiovascular events but do not slow CKD progression (SHARP trial; standard teaching) |
| Key adverse effects | Genital yeast infections, UTIs, volume depletion, euglycemic DKA, Fournier gangrene (rare) | Myalgia (most common), myositis with high CK, rhabdomyolysis (rare, most serious), raised transaminases, slight diabetes risk |
| Interactions / avoid | Volume-depleted patients | Gemfibrozil (rhabdomyolysis); CYP3A4 inhibitors with simvastatin, lovastatin, atorvastatin; pregnancy: stop in most patients. Sources differ: MSD still lists statins as contraindicated in pregnancy, but in 2021 the FDA removed the class-wide contraindication while still advising most pregnant patients to stop ([FDA Drug Safety Communication, 20 July 2021](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-and-availability/fda-requests-removal-strongest-warning-against-using-cholesterol-lowering-statins-during-pregnancy)); avoid while breastfeeding |

Sources: [StatPearls: SGLT2 Inhibitors](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK576405/), [StatPearls: HMG-CoA Reductase Inhibitors](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK542212/). MSD confirms the SGLT2 site (proximal tubule), fewer HF hospitalizations and deaths, slower CKD progression and the SGLT2 adverse effects: [MSD Manual: Medications for Diabetes](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/diabetes-mellitus-and-hypoglycemia/medications-for-diabetes-mellitus-treatment). MSD confirms statin myositis/rhabdomyolysis, gemfibrozil and CYP3A4-inhibitor interactions: [MSD Manual: Dyslipidemia](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/lipid-disorders/dyslipidemia). Statin pregnancy and breastfeeding advice: [FDA, 2021](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-and-availability/fda-requests-removal-strongest-warning-against-using-cholesterol-lowering-statins-during-pregnancy).

## High-yield facts
- SGLT2 inhibitors and ACE inhibitors both lower glomerular pressure, from opposite ends: SGLT2 inhibitors constrict the **afferent** arteriole; ACE inhibitors dilate the **efferent** arteriole. SGLT2 half: [StatPearls: SGLT2 Inhibitors](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK576405/), [Primary Care Notebook: SGLT2 inhibitors, kidney protection](https://primarycarenotebook.com/pages/renal-medicine/sglt2-inhibitors-proposed-mechanism-for-kidney-protection) (citing Benzing and Salant, N Engl J Med 2021;384:1437-46) ("afferent vasoconstriction"). ACE inhibitor half: [StatPearls: ACE Inhibitors](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK431051/); the Primary Care Notebook page also describes RAAS blockade preventing "angiotensin II-mediated constriction of the efferent arteriole". See also [03-ace-inhibitors-renal-artery-stenosis.md](03-ace-inhibitors-renal-artery-stenosis.md).
- Euglycemic DKA: an anion gap metabolic acidosis with ketones but glucose that is not very high, because glucose keeps leaving in the urine. [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK576405/)
- Dapagliflozin and empagliflozin carry heart failure and CKD indications. [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK576405/)
- CYP3A4-metabolized statins: simvastatin, lovastatin, atorvastatin; pravastatin and rosuvastatin are safer with CYP3A4 inhibitors. [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK542212/)
- **Other source (which statins avoid P450):** your El-Husseiny notes name only pravastatin as the exception (Cardiovascular, p. 213), and your pharmacology question bank marks **clarithromycin** as the drug not to combine with rosuvastatin (Pharmacology High Yield, PDF p. 131). StatPearls groups rosuvastatin with pravastatin as safer with CYP3A4 inhibitors. Rosuvastatin is less affected, not immune; if a question names one statin that skips P450, pick pravastatin (standard teaching).

## Clinical vignette
A 58-year-old woman with type 2 diabetes and CKD starts empagliflozin. Two weeks later, her eGFR has dipped slightly and her urine albumin is lower. Which change in glomerular hemodynamics explains both findings?

**Answer:** afferent arteriole constriction through tubuloglomerular feedback, lowering intraglomerular pressure.
**Why:** blocking proximal sodium reabsorption raises sodium delivery to the macula densa, which constricts the afferent arteriole. Lower glomerular pressure slightly reduces GFR and reduces albumin leak. [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK576405/)
**Trap:** choosing efferent dilation, which is how ACE inhibitors and ARBs lower glomerular pressure.

## Common traps
- SGLT2 = proximal tubule. Not the collecting duct, not the loop.
- Statins raise LDL receptors; they do not block absorption. Ezetimibe is the cholesterol absorption inhibitor ([MSD Manual: Dyslipidemia](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/lipid-disorders/dyslipidemia)); it blocks absorption of dietary and biliary cholesterol in the gut (NPC1L1 transporter, standard teaching).
- Muscle pain on a statin plus gemfibrozil: think rhabdomyolysis.
- Muscle aches with a **normal CK** = myalgia; aches with a **high CK** = myositis (El-Husseiny Cardiovascular, p. 212).
- Glucose in the urine with a blood glucose below 200 mg/dL: think SGLT2 inhibitor or pregnancy (El-Husseiny Nephrology, p. 39).
- A patient on an SGLT2 inhibitor with acidosis and a near-normal glucose can still have DKA.

## Practice questions
1. A patient on dapagliflozin has nausea, deep breathing, pH 7.21, anion gap 22, glucose 190 mg/dL and positive serum ketones. Diagnosis?
   - **Answer:** euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis.
   - **Why:** high anion gap acidosis with ketones; glucose stays only mildly high because SGLT2 inhibition keeps excreting it. [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK576405/)
   - **Trap:** ruling out DKA because glucose is below 250.
2. Statins lower plasma LDL mainly by which change in the liver?
   - **Answer:** more LDL receptors on hepatocytes.
   - **Why:** lower intracellular cholesterol increases LDL receptor transcription, clearing LDL from plasma. [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK542212/)
   - **Trap:** "decreased LDL production" alone misses the receptor upregulation that boards ask about.
3. A patient on simvastatin starts clarithromycin and develops severe muscle pain and dark urine. Why?
   - **Answer:** clarithromycin inhibits CYP3A4, raising simvastatin levels and causing rhabdomyolysis.
   - **Why:** simvastatin is a CYP3A4 substrate; inhibitors raise statin levels and myotoxicity. [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK542212/)
   - **Trap:** pravastatin and rosuvastatin are not CYP3A4-dependent. (Clarithromycin as a CYP3A4 inhibitor is standard pharmacology, not on the cited page.)
4. Through which nephron segment and transporter do SGLT2 inhibitors act?
   - **Answer:** the proximal convoluted tubule, SGLT2.
   - **Why:** SGLT2 reabsorbs filtered glucose with sodium in the proximal tubule. [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK576405/)
   - **Trap:** confusing it with thiazides (distal convoluted tubule) or loop diuretics (thick ascending limb).
