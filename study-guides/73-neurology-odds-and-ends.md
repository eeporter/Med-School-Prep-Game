# Neurology odds and ends: anterior horn disease, Klüver-Bucy, touch receptors, vomiting, jugular foramen, blowout fracture, muscle relaxants

Exam focus: USMLE Step 1 and NBME · Study material, not patient care guidance · Auditor: cleared 2026-11-02
Based on your notes: El-Husseiny's Essentials of Neurology (pp. 30, 86, 132-133, 142, 145, 148, 208, 236-237). These topics did not fit any earlier guide. Each fact is also checked against StatPearls and the MSD Manual; where a newer source disagrees with your notes, a marked note says so.

## In one line
**Spinal muscular atrophy and polio** destroy anterior horn cells: pure lower motor neuron weakness with normal sensation. **Bilateral amygdala damage** causes Klüver-Bucy syndrome. **CN IX, X and XI** run through the jugular foramen. **A blow to the eye** breaks the thin orbital floor and traps the inferior rectus. **Succinylcholine** stays on the receptor and depolarizes; **rocuronium** blocks the receptor.

## Core mechanism
1. **Anterior horn cells** are the cell bodies of lower motor neurons. Damage gives flaccid weakness, low reflexes, fasciculations and atrophy (El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 30).
2. **The area postrema** sits outside the blood-brain barrier, so it can detect toxins in the blood and trigger vomiting (p. 145).
3. **Cerebral perfusion pressure:** CPP = MAP - ICP (p. 148). [MSD Manual: Traumatic Brain Injury](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/injuries-poisoning/traumatic-brain-injury-tbi/traumatic-brain-injury-tbi)
4. **CO2 sets brain blood flow:** high PCO2 dilates brain vessels and low PCO2 constricts them, so hyperventilation briefly lowers ICP (p. 148).

## High-yield facts

### Spinal muscular atrophy (SMA)
- **Your notes (p. 30):** autosomal recessive, congenital degeneration of the anterior horns; a floppy baby with lower motor neuron signs. Your notes call it Werdnig-Hoffmann disease and give a median age at death of 7 months.
- MSD names the gene: *SMN1*. Type 1 infants have hypotonia, low reflexes and **tongue fasciculations**. [MSD Manual: Spinal Muscular Atrophies](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/neurologic-disorders/peripheral-nervous-system-and-motor-unit-disorders/spinal-muscular-atrophies-smas)
- **Recent change:** there are now disease-modifying drugs: nusinersen, risdiplam and onasemnogene abeparvovec. MSD lists all three, so older survival figures do not describe treated infants.

### Poliomyelitis (p. 30)
- Fecal-oral spread; the virus replicates in the throat and gut, then reaches the CNS through the blood.
- Destroys anterior horn cells. Unlike Guillain-Barré syndrome, the damage is irreversible.
- Fever and malaise, then flaccid paralysis, low reflexes, fasciculations and atrophy.
- CSF: more white cells, slightly raised protein and normal glucose.
- MSD describes "asymmetric flaccid limb paralysis or bulbar palsies without sensory loss". [MSD Manual: Poliomyelitis](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/infectious-diseases/enteroviruses/poliomyelitis)

### Klüver-Bucy syndrome (pp. 132-133)
Bilateral amygdala lesions cause hyperorality, hyperphagia and hypersexuality. StatPearls describes bilateral temporal lobe lesions, especially the hippocampus and amygdala. [StatPearls: Kluver-Bucy Syndrome](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK544221/) Herpes simplex encephalitis is a classic cause because it favors the temporal lobe (p. 162).

### Sensory receptors (p. 142)
| Receptor | Location | Senses | Adapts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free nerve endings (Aδ, C fibers) | All skin, some viscera | Pain, temperature | |
| Meissner corpuscle | Hairless skin | Fine touch, low-frequency vibration | Quickly |
| Merkel disc | Fingertips, superficial skin | Static touch, shapes, edges | Slowly |
| Pacinian corpuscle | Deep skin, ligaments, joints | Pressure, high-frequency vibration | Quickly |
| Ruffini corpuscle | Fingertips, joints | Stretch, slippage, joint angle | Slowly |

[StatPearls: Physiology, Mechanoreceptors](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK541068/) lists the same four touch receptors.

### Vomiting (p. 145)
- The **nucleus tractus solitarius** in the medulla coordinates vomiting.
- Inputs: the **chemoreceptor trigger zone** in the area postrema, the GI tract through the vagus nerve, the vestibular system and the CNS.
- Receptors: M1, D2, H1, 5-HT3 and NK-1.
- **Chemotherapy vomiting:** 5-HT3, D2 and NK-1 antagonists. **Motion sickness:** H1 and M1 antagonists.
- [MSD Manual: Motion Sickness](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/injuries-poisoning/motion-sickness/motion-sickness) names the same pathways and says scopolamine is effective for prevention.

### Jugular foramen (Vernet) syndrome (p. 86)
| Nerve | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| CN IX | Loss of taste on the back third of the tongue, loss of gag reflex |
| CN X | Hoarseness, swallowing trouble, uvula pointing away from the lesion |
| CN XI | Weak sternocleidomastoid and trapezius |

Causes include tumors, trauma and infection. [StatPearls: Jugular Foramen Syndrome](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK549871/)

### Orbital blowout fracture (p. 208)
- A blunt blow raises orbital pressure, which breaks the thin medial wall or floor. The floor separates the orbit from the maxillary sinus.
- Orbital contents can herniate into the sinus.
- **Double vision and limited upward gaze** come from inferior rectus entrapment. Numbness below the eye suggests trigeminal branch damage, and a sunken eye is enophthalmos. [StatPearls: Orbital Floor Fracture](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK534825/)

### Neuromuscular blockers (pp. 236-237)
| | Succinylcholine | Rocuronium, vecuronium, atracurium |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Type | Depolarizing nicotinic agonist | Competitive nicotinic antagonist |
| Action | Sustained depolarization, then desensitization | Blocks ACh binding |
| Broken down by | Pseudocholinesterase | |
| Reversal | None in phase I; AChE inhibitors may worsen it | Neostigmine with atropine or glycopyrrolate |
| Risks | Hyperkalemia, malignant hyperthermia, long paralysis with faulty pseudocholinesterase | Myasthenia patients are extremely sensitive |

- [StatPearls: Neuromuscular Blocking Agents](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK537168/), [StatPearls: Succinylcholine](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK499984/), [StatPearls: Muscarinic Antagonists](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK557541/).
- **Dantrolene** blocks the ryanodine receptor and stops calcium release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum. It treats malignant hyperthermia and neuroleptic malignant syndrome (p. 237).

### Hyperventilation and ICP (p. 148)
Hyperventilation lowers PCO2, which constricts brain vessels and lowers blood volume and ICP.

**Other source:** MSD warns that this also lowers brain perfusion and can worsen ischemia, so it reserves hyperventilation for the first several hours and for ICP unresponsive to other measures. [MSD Manual: Traumatic Brain Injury](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/injuries-poisoning/traumatic-brain-injury-tbi/traumatic-brain-injury-tbi)

## Compare and contrast: weakness from anterior horn disease
| | SMA type 1 | Polio | ALS | Guillain-Barré syndrome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Age | Infant | Any, usually unvaccinated | Adult | Any |
| Cause | *SMN1* mutation | Poliovirus | Degeneration | Autoimmune demyelination |
| UMN signs | No | No | Yes | No |
| Sensory loss | No | No | No | Mild |
| Recovery | No | Irreversible at lost neurons | No | Usually |

Source: El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 30; guides 16 and 25.

## Clinical vignette
A 28-year-old man is hit in the eye by a squash ball. He has double vision when looking up, and the eye does not elevate fully. CT shows soft tissue in the maxillary sinus. Which structure is trapped?

**Answer:** the inferior rectus muscle.
**Why:** the thin orbital floor gives way, and the muscle caught in the fracture stops the eye from moving up. (El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 208; [StatPearls: Orbital Floor Fracture](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK534825/))
**Trap:** a CN IV palsy, which gives trouble looking down when the eye is turned in.

## Common traps
- **Polio spares sensation.** Numbness points elsewhere.
- **Amygdala means Klüver-Bucy; hippocampus means anterograde amnesia; mammillary bodies mean Korsakoff.** (p. 133)
- **A CN X lesion moves the uvula away from the lesion**; a CN XII lesion moves the tongue toward it. (p. 86)
- **Neostigmine does not reverse early succinylcholine block**; it can worsen it. (p. 236)
- **Do not confuse Meissner with Merkel:** Meissner adapts quickly, Merkel slowly.
- **Hyperventilation lowers ICP but also lowers brain blood flow.**

## Practice questions
1. A patient has a mean arterial pressure of 85 mm Hg and an intracranial pressure of 25 mm Hg. What is the cerebral perfusion pressure?
   - **Answer:** 60 mm Hg.
   - **Why:** CPP = MAP - ICP = 85 - 25 = 60. (El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 148)
   - **Trap:** adding the two numbers.
2. A 2-month-old with a weak cry, areflexia and tongue fasciculations has normal facial expression and alert eyes. Which cells are lost?
   - **Answer:** anterior horn cells.
   - **Why:** this is SMA type 1. (El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 30; [MSD Manual: Spinal Muscular Atrophies](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/neurologic-disorders/peripheral-nervous-system-and-motor-unit-disorders/spinal-muscular-atrophies-smas))
   - **Trap:** the neuromuscular junction, as in infant botulism, which adds ptosis and constipation.
3. A surgical patient remains paralyzed for hours after a dose of succinylcholine. What is the most likely cause?
   - **Answer:** an inherited defect in pseudocholinesterase.
   - **Why:** this enzyme normally clears the drug quickly. (El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 236; [StatPearls: Succinylcholine](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK499984/))
   - **Trap:** malignant hyperthermia, which causes rigidity and fever.
4. After herpes encephalitis, a 35-year-old puts objects in his mouth, overeats and is sexually disinhibited. Where is the lesion?
   - **Answer:** both amygdalae.
   - **Why:** (El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 132; [StatPearls: Kluver-Bucy Syndrome](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK544221/))
   - **Trap:** the frontal lobes, which also disinhibit but do not cause hyperorality.
5. A 55-year-old has hoarseness, trouble swallowing and a droopy shoulder on one side. Which skull opening is affected?
   - **Answer:** the jugular foramen.
   - **Why:** CN IX, X and XI pass through it. (El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 86; [StatPearls: Jugular Foramen Syndrome](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK549871/))
   - **Trap:** the hypoglossal canal, which affects the tongue.
