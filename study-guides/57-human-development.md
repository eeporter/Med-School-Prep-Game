Abbreviations: **CDC** = Centers for Disease Control and Prevention; **AAP** = American Academy of Pediatrics; **GnRH** = gonadotropin-releasing hormone; **LH** = luteinizing hormone; **FSH** = follicle-stimulating hormone; **UMN** = upper motor neuron; **SGA/LGA** = small/large for gestational age; **IM** = intramuscular.

## In one line
Development runs in a fixed **order**, with timing that varies (MSD). Learn a few anchor ages:
- social smile by 2 months;
- rolls and laughs by 6 months;
- stranger anxiety and sitting alone by 9 months;
- pincer grasp, pulling to stand and "mama/dada" by 12 months;
- walking alone and 3+ words by 18 months;
- 2-word phrases and running by 2 years;
- copying a circle and talking in short conversations by 3 years;
- hopping on one foot by 5 years.

These come from the CDC 2022 checklists, which list what **75% or more** of children do by each age. Most **primitive reflexes are gone by about 6 months**, and persistence suggests cerebral palsy (StatPearls). Puberty starts with **breast budding in girls** and **testicular enlargement in boys** (MSD; Osmosis Physiology p. 586).

## Newborn
**Published research (thin area).**

**Apgar score (MSD: Initial Evaluation and Preventive Care in the Healthy Term Newborn, Jun 2026)**
- It is scored at **1 and 5 minutes**. Five items get 0-2 points each:

| Item | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A**ppearance (color) | Blue or pale all over | **Pink body, blue extremities** | Pink all over |
| **P**ulse | Absent | **< 100/min** | > 100/min |
| **G**rimace (reflex response) | None | Grimace | Sneeze or cough |
| **A**ctivity (tone) | Limp | Some flexion | Active movement |
| **R**espiration | Absent | Slow, irregular | Good, crying |

- At 5 minutes, **7-10 is normal, 4-6 intermediate and 0-3 low**. A score under 7 at 5 minutes is linked to higher illness and death rates in population studies.
- **The Apgar score is not used to decide whether to start resuscitation.** Heart rate, breathing effort and tone are used directly instead (MSD).

**Routine newborn care (MSD)**
- **Vitamin K (phytonadione) 1 mg IM** within 6 hours of birth, for infants over 1500 g.
- **Erythromycin 0.5% eye ointment** in both eyes, to prevent gonococcal eye infection (ophthalmia neonatorum).
- Hepatitis B vaccine is also routine (MSD; schedule details not covered here).

**Size at birth**
- **SGA** is a birth weight below the **10th percentile** for gestational age. **LGA** is above the **90th** (MSD: Growth Parameters in Neonates).
- Preterm is birth before 37 weeks, and low birth weight is under 2500 g (standard teaching).

**Primitive reflexes (StatPearls: Primitive Reflexes)**
| Reflex | How to elicit | Response | Gone by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Moro** (startle) | Lift the arms slightly off the bed and release | Extends the arms with fingers extended, flexes the hips and cries (MSD) | **6 months** |
| **Rooting** | Stroke the cheek | Turns toward the touch and opens the mouth | Fades after **1 month** |
| **Sucking** | Touch the mouth | Sucks | Present from 14 weeks of gestation |
| **Palmar grasp** | Press the palm | Fingers flex around the object | **6 months** |
| **Asymmetric tonic neck** ("fencer") | Turn the head to one side | Arm on the facing side extends; the other arm flexes | **3 months** |
| **Babinski** | Stroke the outer sole | Big toe up, toes fan | Normal in infants. In an **adult it means a UMN lesion** (StatPearls); it usually disappears by about 1-2 years (standard teaching) |

- **Persistence past 4-6 months, or absence of a reflex that should be there, predicts cerebral palsy** (StatPearls).
- Primitive reflexes **reappearing in adults** ("frontal release signs") can point to frontal lobe disease or dementia (StatPearls).
- Reflex ages differ a little between sources. The safe exam rule is that **most are gone by about 6 months**.

## Developmental milestones (CDC checklists, 2022)
**Published research (thin area):** the [CDC milestone checklists](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/media/pdfs/2025/10/cdc-milestone-checklists-ltsae-english-508.pdf) list what "most children (75% or more) can do by each age."

| Age | Motor | Language | Social and cognitive |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **2 mo** | Holds head up on tummy | Makes sounds other than crying | **Smiles** when you talk or smile; looks at your face |
| **4 mo** | Holds head steady when held; pushes up on forearms; brings hands to mouth | **Coos**; turns to your voice | Smiles on own to get attention; chuckles |
| **6 mo** | **Rolls tummy to back**; leans on hands when sitting | Takes turns making sounds; blows raspberries | **Laughs**; knows familiar people; reaches for a toy |
| **9 mo** | **Sits without support**; moves objects hand to hand; rakes food | "Mamamama, babababa" (babbling); lifts arms to be picked up | **Shy or fearful with strangers**; reacts when the parent leaves; peekaboo; **looks for a dropped object** |
| **12 mo** | **Pulls to stand**; walks holding furniture; **picks up small food between thumb and pointer finger** (pincer) | **"Mama/dada"** for a parent; waves bye-bye; understands "no" | Plays pat-a-cake; looks for a toy hidden under a blanket |
| **15 mo** | **Takes a few steps alone** | Tries 1-2 words besides mama/dada; **points to ask** | Shows affection; copies other children |
| **18 mo** | **Walks alone**; scribbles; climbs on and off a couch | **3 or more words**; follows one-step directions without gestures | Points to show something interesting; copies chores |
| **2 yr** | **Runs**; kicks a ball; walks up a few stairs | **2-word phrases** ("More milk"); points to 2 body parts | Notices when others are upset |
| **3 yr** | Puts on loose clothes; uses a fork | **Conversation** with 2 or more back-and-forth exchanges; asks who, what, where, why; **understood by others most of the time** | **Draws a circle** when shown; joins other children to play |
| **4 yr** | Catches a large ball; holds a crayon with fingers | **Sentences of 4+ words** | Pretend play; names a few colors; draws a person with 3+ parts |
| **5 yr** | **Hops on one foot**; buttons some buttons | Tells a story with 2+ events; rhymes | Counts to 10; follows game rules |

- **Stranger anxiety** is normal around 9-12 months. CDC lists "shy, clingy, or fearful around strangers" at 9 months. DSM-5-TR calls heightened separation anxiety around age 1 year part of normal development and a sign of secure attachment (p. 219).
- MSD dates separation anxiety from about **8 months**, and it can last many months (MSD: Childhood Development).
- **Handedness** shows by about 3 years. Earlier strong hand preference is a red flag (standard teaching). MSD lists "favors using one hand" at 3 years.

**Recent change (milestones, 2022):** the CDC and AAP revised the milestone checklists in 2022 (Zubler et al., *Pediatrics* 2022;149(3):e2021052138).
- **What changed:**
  - Milestones are now set at the age **75% or more** of children reach them. Older lists used the **50th percentile** (median).
  - **15- and 30-month** checklists were added.
  - About a third of the kept milestones **moved, mostly to older ages** ([Contemporary Pediatrics, 16 Mar 2022](https://www.contemporarypediatrics.com/view/cdc-aap-update-developmental-surveillance-tools)).
- **Why it matters:** with the 75% cutoff, a child who **misses a CDC milestone at the listed age** is behind most peers and should get developmental screening. The checklists support routine screening; they don't replace it.
- **For exam answers:** older review tables and the MSD table (which gives median ages) list some skills earlier. For example, MSD puts sitting unsupported at 7 months, while CDC lists it by 9 months. Questions that ask what is **typical** at an age often use the earlier median ages. Questions that ask whether a child is **delayed** fit the CDC ages.

## Growth (MSD: Physical Growth of Infants and Children)
- Newborns lose **5-8% of birth weight** in the first days and **regain it within 2 weeks**.
- Weight **doubles by about 5 months** and **triples by 12 months**. Length rises more than 50% (about 25 cm) in the first year.
- Head circumference grows about **1 cm per month** in the first year. The brain reaches about 75% of adult size by 12 months.
- **Teeth:** about 6 teeth by 12 months, 16 by 2 years and all **20 by 2½ years**.
- **Sleep need falls with age:** infants sleep about 16 hours a day, adults 7.5-8.5 hours (Osmosis Physiology p. 459).

## Puberty (Osmosis Physiology pp. 586-587; MSD)
- **Trigger:** the hypothalamus starts releasing **GnRH in pulses**. This drives pulsatile **LH and FSH**, and the pituitary becomes more sensitive to GnRH (Osmosis p. 586).
  - In males, LH acts on **Leydig cells** to make testosterone, and FSH acts on **Sertoli cells** to support sperm production.
- **Age at onset:** about **10-14 in girls** and **12-16 in boys** (Osmosis p. 586).
- **Terms:**
  - **Thelarche**: breast budding (estradiol).
  - **Pubarche**: pubic hair (adrenal androgens).
  - **Adrenarche**: rising adrenal androgens.
  - **Menarche**: first period. The first cycles are often **anovulatory**.
  - **Spermarche**: first sperm production (Osmosis p. 586).
- **First sign:** **breast budding in girls**, closely followed by the growth spurt. **Testicular and scrotal enlargement in boys**, then penile growth (MSD).
- **Menarche** comes about **2 years after breast budding**, after peak height growth. Most US girls start at 12-13 (MSD).
- **Peak height velocity:** girls reach it at **11-12½**, about 2 years earlier than boys (**13-14**). Boys' growth spurt starts about a year after testicular enlargement (MSD).
- **Tanner stages** (Osmosis p. 587):

| Stage | Both sexes (pubic hair) | Boys | Girls |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | None | Small penis and testes | Flat chest |
| 2 | Soft hair appears | **Testes enlarge** | **Breast buds** |
| 3 | Coarser hair | Penis lengthens | Breast mound |
| 4 | Covers the pubic area | Penis widens | "Mound-on-mound" contour |
| 5 | Spreads to the inner thigh | Adult size | Adult contour |

**Precocious puberty (MSD: Precocious Puberty, Aug 2026)**
- **Definition:** puberty starting **before age 8 in girls** or **before age 9 in boys**.
- **Central (GnRH-dependent) is more common.** In most girls no cause is found. **Boys are more likely to have a brain lesion** (for example a hypothalamic or pineal tumor): 13% of boys vs 2% of girls in one study. MSD says brain MRI may be done to exclude a brain lesion in boys with GnRH-dependent precocious puberty, and is not routinely needed in girls aged 6-8 without symptoms.
- **Peripheral (GnRH-independent):** high sex hormones without activation of the axis. It is much rarer.
- **Tests:** a **morning LH** is the best first test. If the result is unclear, a GnRH-agonist (leuprolide) stimulation test helps; a rise in LH means central.
- **Treatment of central precocious puberty:** a **GnRH agonist** (leuprolide, triptorelin or a histrelin implant).

**Other source (precocious puberty cutoffs):** MSD notes that the 8 and 9 year cutoffs are being reevaluated because US girls are starting puberty earlier. For exams, use **before 8 (girls) and before 9 (boys)**.

## Menopause (Osmosis Physiology p. 572)
- **Diagnosis:** **no periods for a full year** with no other cause. It usually begins **around age 50**.
- **Perimenopause** comes first, about **4 years** before the last period, with irregular cycles.
- **Cause:** the ovaries run out of follicles. This leads to **low estrogen and progesterone**, so the hypothalamus loses its negative feedback, and **FSH and LH rise**.
- **Symptoms:**
  - **hot flashes and night sweats**, from vasomotor instability in the hypothalamus;
  - **vaginal dryness and atrophy**, with pain during sex;
  - urinary urgency;
  - mood changes;
  - thinner skin;
  - loss of lean mass.
- **Long-term risks:** **osteoporosis** and **cardiovascular disease**. Calcium and vitamin D help bone.
- **Treatment:** hormone therapy is individualized. Vaginal estrogen treats atrophy.

## Normal aging (FA p. 270)
- **Sexual function:**
  - **Men:** slower erection and ejaculation and a **longer refractory period**, but **libido is unchanged**.
  - **Women:** vaginal shortening, thinning and dryness.
- **Sleep:** **less REM and slow-wave sleep**, longer time to fall asleep, **early waking**.
- **Other changes:** the suicide rate rises. Vision, hearing, immune response, and kidney, lung and GI function decline. Muscle mass falls and fat rises.
- **Intelligence does not decline**, so a cognitive decline is never "just aging."
- Ethics, end-of-life care and healthcare delivery are in guide 56.

## Clinical vignette
A 12-month-old girl comes for a well visit. She was 3.4 kg at birth and now weighs 10.1 kg. She pulls to stand and walks holding the couch. She picks up cereal pieces between her thumb and index finger and calls her mother "mama." She waves bye-bye and plays pat-a-cake. She does not yet walk alone. Her Moro reflex is absent.

- **Assessment:** **normal development and growth.**
- **Growth:** her weight has about tripled, as expected by 12 months (MSD).
- **Milestones:** pulling to stand, cruising, pincer grasp, "mama," waving and pat-a-cake are all **12-month** CDC milestones.
- **Walking alone** is expected by **15 months** for a few steps and **18 months** for walking well (CDC). Not walking alone at 12 months is **not** a delay.
- **No Moro reflex at 12 months is normal.** It should be gone by 6 months (StatPearls).

## Common traps
- **The Apgar score is not used to decide whether to start resuscitation.** Act on heart rate and breathing (MSD).
- **Apgar:** pink body with blue hands and feet scores **1**, and a heart rate under 100 scores **1** (MSD).
- **Primitive reflexes past about 6 months → think cerebral palsy.** Babinski in an adult → UMN lesion (StatPearls).
- **Stranger anxiety at 9-12 months is normal**, not an anxiety disorder (CDC; DSM p. 219).
- **CDC milestones use the 75% age (2022).** Missing one should trigger screening; older tables used median ages.
- **Weight doubles by about 5 months and triples by 12 months.** Newborns lose 5-8% and regain it by 2 weeks (MSD).
- **First sign of puberty:** breast budding in girls, **testicular enlargement** (not pubic hair) in boys (MSD).
- **Menarche comes about 2 years after thelarche**; girls peak in growth about 2 years before boys (MSD).
- **Precocious puberty:** before 8 in girls and before 9 in boys. **Boys need a brain lesion search.** Treat central cases with a GnRH agonist (MSD).
- **Menopause:** **FSH up** (lost feedback), estrogen down; 12 months without periods (Osmosis p. 572).
- **Aging:** libido is unchanged and intelligence does not decline (FA p. 270).

## Practice questions
Answer, then check the explanation.

1. A healthy 6-month-old boy is brought for a well visit. Which skill is he most likely to have?
   - **Answer:** **rolling from tummy to back.**
   - **Why:** it is a 6-month CDC milestone, along with laughing and leaning on his hands to sit.
   - **Trap:** **picking up small objects with a pincer grasp**, which is a 12-month milestone (CDC).

2. A 9-month-old girl cries and clings to her father when her grandmother, whom she sees monthly, tries to hold her. She calms once back in her father's arms. Her development is otherwise normal. Which is the most likely explanation?
   - **Answer:** **normal stranger anxiety.**
   - **Why:** CDC lists being "shy, clingy, or fearful around strangers" at 9 months. DSM-5-TR calls heightened separation anxiety around age 1 normal and a sign of secure attachment (p. 219).
   - **Trap:** **separation anxiety disorder**. That needs fear or anxiety about separation that is developmentally inappropriate and excessive (DSM pp. 215, 217).

3. A 24-month-old boy runs and kicks a ball. He says about 4 single words and has never put two words together. He points to show his parents things. Which statement is most accurate?
   - **Answer:** **he has not met an expected 2-year language milestone and needs further evaluation**, including a hearing test (hearing test is standard teaching).
   - **Why:** by 2 years, 75% or more of children say at least two words together (CDC). Missing a CDC milestone should prompt screening (Zubler 2022).
   - **Trap:** **this is normal until age 3**.

4. A 10-month-old girl born at 28 weeks has stiff legs that scissor when she is held upright. When her arms are lifted slightly off the mat and suddenly released, she still extends her arms, flexes her hips and cries. Which is the most likely diagnosis?
   - **Answer:** **cerebral palsy.**
   - **Why:** a persistent Moro reflex (normally gone by 6 months) is a primitive reflex lasting past 4-6 months, which predicts cerebral palsy. Prematurity and spasticity fit (StatPearls).
   - **Trap:** **normal development for a premature baby**. Even corrected for prematurity (about 7 months), the spasticity is not normal.

5. At 1 minute after birth, a newborn has a pink body with blue hands and feet and a heart rate of 90/min. She grimaces when suctioned, has some flexion of the arms and legs, and has slow, irregular breathing. What is her 1-minute Apgar score?
   - **Answer:** **5.**
   - **Why:** each item scores 1: color (pink body, blue extremities), pulse (under 100), grimace, tone (some flexion) and respiration (slow, irregular) (MSD).
   - **Trap:** **7**, which you get by scoring a heart rate under 100 as 2.

6. A term newborn is limp and not breathing, with a heart rate of 50/min, immediately after delivery. Which is the most appropriate next step?
   - **Answer:** **begin neonatal resuscitation now.**
   - **Why:** the Apgar score is not used to guide resuscitation. Heart rate, breathing effort and tone are used directly (MSD).
   - **Trap:** **wait to assign the 1-minute Apgar score before acting**.

7. A breastfed term newborn weighed 3.6 kg at birth and weighs 3.35 kg on day 4. She feeds 8-10 times a day, has 5 wet diapers a day and looks well. Which is the most appropriate next step?
   - **Answer:** **reassure the parents and recheck her weight**, expecting her to regain birth weight by 2 weeks.
   - **Why:** she has lost about 7%. Newborns normally lose 5-8% and regain it within 2 weeks (MSD).
   - **Trap:** **switch to formula because of weight loss**.

8. An 11-year-old boy asks what the first change of puberty will be. Which change usually comes first in boys?
   - **Answer:** **enlargement of the testes and scrotum.**
   - **Why:** in boys, sexual changes begin with testicular and scrotal enlargement, then penile growth (MSD). Testicular enlargement is Tanner stage 2 (Osmosis p. 587).
   - **Trap:** **pubic hair**, which comes later in boys.

9. A 10-year-old girl developed breast buds 3 months ago. Her mother asks when her first period is likely. Which answer is best?
   - **Answer:** **in about 2 years**, around age 12.
   - **Why:** menarche usually comes about 2 years after breast development begins, after peak height growth (MSD).
   - **Trap:** **within the next few months**.

10. A 7-year-old boy has had pubic hair, penile and testicular growth and a rapid growth spurt over 6 months. His bone age is advanced, and his morning LH is in the pubertal range. Which is the most appropriate next step?
    - **Answer:** **MRI of the brain** to look for a hypothalamic or pineal lesion.
    - **Why:** this is central (GnRH-dependent) precocious puberty, starting before age 9 in a boy. Boys are much more likely than girls to have a brain lesion (MSD). MSD: brain MRI may be done in boys with GnRH-dependent precocious puberty to exclude a brain lesion.
    - **Trap:** **reassurance, because most cases are idiopathic**. That is true for girls, not boys (MSD).

11. A 51-year-old woman has had no menstrual period for 13 months. She has hot flashes and night sweats. Which hormone pattern is expected?
    - **Answer:** **high FSH and LH with low estradiol.**
    - **Why:** follicle depletion lowers estrogen and progesterone, which removes negative feedback, so FSH and LH rise (Osmosis Physiology p. 572).
    - **Trap:** **low FSH with low estradiol**, which suggests a hypothalamic or pituitary cause.

12. A 72-year-old man in good health says erections take longer, and he needs more time before another erection is possible. His interest in sex is unchanged. Which is the most likely explanation?
    - **Answer:** **normal aging.**
    - **Why:** slower erection and ejaculation with a longer refractory period and unchanged libido are normal age changes (FA p. 270).
    - **Trap:** **testosterone deficiency**, which would lower libido.

13. Parents of a newborn decline all routine injections. At 6 days old, the baby bleeds from the umbilical stump and has blood in the stool. Platelets are normal. Which routine birth intervention would most likely have prevented this?
    - **Answer:** **vitamin K (phytonadione) 1 mg IM at birth.**
    - **Why:** routine IM vitamin K is given within 6 hours of birth (MSD). Without it, newborns can develop vitamin K deficiency bleeding (standard teaching).
    - **Trap:** **erythromycin eye ointment**, which prevents gonococcal eye infection, not bleeding (MSD).
