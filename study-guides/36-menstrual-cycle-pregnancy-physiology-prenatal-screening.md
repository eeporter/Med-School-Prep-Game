# Menstrual cycle, normal pregnancy physiology and prenatal screening

Exam focus: NBME comprehensive (CBSE) and USMLE Step 1 · Study material, not patient care guidance · Auditor: cleared 2026-10-28
Based on your notes: El-Husseiny's Essentials of Obstetrics (physiology of reproduction pp. 1-6, physiologic changes pp. 7-13, diagnosis and dating pp. 14-19, normal pregnancy events pp. 20-22, prenatal testing pp. 23-30). Each fact is also checked against the MSD Manual; where another source disagrees, guidance has changed, or a document is wrong, a marked note says so.

## In one line
Pregnancy is a **high-flow, low-resistance, hypercoagulable, mildly alkalotic** state: plasma volume, cardiac output, tidal volume and GFR all rise by up to about half, so hemoglobin, BUN, creatinine and PCO2 all **fall**, and those lower numbers are normal.

## Core mechanism: the menstrual cycle (El-Husseiny Obstetrics, pp. 1-4)
1. **Follicular phase** (days 1-14): FSH grows the dominant follicle. **Theca cells** (LH receptors) make androgen; **granulosa cells** (FSH receptors) turn it into estrogen with **aromatase** and also make inhibin, which holds FSH down. Estrogen rebuilds the endometrium and makes cervical mucus thin and watery.
2. **Ovulation:** the **LH surge** triggers ovulation on about day 14.
3. **Luteal phase** (days 14-28): the corpus luteum makes **progesterone**, which turns the endometrium **secretory** and makes cervical mucus thick. Without pregnancy the corpus luteum fails by about day 23; progesterone falls, spiral arteries clamp down and menstruation follows.
4. **The luteal phase is fixed at 14 days**, so cycle length differences come from the follicular phase. Ovulation day = cycle length minus 14: a 35-day cycle ovulates on day 35 - 14 = **21**; a 21-day cycle on day **7** (pp. 1, 16).
5. **hCG** from the **syncytiotrophoblast** keeps the corpus luteum alive until the placenta makes its own progesterone. Its alpha subunit is shared with LH, FSH and TSH; the beta subunit is what pregnancy tests detect. It turns positive at implantation, about 7-8 days after ovulation, so **before** the missed period (pp. 2, 5).
   - High hCG: twins, hydatidiform mole, choriocarcinoma. Low hCG: ectopic pregnancy, threatened or missed abortion (p. 5).
6. **Human placental lactogen** (hPL, also from the syncytiotrophoblast) causes **insulin resistance**, shunting glucose to the baby. Gestational diabetes appears when the mother's pancreas cannot keep up (pp. 5-6). MSD is more cautious: it blames mainly more corticosteroids and placental progesterone, "as does the stress of pregnancy and possibly the increased level of human placental lactogen" ([MSD Manual: Physiology of Pregnancy](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/gynecology-and-obstetrics/approach-to-the-pregnant-woman-and-prenatal-care/physiology-of-pregnancy)).

Cycle words (pp. 4-5): **oligomenorrhea** = cycles over 35 days; **polymenorrhea** = under 21 days; **metrorrhagia** = bleeding between periods; **menorrhagia** = over 80 mL or over 7 days.

## Normal changes in pregnancy
| System | Change (your notes) | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blood pressure | Falls early, diastolic more than systolic; **never** normally rises above baseline (p. 8) | Progesterone relaxes vessels; **SVR falls about 30%** (p. 8) |
| Cardiac output | Up to **+50%** (both HR and SV); lowest lying **supine**, highest on the **left side** (p. 8) | The uterus presses on the IVC when she lies flat (p. 8) |
| Murmurs | **Systolic** flow murmur is normal; a **diastolic** murmur is never normal (p. 8) | More flow across aortic and pulmonary valves |
| Plasma vs red cells | Plasma **+50%**, red cell mass **+30%**, so Hb and Hct fall about 15% (p. 10) | Dilution, not true anemia |
| White cells, ESR | WBC up to 16,000/mm3; ESR up (p. 10) | |
| Clotting | Factors V, VII, VIII, IX, XII and von Willebrand factor rise: **hypercoagulable** (p. 10) | Limits bleeding at delivery |
| Breathing | Tidal volume **+40%** (the only lung volume that rises); rate unchanged; PCO2 **40 → 30 mm Hg**, pH 7.40 → 7.45 (pp. 8-9) | **Progesterone** drives the respiratory center; kidneys dump bicarbonate to compensate (p. 9) |
| Gut | Slow stomach and colon (aspiration risk, constipation), reflux (pp. 10-11) | Progesterone relaxes smooth muscle |
| Kidneys | GFR and renal plasma flow **+50%**, so BUN, creatinine and uric acid fall about 25%; glucose and protein in urine rise; right-sided hydronephrosis (p. 11) | More plasma volume; progesterone widens ureters; uterus compresses them |
| Endocrine | Pituitary up to **3 times bigger** (lactotroph growth) → **Sheehan syndrome** risk after postpartum shock; cortisol 2-3 times higher; **TBG up** so total T3/T4 up but **free** T4 normal; levothyroxine dose must go up (p. 13) | Estrogen raises TBG |
| Skin | Linea nigra, chloasma, striae, spider angiomas, palmar erythema; **Chadwick sign** (blue cervix) (p. 7) | More estrogen and blood flow |

- A creatinine of **1.2 mg/dL** is normal outside pregnancy but means **kidney impairment** in pregnancy (pp. 11-12). MSD gives the usual pregnant range as 0.5-0.7 mg/dL and BUN usually under 10 mg/dL ([MSD Manual: Physiology of Pregnancy](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/gynecology-and-obstetrics/approach-to-the-pregnant-woman-and-prenatal-care/physiology-of-pregnancy)).

**Other source (size of the changes):** MSD gives slightly different numbers from your notes: cardiac output up **30-50%**, peaking at 16-28 weeks; GFR up **30-50%**; red cell mass up about **25%**; pituitary up about **135%** ([MSD Manual: Physiology of Pregnancy](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/gynecology-and-obstetrics/approach-to-the-pregnant-woman-and-prenatal-care/physiology-of-pregnancy)). The direction of each change is the same, and that is what exams test.

## Confirming and dating a pregnancy (pp. 14-18)
- **Transvaginal ultrasound** shows a gestational sac at 4-5 weeks (beta-hCG about 1,500 mIU/mL); fetal heart motion at 5-6 weeks; Doppler heart sounds at 8-10 weeks (p. 14).
- **Naegele's rule:** due date = first day of the last period **minus 3 months plus 7 days**. Example: last period 10 January → 10 October + 7 days = **17 October** (p. 15). Pregnancy lasts **280 days (40 weeks) from the last period**, or 266 days (38 weeks) from conception (p. 15).
- **Embryo** = fertilization to 8 weeks; **fetus** = 8 weeks to birth (p. 17).
- **G/TPAL:** gravida = times pregnant; T = term births, P = preterm births, A = abortions (spontaneous or induced), L = living children. Example from your notes: sixth pregnancy, 2 term births, 1 preterm **twin** birth, 2 abortions, 4 living children = **G6 P2124** (p. 18).
- Do a pregnancy test before any X-ray or CT in a woman who could be pregnant (p. 19).

**Recent change (the hCG cutoff for "should see it on ultrasound"):** your notes use **1,500 mIU/mL** for transvaginal ultrasound (p. 14). MSD now says "the recommended threshold has been increased to **3500 mIU/mL**," so that a wanted intrauterine pregnancy is not mistaken for an ectopic and treated ([MSD Manual: Ectopic Pregnancy](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/gynecology-and-obstetrics/early-pregnancy-disorders/ectopic-pregnancy)).

## Nausea and hyperemesis gravidarum (p. 22)
- Hyperemesis = severe vomiting with **ketones in urine**, dehydration, lab changes and weight loss **over 5%**. Risk factors: twins, **hydatidiform mole**, reflux history.
- Labs: **hypochloremic, hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis** from losing stomach acid.
- **Give thiamine before glucose**: hyperemesis can cause **Wernicke encephalopathy** (confusion, eye movement problems, ataxia), and glucose first can make it worse.

## Prenatal screening by trimester (pp. 23-30)
| When | Test | What it is for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First visit | CBC, blood type and Rh with antibody screen, rubella IgG, **HBsAg**, syphilis (RPR/VDRL then treponemal test), HIV (opt-out), chlamydia and gonorrhea, urinalysis and **urine culture** | Treat asymptomatic bacteriuria (8% of pregnancies; 30% go on to pyelonephritis if untreated) (pp. 23-25) |
| 11-14 weeks | Ultrasound for dating and **nuchal translucency** (p. 23) | |
| From 10 weeks | **Cell-free fetal DNA** in mother's blood (pp. 27-28) | Trisomy screening |
| 15-20 weeks | **Quad screen**: AFP, hCG, estriol, inhibin A (p. 25) | Neural tube defects and trisomies |
| 24-28 weeks | 1-hour 50 g glucose test; CBC; antibody screen, then **anti-D immune globulin (RhoGAM) at 28 weeks** if Rh-negative (pp. 29-30) | Gestational diabetes, iron deficiency, Rh disease |
| Late third trimester | Vaginal-rectal **group B strep** culture (p. 30) | Intrapartum penicillin if positive |

- **Rubella vaccine is live**, so it is **not** given in pregnancy; give it after delivery (p. 23).
- Syphilis in pregnancy must be treated with **penicillin**; if allergic, **desensitize** and then give benzathine penicillin G (p. 24).
- **High MS-AFP:** open neural tube defect, abdominal wall defect, twins, placental bleeding. **Most common cause of high or low AFP: wrong dates**, so the first step is a dating ultrasound. If dates are right, amniocentesis: high **acetylcholinesterase** confirms an open neural tube defect (pp. 26-27).
- **Low MS-AFP:** trisomy 21. AFP alone finds only 20%; the quad screen raises that to 80% (p. 27).
- **Glucose testing:** 1-hour 50 g screen abnormal if **140 mg/dL or more**. Then the 3-hour 100 g test: normal fasting under 95, 1 h under 180, 2 h under 155, 3 h under 140; **2 or more abnormal values = gestational diabetes**. A fasting value over 125 means overt diabetes (p. 29).

**Other source (quad screen patterns):** your notes give only "low AFP" for Down syndrome. The classic patterns are: **trisomy 21** = AFP low, **hCG high**, estriol low, **inhibin A high** (standard teaching); **trisomy 18** = "low levels of all 4 serum markers" ([MSD Manual: Noninvasive Prenatal Fetal Screening Tests](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/gynecology-and-obstetrics/prenatal-genetic-counseling-and-evaluation/noninvasive-prenatal-fetal-screening-tests)). First-trimester Down syndrome pattern: "high levels of beta-hCG, low levels of PAPP-A, and enlarged fetal nuchal translucency" (MSD, same page).

**Other source (who gets cell-free DNA):** your notes describe offering cell-free DNA after an abnormal quad screen (p. 27). MSD says ACOG now "recommends offering cell-free DNA screening to **all** pregnant women," and gives sensitivities of 99% (trisomy 21), 98% (trisomy 18) and 91% (trisomy 13) (same MSD page). A positive result is confirmed by CVS or amniocentesis (p. 28).

**Recent change (group B strep timing):** your notes say screening at **35-37 weeks** (p. 30). ACOG and the AAP moved it to **36 0/7 to 37 6/7 weeks** in July 2019 ([AAFP news, 24 Jul 2019](https://www.aafp.org/about/news/20190724groupbstrep)).

**Other source (anemia cutoffs):** your notes call Hb 10-12 g/dL normal in pregnancy and Hb under 10 anemia (pp. 23, 30). MSD defines anemia as Hb **under 11 g/dL in the first and third trimesters** and **under 10.5 g/dL in the second** ([MSD Manual: Anemia in Pregnancy](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/gynecology-and-obstetrics/approach-to-the-pregnant-woman-and-prenatal-care/anemia-in-pregnancy)). Both agree that **MCV** is the best clue to true anemia, and iron deficiency is the most common cause (p. 23).

**Correction (which Coombs test):** p. 24 says blood type and Rh are "determined with the direct Coombs test." The mother's **antibody screen** is an **indirect** Coombs test, which looks for free antibodies in her serum, as your own p. 30 says ("an indirect Coombs test is performed at 28 weeks"). The **direct** Coombs test looks for antibody already stuck to red cells, for example the baby's cells in hemolytic disease of the newborn (standard teaching).

## Clinical vignette
A 29-year-old at 30 weeks feels light-headed whenever she lies flat on her back for an ultrasound, and better when she turns onto her left side. She also has a soft systolic murmur at the left sternal border and mild ankle swelling. Hb is 11.2 g/dL with normal MCV.
- **Diagnosis:** normal pregnancy (supine hypotension from IVC compression, plus a flow murmur and dilutional fall in Hb).
- **Why:** cardiac output is lowest supine because the uterus compresses the IVC and cuts venous return; a systolic flow murmur and mild swelling are normal (El-Husseiny Obstetrics, pp. 8, 10).
- **Next step:** reassurance; tilt her to the left side for scans (p. 8).

## Common traps
- **Low hemoglobin in pregnancy is often dilution.** Check MCV before calling it iron deficiency.
- **PCO2 of 30 is normal in pregnancy.** A "normal" PCO2 of 40 can mean she is tiring out.
- **Free T4 does not change**; total T4 rises because TBG rises.
- **Abnormal AFP? First check the dates** with ultrasound.
- **Rubella vaccine is never given during pregnancy**; it is given after delivery.
- **Antibody screen = indirect Coombs** (mother's serum); **direct Coombs = antibodies on red cells** (baby).
- **The luteal phase is always about 14 days**; a long cycle means a long follicular phase.

## Practice questions
1. A woman with a regular 32-day cycle wants to know when she ovulates. Which day?
   - **Answer:** day 18.
   - **Why:** the luteal phase lasts about 14 days, so ovulation = 32 - 14 = 18 (El-Husseiny Obstetrics, pp. 1, 16).
   - **Trap:** answering day 14, which fits only a 28-day cycle.
2. At 34 weeks, a pregnant woman has an arterial blood gas of pH 7.45, PCO2 30 mm Hg and bicarbonate 20 mEq/L. Interpretation?
   - **Answer:** normal for pregnancy (compensated respiratory alkalosis).
   - **Why:** progesterone drives the breathing center, tidal volume rises about 40%, PCO2 falls from 40 to 30, and the kidneys excrete bicarbonate (pp. 8-9).
   - **Trap:** looking for a pulmonary embolism or anxiety; check the pregnancy normals first.
3. At 17 weeks, MS-AFP is 2.8 times the median. What is the next step?
   - **Answer:** ultrasound to confirm gestational age (and look for a neural tube or abdominal wall defect or twins).
   - **Why:** dating error is the most common cause of an abnormal AFP; if dates are right and the scan explains nothing, do amniocentesis for AFP and acetylcholinesterase (pp. 26-27).
   - **Trap:** going straight to amniocentesis.
4. Quad screen at 16 weeks: low AFP, high hCG, low estriol, high inhibin A. Most likely?
   - **Answer:** trisomy 21 (Down syndrome).
   - **Why:** low AFP is the Down syndrome marker in your notes (p. 27); the full four-marker pattern is standard teaching. Trisomy 18 lowers all four (MSD).
   - **Trap:** trisomy 18, where hCG and inhibin are low too.
5. A G2P1001 woman at 28 weeks is Rh-negative with a negative antibody screen. What is given now?
   - **Answer:** anti-D immune globulin (RhoGAM).
   - **Why:** the indirect Coombs at 28 weeks shows she is not yet sensitized; about 0.2% of Rh-negative women become sensitized before 28 weeks from small bleeds; if she already has anti-D antibodies, RhoGAM is useless (p. 30).
   - **Trap:** skipping it because the screen is negative; a negative screen is the reason to give it.
