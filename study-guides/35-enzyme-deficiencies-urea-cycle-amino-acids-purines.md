## In one line
Block an enzyme and two things happen: what comes **before** the block builds up and is often toxic (ammonia, phenylalanine, homogentisic acid, uric acid), and what comes **after** it runs short and may become an essential nutrient (tyrosine in PKU, cysteine in homocystinuria, arginine in urea cycle defects). Almost all are **autosomal recessive**; the high-yield **X-linked** ones are **OTC deficiency, Lesch-Nyhan and PRPP synthetase overactivity** (El-Husseiny Biochemistry, pp. 86, 93, 209; your table).

## Core mechanism
1. **Urea cycle:** five steps, two in the mitochondria (**CPS I**, **OTC**) and three in the cytosol (argininosuccinate synthetase, argininosuccinate lyase, arginase). CPS I is the rate-limiting step and needs **N-acetylglutamate** as its activator (El-Husseiny Biochemistry, p. 208; Top Concepts p. 298).
2. **Any urea cycle block:** high ammonia, high glutamine, **low BUN**. Ammonia uses up alpha-ketoglutarate (slowing the TCA cycle) and glutamate; astrocytes turn glutamate into glutamine and swell, giving vomiting, confusion, cerebral edema and asterixis. Breathing fast from brain swelling gives **respiratory alkalosis** (pp. 209-210).
3. **CPS I vs OTC:** in OTC deficiency, carbamoyl phosphate cannot enter the urea cycle, spills into pyrimidine synthesis and makes **orotic acid**. CPS I deficiency has **no** orotic acid, because carbamoyl phosphate is never made (p. 209).
4. **Purine salvage:** about **90%** of free purine bases are recycled by **HGPRT** and only about 10% become uric acid. With no HGPRT, 100% go to uric acid, and spare PRPP drives even more new purine synthesis (pp. 230-231).

## Urea cycle disorders (El-Husseiny Biochemistry, pp. 208-210; Osmosis Metabolic p. 6; your table)
| Disorder | Missing enzyme | Clue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hyperammonemia type I | Carbamoyl phosphate synthetase I | High ammonia, **no** orotic acid |
| **OTC deficiency** (hyperammonemia type II) | **Ornithine transcarbamylase** | **Most common**; **X-linked**; high ammonia and glutamine, **high orotic acid**, low BUN and citrulline, **no megaloblastic anemia** |
| Citrullinemia | Argininosuccinate synthetase | High citrulline (MSD) |
| Argininosuccinic aciduria | Argininosuccinate lyase ("argininosuccinase") | High argininosuccinate |
| Argininemia | Arginase | High arginine |

- Boys are fully affected by OTC deficiency; girls are usually symptom-free carriers (Osmosis Metabolic p. 6). Illness, fasting or a high-protein meal can trigger a crisis (El-Husseiny, p. 210).
- Treatment: low-protein diet, "nitrogen sinks" that carry nitrogen out in urine, arginine, hemodialysis in a crisis, and often a liver transplant later ([MSD Manual: Urea Cycle Disorders](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/inherited-disorders-of-metabolism/urea-cycle-disorders)). For acquired high ammonia from liver disease: **lactulose** and **rifaximin or neomycin** (El-Husseiny, p. 209).

**Correction (drug name):** El-Husseiny Biochemistry p. 209 lists "sodium benzoate or **phenylpyruvate**." The nitrogen sinks are **sodium benzoate, sodium phenylacetate and sodium phenylbutyrate**; phenylpyruvate is the PKU breakdown product, not a drug ([MSD Manual: Urea Cycle Disorders](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/inherited-disorders-of-metabolism/urea-cycle-disorders)).

**Other source (OTC treatment):** Osmosis p. 6 lists arginine; MSD adds that "oral citrulline is more effective than arginine in patients with OTC deficiency," and that N-acetylglutamate synthase deficiency is treated with N-carbamylglutamate.

## Phenylalanine and tyrosine disorders
| Disorder | Missing enzyme | Clue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Classic PKU** | **Phenylalanine hydroxylase** | Intellectual disability, seizures, eczema, **musty (mousy) odor**, fair skin and blue eyes; **tyrosine becomes essential**; about 1 in 10,000 (El-Husseiny, pp. 215-217) |
| "Malignant" PKU (BH4 deficiency) | Dihydropteridine reductase, or a BH4-making enzyme | High phenylalanine **plus** low dopamine and serotonin, so the brain gets worse **even on the diet**; high prolactin (less dopamine); treat with BH4 plus neurotransmitter precursors (p. 217; your table rows "type II-V") |
| **Alkaptonuria** | **Homogentisate oxidase** | Urine turns **black on standing**, blue-black ear cartilage and sclera (**ochronosis**), spine and large-joint arthritis, disc calcification; usually harmless in childhood (p. 219; Osmosis Metabolic pp. 1-2) |
| Tyrosinemia type I | **Fumarylacetoacetate hydrolase** | Liver failure in infancy, **Fanconi syndrome**, "cabbage-like" odor, **high succinylacetone**; later liver cancer; treat with **nitisinone** (your table; El-Husseiny Nephrology p. 126; [MSD Manual: Tyrosine Metabolism Disorders](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/inherited-disorders-of-metabolism/tyrosine-metabolism-disorders)) |
| Tyrosinemia type II (Richner-Hanhart) | Tyrosine aminotransferase | Eye ulcers, thick palm and sole skin, intellectual disability (your table; MSD) |
| Neonatal (transient) tyrosinemia | 4-Hydroxyphenylpyruvate dioxygenase | Mostly preterm babies, usually no symptoms (your table; MSD) |

- **Why pale:** extra phenylalanine blocks **tyrosinase**, so less melanin (El-Husseiny, p. 217).
- **Screening timing:** the heel-prick test is done 2-3 days after birth, once the baby has had protein feeds; at birth levels are normal because the mother cleared the phenylalanine (p. 216; Osmosis Metabolic p. 7).
- **PKU treatment:** low-phenylalanine diet with tyrosine added, **no aspartame**; sapropterin (a BH4 drug) for responsive patients (El-Husseiny, p. 217; Osmosis Metabolic p. 7).
- **Maternal PKU:** high phenylalanine in a pregnant woman with PKU causes **microcephaly**, intellectual disability, growth restriction and **congenital heart defects** in the baby (El-Husseiny, p. 217; Osmosis Metabolic p. 7).

**Other source (most common BH4 defect):** El-Husseiny Biochemistry p. 217 calls dihydropteridine reductase deficiency "the most common cause" of BH4 deficiency. MedlinePlus Genetics says **6-pyruvoyl-tetrahydropterin synthase (PTS) deficiency** "accounts for more than half of all cases" ([MedlinePlus: PTS gene](https://medlineplus.gov/genetics/gene/pts)), and Qiu et al. write that "PTS deficiency (PTPSD) is the most common form of BH4D" ([Qiu et al., Front Genet 2023;14:1250568](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/genetics/articles/10.3389/fgene.2023.1250568/full)). In their patients, PTS deficiency gave very low urine biopterin, while DHPR deficiency gave a normal biopterin fraction. For the exam, the classic "malignant PKU" enzyme to name is still dihydropteridine reductase, because questions usually test the BH4 recycling step.

**Recent change (PKU):** on **28 July 2025** the FDA approved **sepiapterin (Sephience)** for children and adults with PKU, alongside a phenylalanine-restricted diet ([Contemporary Pediatrics](https://www.contemporarypediatrics.com/view/fda-approves-sepiapterin-to-treat-phenylketonuria-in-adult-pediatric-patients)). Your notes list sapropterin, which is still used.

**Recent change (alkaptonuria):** Osmosis p. 2 lists nitisinone. MSD (Mar 2024) still says "there is no effective treatment," but the European Medicines Agency recommended nitisinone (Orfadin) for adults with alkaptonuria on **18 Sep 2020**, because it lowers homogentisic acid in blood and urine ([EMA news](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/news/first-treatment-rare-metabolic-disorder-alkaptonuria)).

## Branched-chain and propionate pathway
| Disorder | Missing enzyme | Clue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Maple syrup urine disease** | **Branched-chain alpha-ketoacid dehydrogenase** (needs B1) | Isoleucine, leucine, valine build up ("I Love Vermont maple syrup", standard teaching); sweet burnt-sugar urine, poor feeding, vomiting and brain damage in the first days of life; **alloisoleucine** in plasma; restrict the three amino acids and give **thiamine** (El-Husseiny, p. 218; Osmosis Metabolic p. 5) |
| Propionic acidemia | **Propionyl-CoA carboxylase** (needs biotin) | Poor feeding, vomiting, low tone, **anion gap acidosis** (El-Husseiny, p. 223) |
| Methylmalonic acidemia | **Methylmalonyl-CoA mutase** (needs B12) | Vomiting, acidosis, coma (El-Husseiny, p. 223; your table) |
| Isovaleric acidemia | Isovaleryl-CoA dehydrogenase | Odor, vomiting, acidosis (your table) |

- Branched-chain alpha-ketoacid dehydrogenase, pyruvate dehydrogenase and alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase all use the same five cofactors: **B1 (thiamine), lipoate, CoA, FAD, NAD** ("Tender Loving Care For Nancy") (El-Husseiny, p. 218).
- Propionyl-CoA comes from **isoleucine, valine, threonine, methionine** and **odd-chain fatty acids** (p. 223).

**Correction (methylmalonic aciduria row):** your table gives the cause as "deficiency of vitamin B12," and Top Concepts p. 283 says "Methylmalonic Acidemia occurs due to lack of B12." Both mix up two things. Acquired **B12 deficiency** does raise methylmalonic acid, which is how it is told apart from folate deficiency (El-Husseiny, pp. 240, 242). The **inherited disease** is a defect of the **methylmalonyl-CoA mutase** enzyme itself (El-Husseiny, p. 223) or of the cell's handling of cobalamin, and only some forms respond to high-dose hydroxocobalamin ([MSD Manual: Branched-Chain Amino Acid Metabolism Disorders](https://msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/inherited-disorders-of-metabolism/branched-chain-amino-acid-metabolism-disorders)).

**Other source (MSUD enzyme name and outlook):** your table names "alpha-ketoacid **decarboxylase**"; El-Husseiny and MSD name the whole **dehydrogenase** complex, with thiamine as the cofactor "for the decarboxylation" step, so both point at the same enzyme. The table says death "usually" comes by the end of year one; Osmosis p. 5 says it is fatal if **untreated** in the first five months, and MSD says diet controls it and liver transplant is curative.

**Other source (isovaleric acidemia odor):** your table says a "cheesy" odor; MSD describes it as a **sweaty feet** smell. Expect "sweaty feet" on the exam.

## Sulfur amino acids and transport defects
| Disorder | Defect | Clue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Homocystinuria** (most common form) | **Cystathionine beta-synthase** | Lens dislocates **down and in**, **marfanoid** build, osteoporosis, intellectual disability, **clots in arteries and veins** (the main cause of death); **cysteine becomes essential** (El-Husseiny, pp. 220-221; Osmosis Metabolic p. 4) |
| Homocystinuria, other forms | Low enzyme affinity for **B6**; methionine synthase; MTHFR | B6-responsive form: high-dose B6; methionine synthase form: **add** methionine (El-Husseiny, p. 220) |
| **Cystinuria** | Kidney and gut transporter for **Cystine, Ornithine, Lysine, Arginine** ("COLA") | Recurrent kidney stones from childhood, **hexagonal** crystals, **positive cyanide-nitroprusside** test, staghorn stones in children; treat with fluids, urine alkalinization, penicillamine (El-Husseiny Nephrology, pp. 100-101; Osmosis Metabolic p. 3) |
| **Hartnup disease** | Kidney and gut transporter for **neutral** amino acids, especially **tryptophan** | Low tryptophan, so low niacin: **pellagra-like** rash with sun, diarrhea, ataxia; neutral amino acids in urine but **not** proline or arginine; treat with **nicotinamide** and a high-protein diet (El-Husseiny Biochemistry, pp. 237-238; Osmosis Metabolic pp. 3-4) |
| Cystinosis | Lysosomal cystine carrier | Cystine crystals in tissues; the most common inherited cause of **Fanconi syndrome** in children (your table; El-Husseiny Nephrology p. 126) |

- Homocystinuria treatment for the classic form: **low methionine, more cysteine, B6, B12 and folate** (El-Husseiny, p. 220). MSD adds **betaine**, which lowers homocysteine by remethylating it, and says about half of patients respond to high-dose B6 alone ([MSD Manual: Methionine Metabolism Disorders](https://msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/inherited-disorders-of-metabolism/methionine-metabolism-disorders)).
- Homocystinuria vs Marfan: Marfan lens goes **up and out**; Marfan is autosomal dominant fibrillin (FBN1), with normal homocysteine (El-Husseiny Biochemistry, pp. 50, 221; Top Concepts p. 138).

**Read more on EyeWiki:** [Ectopia Lentis](https://eyewiki.org/Ectopia_Lentis); [Homocystinuria](https://eyewiki.org/Homocystinuria) (links for extra reading only; facts above come from the cited sources)

**Other source (homocystinuria type numbers):** your table numbers four types (I CBS, II MTHFR, III and IV methionine synthase forms), and Osmosis p. 4 numbers three types differently (I CBS, II methionine synthase, III low B6 affinity). The numbers are not consistent between books, so learn each type by its **enzyme**, not its number.

**Correction (Osmosis cystinuria wording):** Osmosis Metabolic p. 3 lists "cysteine" among the transported amino acids. The stone-forming amino acid is **cystine** (two cysteines joined), the "C" in COLA (El-Husseiny Nephrology, p. 100).

**Other source (cystine stones on X-ray):** Top Concepts p. 299 groups cystine stones with uric acid stones as "radiolucent"; El-Husseiny Nephrology p. 100 says **faintly radiopaque on X-ray and moderately radiopaque on CT**. Expect cystine stones to be faint on plain film.

## Purines and pyrimidines
| Disorder | Missing enzyme | Clue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Lesch-Nyhan** | **HGPRT** (complete) | **X-linked**; **self-biting**, dystonia, intellectual disability, aggression, gout, **orange "sand" in the diaper**. "HGPRT: Hyperuricemia, Gout, Pissed off, Retardation, dysTonia" (El-Husseiny, p. 231; Osmosis Metabolic p. 63) |
| Gout with partial HGPRT deficiency | HGPRT (partial) | X-linked; gout without the brain problems (your table) |
| Gout from PRPP synthetase overactivity | PRPP synthetase (resists feedback, or binds ribose-5-phosphate too well) | X-linked; too much uric acid (your table; [MSD purine table](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/multimedia/table/purine-metabolism-disorders)) |
| **ADA deficiency** | **Adenosine deaminase** | Too much dATP poisons lymphocytes: **autosomal recessive SCID** (El-Husseiny, p. 231) |
| Xanthinuria | Xanthine oxidase | Low uric acid, **xanthine stones** (your table; MSD) |
| **Orotic aciduria** | **UMP synthase** (orotate phosphoribosyltransferase plus OMP decarboxylase on one protein) | **Megaloblastic anemia that does not respond to B12 or folate**, growth delay, orotic acid in urine, **normal ammonia**; treat with **uridine** (El-Husseiny, p. 227; Osmosis Metabolic p. 64) |

- Lesch-Nyhan treatment: **allopurinol** (or febuxostat) lowers uric acid (El-Husseiny, p. 231), but MSD says the brain problems have "no known treatment; management is supportive" ([MSD Manual: Purine Salvage Disorders](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/inherited-disorders-of-metabolism/purine-salvage-disorders)).
- Orotic aciduria types: type I lacks both enzyme steps; type II lacks only OMP decarboxylase (your table; Osmosis Metabolic p. 64).
- Drugs that hit these pathways: **leflunomide** (dihydroorotate dehydrogenase), **methotrexate, trimethoprim, pyrimethamine** (dihydrofolate reductase), **5-FU** (thymidylate synthase), **6-MP and azathioprine** (PRPP amidotransferase), **mycophenolate and ribavirin** (IMP dehydrogenase), **hydroxyurea** (ribonucleotide reductase) (El-Husseiny, p. 229).

**Recent change (orotic aciduria drug):** your notes say uridine (El-Husseiny p. 227) or uridine monophosphate (Osmosis p. 64). The FDA-approved drug is **uridine triacetate (Xuriden)** ("Initial U.S. Approval: 2015"), indicated "for the treatment of hereditary orotic aciduria"; the body's esterases turn it into uridine in the blood ([FDA label: Xuriden (uridine triacetate), 2019](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2019/208169s003lbl.pdf)). It is a disease of about 20 known patients worldwide ([The Pharmaceutical Journal](https://pharmaceutical-journal.com/article/news/fda-approves-drug-for-genetic-condition-that-affects-20-patients-worldwide)).

**Other source (ADA-SCID treatment):** your notes give no treatment. MSD lists enzyme replacement and stem cell transplant ([MSD purine table](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/multimedia/table/purine-metabolism-disorders)). The current enzyme replacement, **elapegademase (Revcovi)**, was approved by the FDA in October 2018 for children and adults ([HCPLive](https://www.hcplive.com/view/fda-approves-elapegademase-treatment-adascid-in-pediatric-adult-patients)).

## Clinical vignette
A 3-day-old boy was well at birth, then stopped feeding, began vomiting and became drowsy after starting formula. He is breathing fast. Blood gas shows **respiratory alkalosis**. Ammonia is very high, BUN is low and urine orotic acid is high. His mother's brother died in the newborn period.
- **Diagnosis:** ornithine transcarbamylase deficiency.
- **Why:** high ammonia with low BUN means a urea cycle block; high orotic acid places it at OTC rather than CPS I; a maternal uncle points to X-linked inheritance (El-Husseiny Biochemistry, pp. 209-210; Osmosis Metabolic p. 6).
- **Next steps:** stop protein, give glucose for calories, nitrogen sinks (sodium benzoate and phenylacetate) and arginine, and hemodialysis if ammonia stays high ([MSD Manual](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/inherited-disorders-of-metabolism/urea-cycle-disorders)).

## Common traps
- **High orotic acid does not always mean orotic aciduria.** Check ammonia: high in OTC deficiency, normal in orotic aciduria.
- **Megaloblastic anemia that ignores B12 and folate** points to orotic aciduria.
- **Musty odor = PKU; maple syrup = MSUD; cabbage = tyrosinemia I; sweaty feet = isovaleric acidemia; black urine on standing = alkaptonuria.**
- **Lens down and in = homocystinuria; up and out = Marfan.**
- **Tyrosine is essential in PKU, and cysteine is essential in classic homocystinuria.**
- **Methionine synthase form of homocystinuria needs more methionine**, the opposite of the classic form.
- **Hartnup spares proline and arginine** in urine; Fanconi syndrome loses everything.
- **Inherited methylmalonic acidemia is not "low B12."** It is a mutase or cobalamin-handling defect.

## Practice questions
1. A 2-week-old boy has high ammonia, low BUN and **normal** urine orotic acid. Ammonia does not fall with N-carbamylglutamate. Which enzyme is missing?
   - **Answer:** carbamoyl phosphate synthetase I.
   - **Why:** orotic acid rises only when carbamoyl phosphate is made and then piles up, which happens below CPS I at OTC (El-Husseiny Biochemistry, p. 209).
   - **Trap:** choosing OTC, which gives high orotic acid; or N-acetylglutamate synthase (NAGS) deficiency, which looks the same on these labs but responds to N-carbamylglutamate (MSD Urea Cycle Disorders).
2. A 6-month-old girl has megaloblastic anemia that does not improve with B12 or folate, poor growth, and lots of orotic acid in her urine. Ammonia is normal. Best treatment?
   - **Answer:** uridine triacetate (the body converts it to uridine).
   - **Why:** she cannot make UMP from orotate, so pyrimidines run short; uridine gives UMP directly and switches off CPS II (El-Husseiny Biochemistry, p. 227); uridine triacetate is the FDA-approved form ([FDA label: Xuriden (uridine triacetate), 2019](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2019/208169s003lbl.pdf)).
   - **Trap:** a low-protein diet, which is for OTC deficiency.
3. A child with PKU keeps strictly to his diet, and his phenylalanine is controlled, yet he has worsening tone, seizures and high prolactin. Urine pterins show a normal biopterin fraction. Which enzyme is likely missing?
   - **Answer:** dihydropteridine reductase (a BH4 deficiency, "malignant PKU").
   - **Why:** BH4 is also needed to make dopamine and serotonin, so the diet fixes phenylalanine but not the neurotransmitters; low dopamine raises prolactin (El-Husseiny Biochemistry, p. 217).
   - **Trap:** PTS (6-pyruvoyl-tetrahydropterin synthase), the most common BH4 defect overall, which gives a very low biopterin ([Qiu et al., Front Genet 2023;14:1250568](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/genetics/articles/10.3389/fgene.2023.1250568/full)); or phenylalanine hydroxylase, where the diet works.
4. A tall 8-year-old with a dislocated lens (down and in), intellectual disability and a leg vein clot. Which amino acid becomes essential?
   - **Answer:** cysteine.
   - **Why:** cystathionine beta-synthase makes cystathionine, which becomes cysteine; with the enzyme gone, cysteine has to come from food (El-Husseiny Biochemistry, pp. 220-221).
   - **Trap:** Marfan syndrome, where the lens goes up and out and there are no clots from homocysteine.
5. A 4-year-old boy bites his lips and fingers, has dystonia, and his parents saw orange crystals in his diapers. What percentage of purine bases would normally be recycled by the missing enzyme?
   - **Answer:** about 90% (HGPRT, Lesch-Nyhan syndrome).
   - **Why:** normally about 90% of free purine bases are salvaged and 10% become uric acid; with no HGPRT, all of them go to uric acid, and extra PRPP speeds up new purine synthesis (El-Husseiny Biochemistry, pp. 230-231).
   - **Trap:** thinking allopurinol fixes the self-injury; it lowers uric acid only (MSD).
