# Pregnancy complications: bleeding, ectopic, Rh disease, preeclampsia, diabetes and postpartum problems

Exam focus: NBME comprehensive (CBSE) and USMLE Step 1 · Study material, not patient care guidance · Auditor: cleared 2026-10-28
Based on your notes: El-Husseiny's Essentials of Obstetrics (abortion and early bleeding pp. 31-41, ectopic pp. 42-44, late bleeding pp. 46-54, cervical insufficiency p. 56, alloimmunization pp. 62-64, hypertension pp. 73-84, diabetes pp. 85-87, postpartum pp. 130-141). Each fact is also checked against the MSD Manual; where another source disagrees, guidance has changed, or a document is wrong, a marked note says so.

## In one line
Ask three questions: **when** in pregnancy (before or after 20 weeks), **does it hurt**, and **is the mother stable**. Painful late bleeding is abruption until proven otherwise; painless late bleeding is previa; bleeding with a positive test and one-sided pain early on is ectopic.

## Early pregnancy loss (pp. 34-41)
- **Abortion** = pregnancy ending before **20 weeks** or a fetus under 500 g; about 80% happen before 12 weeks, mostly from **chromosome abnormalities** of the embryo (p. 34).
- **Antiphospholipid syndrome** is an uncommon cause; treated with heparin (p. 34).

| Type | Bleeding | Cervix | Tissue passed | Your notes' management |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Threatened** | Yes | **Closed** | None; fetus alive | Watch; half go to term (p. 36) |
| **Missed** | No | Closed | None; fetus dead | Wait, misoprostol or suction D&C (p. 35) |
| **Inevitable** | Yes, with cramps | **Open** | None yet | Wait, misoprostol, or D&C if heavy (p. 36) |
| **Incomplete** | Yes, with cramps | Open | **Some** | Same, plus **anti-D** if Rh-negative (p. 36) |
| **Complete** | Settling | Closing | **All**; empty uterus | Follow hCG to zero unless a uterine pregnancy was seen first (p. 36) |

- **Septic abortion:** fever, heavy bleeding, pus, tender uterus, usually after an unsterile procedure: **broad-spectrum antibiotics plus suction curettage** (p. 38).
- **Fetal death after 20 weeks:** the big risk is **DIC**, usually not before **4 weeks** after the death, from tissue thromboplastin; check platelets, fibrinogen, PT/PTT, D-dimer (p. 39).
- Medical abortion: **mifepristone** (progesterone blocker) then **misoprostol** (prostaglandin E1) (p. 32).

## Ectopic pregnancy (pp. 42-44)
- Most often in the **fallopian tube, ampulla**. 1% of pregnancies; 15% recurrence after one ectopic (pp. 42-43).
- **Risk factors:** past **PID** (most common), tubal surgery or ligation, IUD, DES exposure (p. 43).
- **Classic triad:** missed period, vaginal bleeding, one-sided pelvic pain. Rupture: guarding, low blood pressure, fast pulse → **surgery now** (p. 43).
- **Diagnosis:** quantitative beta-hCG plus **transvaginal** ultrasound. Normal hCG in an early uterine pregnancy roughly doubles every 58 hours (pp. 43-44).
- **Treatment:** **methotrexate** (folate antagonist that kills fast-growing trophoblast) if small, no heart motion and low hCG; otherwise laparoscopic **salpingostomy** (keeps the tube) or **salpingectomy**; anti-D if Rh-negative (p. 44).

**Recent change (hCG cutoff):** your notes call it an ectopic when no uterine sac is seen above **1,500 mIU/mL** (p. 43). MSD now gives **3,500 mIU/mL** as the recommended threshold ([MSD Manual: Ectopic Pregnancy](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/gynecology-and-obstetrics/early-pregnancy-disorders/ectopic-pregnancy)).

**Other source (methotrexate criteria):** your notes use mass under 3.5 cm, no fetal heart motion and hCG under 6,000 (p. 44). MSD lists ACOG's absolute contraindications instead: a uterine pregnancy, **rupture**, **unstable vital signs**, breastfeeding, significant liver, kidney, lung or blood disease, peptic ulcer, and no way to follow up; heart motion, a high starting hCG and a mass over 4 cm are relative contraindications (same MSD page).

## Late pregnancy bleeding (after 20 weeks; pp. 46-54)
**Never do a finger or speculum exam until ultrasound has ruled out placenta previa** (p. 47).

| | **Abruptio placentae** | **Placenta previa** | **Vasa previa** | **Uterine rupture** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What | Normal placenta separates early | Placenta over the lower segment | **Fetal** vessels cross the membranes over the cervix | Uterine wall tears |
| Pain | **Painful** | **Painless** | Painless | **Painful** |
| Clue | Hypertension, **cocaine**, smoking, trauma, prior abruption; fetal distress (loss of variability) | Prior previa, multiparity, twins, **prior cesarean** | Membranes rupture → bleeding → **fetal bradycardia** | Prior **classical (vertical)** cesarean or myomectomy; fetal heart signal lost, **head moves back up** |
| Danger | **DIC** (most common obstetric cause), shock, Couvelaire uterus | Massive bleeding if the cervix is touched | Fetus bleeds to death | Mother and fetus |
| Pages | 48-49 | 50-51 | 53 | 54 |

- **Placenta accreta spectrum** (p. 52): villi invade too deep: **accreta** (into the decidua, about 80%), **increta** (into the myometrium, 15%), **percreta** (through to serosa or bladder, 5%). Risk factors: **prior cesarean**, prior D&C, age over 35. The placenta will not separate, causing severe bleeding; known cases get a planned **cesarean hysterectomy**.
- **Cervical insufficiency** (p. 56): painless dilation in the second trimester; causes include cone biopsy, DES, past trauma; treat with **cerclage**.

## Rh (D) alloimmunization (pp. 62-64)
1. An Rh-negative mother meets Rh-positive fetal red cells, most often **at delivery**, also with abortion, ectopic, amniocentesis, abruption or previa (p. 63).
2. She makes **IgG** anti-D, which crosses the placenta in the **next** pregnancy and destroys fetal red cells: anemia, **hydrops fetalis**, death (p. 62).
3. **Prevention:** anti-D immune globulin (RhoGAM) at **28 weeks**, within **72 hours** of delivery of an Rh-positive baby, and after CVS, amniocentesis or D&C. 28 weeks is chosen because its half-life (about 6 weeks) covers the rest of the third trimester (p. 64).
4. The **Kleihauer-Betke** test measures how much fetal blood entered the mother, to decide whether more than one dose is needed (p. 64).
5. Antibodies are measured with the **indirect Coombs** test as doubling titers (1:2, 1:4, 1:8...) (p. 62).

## Hypertension in pregnancy (pp. 73-84)
| | Starts | Proteinuria | Your notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Chronic hypertension | **Before 20 weeks** or before pregnancy | Maybe (kidney disease) | p. 80 |
| Gestational hypertension | After 20 weeks | **No** | 30% become preeclampsia (p. 75) |
| **Preeclampsia** | After 20 weeks | **300 mg/24 h or protein/creatinine 0.3 or more** | p. 75 |
| Severe features | | | BP **160/110** or more, headache, vision changes, **epigastric/RUQ pain**, platelets under 100,000, liver enzymes doubled, creatinine over 1.1, pulmonary edema (p. 77) |
| **Eclampsia** | | | Preeclampsia **plus seizures** (p. 79) |
| **HELLP** | | | **H**emolysis (schistocytes), **E**levated **L**iver enzymes, **L**ow **P**latelets; RUQ pain from a stretched liver capsule (p. 81) |

- **Mechanism:** loss of the normal resistance to angiotensin and a shift toward **thromboxane** (constricts) over **prostacyclin** (dilates) cause diffuse **vasospasm** and leaky capillaries: poor flow to placenta (growth restriction), kidneys, liver and brain (p. 76).
- **Risk factors:** first pregnancy (8 times), twins, **hydatidiform mole**, diabetes, chronic hypertension, kidney disease, age extremes (p. 75).
- **Prevention:** **low-dose aspirin** from 12-28 weeks (best before 16) for high-risk women (p. 75). MSD: 81 mg/day ([MSD Manual: Preeclampsia and Eclampsia](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/gynecology-and-obstetrics/antenatal-complications/preeclampsia-and-eclampsia)).
- **Cure = delivery.** **Magnesium sulfate** prevents and stops eclamptic seizures and is continued 24 h after delivery; BP is lowered with IV **hydralazine or labetalol** to a diastolic of 90-100 (pp. 78-79).
- **Magnesium toxicity:** sleepiness, **lost reflexes**, slowed breathing; treat with **calcium gluconate**; risk is higher with kidney impairment because magnesium leaves through the kidneys (p. 84).
- **Never in pregnancy:** **ACE inhibitors** (fetal kidney failure, oligohydramnios, skull defects) and starting diuretics (p. 82). MSD adds **ARBs** ([MSD Manual: Hypertension in Pregnancy](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/gynecology-and-obstetrics/pregnancy-complicated-by-disease/hypertension-in-pregnancy)).

**Recent change (preeclampsia without protein in urine):** your notes require proteinuria (p. 75). MSD says preeclampsia can also be diagnosed without proteinuria when new hypertension comes with "new-onset signs of end-organ damage," such as low platelets, raised liver enzymes, kidney impairment, pulmonary edema, headache or visual symptoms ([MSD Manual: Preeclampsia and Eclampsia](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/gynecology-and-obstetrics/antenatal-complications/preeclampsia-and-eclampsia)).

**Other source (magnesium without severe features):** your notes give magnesium to every preeclamptic woman delivered at 37 weeks or more (p. 77). MSD calls this "controversial" and says magnesium is "usually not" given in mild preeclampsia; it is given with severe features (same MSD page).

**Other source (first-choice oral drug):** your notes call **methyldopa** the drug of choice (p. 82). MSD lists methyldopa, beta-blockers and calcium channel blockers as first-line, calls labetalol "the most commonly used beta-blocker," and says extended-release **nifedipine** "may be preferred" because it is once a day ([MSD Manual: Hypertension in Pregnancy](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/gynecology-and-obstetrics/pregnancy-complicated-by-disease/hypertension-in-pregnancy)).

## Diabetes in pregnancy (pp. 85-87)
- **Gestational diabetes** comes from placental hormones (hPL) causing insulin resistance (see guide 36). Screening and the 3-hour test cut-offs are in guide 36 (p. 86).
- **Treatment:** diet first (controls 80%); target fasting under 90 and 1-hour after meals under 140; **insulin** if targets are missed (pp. 86-87).

**Other source (glucose targets):** MSD gives fasting "< 95 mg/dL" and "2-hour postprandial levels at ≤ 120 mg/dL", with no 1-hour target ([MSD Manual: Diabetes Mellitus in Pregnancy](https://msdmanuals.com/en-in/professional/gynecology-and-obstetrics/pregnancy-complicated-by-disease/diabetes-mellitus-in-pregnancy)).
- **Birth defects** come from high sugar **during organ formation**, so they rise with **pre-existing** diabetes but **not** with gestational diabetes. Most common: **neural tube defects and heart defects**; most specific: **caudal regression syndrome** (p. 87).
- Folic acid **4 mg** a day from 3 months before conception in women with pre-existing diabetes (p. 87).

**Other source (pills for gestational diabetes):** your notes say **glyburide** is being used (p. 87). MSD does not mention glyburide; it says insulin is used when diet fails for 2 weeks or more and that **metformin** "is safe during pregnancy" and controls glucose as well as insulin. MSD also lists the newborn problems: hypoglycemia, low calcium, jaundice, polycythemia and breathing trouble ([MSD Manual: Diabetes Mellitus in Pregnancy](https://msdmanuals.com/en-in/professional/gynecology-and-obstetrics/pregnancy-complicated-by-disease/diabetes-mellitus-in-pregnancy)).

## After delivery (pp. 130-141)
### Postpartum hemorrhage (pp. 132-137)
Definition p. 132; causes and their shares pp. 133-134.

| Cause | Share | Clue | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Uterine atony** | **80%** | **Soft, boggy** uterus above the umbilicus; risks: long labor, big baby, twins, oxytocin exposure | Bimanual massage, **oxytocin**, then tranexamic acid, carboprost, methylergonovine |
| Lacerations | 15% | Bleeding with a **firm** uterus | Repair |
| Retained placenta | 5% | Missing cotyledons, firm uterus | Manual removal or curettage |
| DIC | Rare | Oozing from IV sites; after abruption, severe preeclampsia, amniotic fluid embolism, dead fetus | Empty uterus, blood products |
| **Uterine inversion** | Rare | Beefy mass in the vagina, **no fundus** felt in the abdomen | Push it back **first**, then uterotonics; leave an attached placenta until after |

- **Drug cautions:** **carboprost** (a prostaglandin) causes bronchospasm, so **not in asthma**; **methylergonovine** constricts vessels, so **not in hypertension**; tranexamic acid works best within 3 hours (pp. 133-134).
- **Sheehan syndrome:** the pituitary grows during pregnancy, so heavy postpartum bleeding and low blood pressure can infarct it; the first sign is often **failure to breastfeed** (p. 13; failure of lactation is standard teaching).

**Recent change (how much blood is a hemorrhage):** your notes define it as **500 mL or more after vaginal birth or 1,000 mL after cesarean** (p. 132). MSD now defines it as "blood loss of **> 1000 mL** or blood loss accompanied by symptoms or signs of hypovolemia" within 24 hours ([MSD Manual: Postpartum Hemorrhage](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/gynecology-and-obstetrics/intrapartum-complications/postpartum-hemorrhage)).

### Postpartum fever (pp. 138-141)
Fever 38 °C (100.4 °F) or more twice, 6 or more hours apart, after the first 24 hours (p. 138).

| Day | Cause | Clue | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Atelectasis | Mild fever, crackles, shallow breathing after general anesthesia | Deep breathing, incentive spirometer |
| 1-2 | Urinary tract infection | Flank pain, positive urinalysis | IV antibiotic |
| 2-3 | **Endometritis** (most common) | **Very tender uterus**, after **cesarean** with long labor and long ruptured membranes; polymicrobial | **Clindamycin plus gentamicin** |
| 4-5 | Wound infection | Red, draining incision | Antibiotics; drain an abscess |
| 5-6 | **Septic pelvic thrombophlebitis** | **Fever spikes despite antibiotics**, normal exam | **Heparin** plus antibiotics |

- Postpartum normals that are not disease: shivering, lochia (red, then pink, then white over 2-3 weeks), cramps, engorgement on days 3-5 without fever (p. 130).
- Contraception: **no estrogen pills** while breastfeeding (less milk); if not breastfeeding, wait 3 weeks (clot risk) (p. 131).

## Clinical vignette
A 32-year-old at 33 weeks who uses cocaine has sudden, constant abdominal pain and dark vaginal bleeding. Her uterus is hard and tender, and the fetal heart tracing loses variability. Her platelets are low and fibrinogen is falling.
- **Diagnosis:** abruptio placentae with DIC.
- **Why:** painful late bleeding over a normally placed placenta; cocaine and hypertension are risk factors; tissue factor from the bleeding site sets off DIC (El-Husseiny Obstetrics, pp. 47-49).
- **Next steps:** large IV lines, fluids, blood products, left-side position, and emergency cesarean if mother or fetus is in danger (p. 49).

## Common traps
- **No vaginal exam in late bleeding until previa is ruled out.**
- **Painful = abruption; painless = previa; painless plus fetal bradycardia after membranes rupture = vasa previa.**
- **Closed cervix with bleeding and a live fetus = threatened**; open cervix = inevitable or incomplete.
- **Gestational diabetes does not cause birth defects**; pre-existing diabetes does.
- **Seizure in a hypertensive pregnant woman after 20 weeks = eclampsia: protect the airway, then magnesium sulfate** (p. 79).
- **Areflexia on magnesium = toxicity: calcium gluconate.**
- **Asthma → no carboprost; hypertension → no methylergonovine.**
- **Fever that keeps spiking on antibiotics with a normal exam = septic pelvic thrombophlebitis → heparin.**

## Practice questions
1. A woman at 7 weeks has spotting and cramps. The cervix is closed and ultrasound shows a live embryo with a heartbeat. Diagnosis?
   - **Answer:** threatened abortion.
   - **Why:** a viable pregnancy with bleeding and no cervical dilation; half continue to term; observe only (El-Husseiny Obstetrics, p. 36).
   - **Trap:** inevitable abortion, which needs an open cervix.
2. A G1 at 35 weeks has BP 168/112, a headache that will not go away and platelets of 85,000. What drug prevents the most dangerous complication?
   - **Answer:** IV magnesium sulfate (prevents eclamptic seizures).
   - **Why:** BP of 160/110 or more, persistent headache and platelets under 100,000 are severe features; magnesium is continued 24 h after delivery, which is the cure (pp. 77-78).
   - **Trap:** choosing an antihypertensive; BP control is needed too, but magnesium prevents the seizures.
3. Minutes after delivery of a 4.4 kg baby after a 20-hour labor, a woman bleeds heavily. Her uterus is soft and above the umbilicus. She has asthma. Oxytocin is running; the team chooses a second uterotonic but leaves out carboprost. Why is carboprost withheld for her?
   - **Answer:** carboprost is a prostaglandin that causes bronchospasm.
   - **Why:** atony (boggy uterus after a long labor and a big baby) is 80% of postpartum hemorrhage; carboprost is a prostaglandin that causes bronchospasm, so it is not used in asthma (pp. 133-134).
   - **Trap:** blaming high blood pressure; that is the reason to withhold methylergonovine.
4. An Rh-negative woman has just delivered an Rh-positive baby after a placental abruption. What test decides how much anti-D immune globulin she needs?
   - **Answer:** the Kleihauer-Betke test.
   - **Why:** it measures the volume of fetal blood in her circulation; a big bleed such as an abruption may need more than one vial (p. 64).
   - **Trap:** the direct Coombs test, which looks for antibody on red cells.
5. Day 5 after an emergency cesarean, a woman still spikes fevers after 72 hours of clindamycin and gentamicin. Her exam, urine and blood cultures are normal. Next treatment?
   - **Answer:** add heparin (septic pelvic thrombophlebitis).
   - **Why:** persistent fever despite broad antibiotics with a normal exam and negative workup points to infected pelvic vein clots, a diagnosis of exclusion treated with anticoagulation plus antibiotics (pp. 140-141).
   - **Trap:** changing antibiotics again.
