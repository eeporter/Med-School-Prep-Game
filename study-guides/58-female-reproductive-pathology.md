## In one line
Sort female reproductive disease by **organ**, as your notes do (NBME TC p. 354):
- **Ovary:** PCOS and ovarian tumors (surface, germ cell, stromal, metastatic).
- **Uterus:** endometriosis and endometrial cancer.
- **Cervix:** HPV.

Then **link each disease to its hormone**:
- high LH and androgens in PCOS;
- estrogen in granulosa cell tumors;
- androgens in Sertoli-Leydig tumors;
- AFP in yolk sac tumors;
- hCG in choriocarcinoma;
- unopposed estrogen in endometrial hyperplasia and cancer (NBME TC pp. 351-364).

## Disorders of sex development (NBME TC pp. 342-350)
| Picture in the question | Diagnosis | Key points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **No sense of smell** plus primary amenorrhea (girls) or low sperm count and delayed puberty (boys) | **Kallmann syndrome**: GnRH neurons fail to migrate from the olfactory area to the hypothalamus | **Low GnRH, FSH, LH and sex steroids**. Anosmia; renal agenesis is linked. The olfactory nerve exits through the cribriform plate (pp. 343, 350) |
| **Short stature plus primary amenorrhea**, broad chest with widely spaced nipples | **Turner syndrome (45,X, or mosaic)**: nondisjunction | **Streak ovaries**, so no estrogen or progesterone and **high FSH/LH**. **Bicuspid aortic valve and coarctation** (rib notching). Short stature from loss of one SHOX gene (pp. 343, 345-346, 350) |
| Tall male, small testes, gynecomastia | **Klinefelter syndrome (47,XXY)** | Seminiferous tubule dysgenesis, so **high FSH/LH**. Reduced body hair, poorer neurocognitive function, tall stature from late growth-plate closure, a **Barr body** (p. 346) |
| Primary amenorrhea, **blind-ending vagina**, breasts present, **pubic hair present** | **Müllerian agenesis (46,XX)** | No uterus. Normal ovaries, so normal estrogen and breasts (pp. 348-350) |
| Primary amenorrhea, blind vagina, breasts present, **scant pubic hair**, inguinal or labial masses | **Androgen insensitivity (46,XY)** | Testes are present and are **removed because of tumor risk** (your notes: seminoma) (p. 349) |
| Ambiguous genitalia at birth; **no penile growth at puberty** | **5-alpha-reductase deficiency** | Male internal organs. Testosterone can't be converted to DHT (dihydrotestosterone) (p. 347) |
| A **mother virilized during pregnancy** and a female baby with ambiguous genitalia | **Placental aromatase deficiency** | The placenta can't convert androgens to estrogen (p. 352) |

## PCOS, now called PMOS (NBME TC pp. 351-353; MSD)
- **Mechanism:**
  - **High LH** drives the theca cells to make **androgens**.
  - Extra fat makes **estrone**, which lowers FSH.
  - The granulosa cells atrophy, and follicles dilate into "cysts" (p. 351).
- **Diagnosis (Rotterdam, at least 2 of 3):** oligo- or anovulation; clinical or lab signs of excess androgen; polycystic ovaries on imaging. Other causes must be excluded (MSD; 2023 International Guideline).
- **Risks:**
  - **Endometrial hyperplasia and cancer**, because estrogen is not opposed by progesterone.
  - Insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes, metabolic syndrome and cardiovascular disease (MSD; NBME TC p. 364).
- **Treatment** (your notes, p. 351):
  - lifestyle change;
  - **combined OCPs**, which regulate periods, treat acne and hirsutism and prevent pregnancy (often first-line per MSD);
  - **spironolactone**, an androgen receptor blocker for hirsutism (needs contraception because it may harm a fetus, per MSD);
  - **metformin**, which lowers insulin resistance;
  - **clomiphene**, which blocks hypothalamic estrogen receptors and triggers LH and FSH release.
- **High-androgen states to tell apart (p. 352):**

| Cause | Clue |
| --- | --- |
| PCOS | High LH |
| Nonclassic CAH | Around puberty; **high 17-hydroxyprogesterone** |
| Ovarian androgen tumor | **Sertoli-Leydig** cell tumor; rapid virilization |
| Adrenal androgen tumor | **High DHEA-S** |
| Cushing syndrome or anabolic steroids | Steroid source |

- **OCPs and the liver:** a young woman on OCPs with RUQ (right upper quadrant) pain and shock may have a ruptured **hepatocellular adenoma**, a benign liver tumor (pp. 352-353).

**Recent change (new name, 2026):** PCOS has been renamed **polyendocrine metabolic ovarian syndrome (PMOS)** through a global consensus published in *The Lancet* on 12 May 2026 (Teede et al.).
- **Why:** the "cysts" are arrested follicles, and the old name hid the endocrine and metabolic features.
- **Transition:** the switch will take **3 years** ([Contemporary OB/GYN, 12 May 2026](https://contemporaryobgyn.net/view/global-consensus-renames-pcos-to-polyendocrine-metabolic-ovarian-syndrome-pmos-)). MSD now uses PMOS.
- **For exams:** recognize **both names**. Questions written before 2026 will say PCOS.

**Other source (metformin and fertility):** your notes say metformin "can induce ovulation" (p. 351). MSD agrees it may induce ovulation (so contraception is needed if pregnancy is not wanted), but says it has **little or no benefit for infertility**, hirsutism or acne; it mainly fixes the metabolic problems and regularizes cycles. For exams: metformin treats insulin resistance; it is not the drug of choice to achieve pregnancy.

**Other source (ovulation induction):** your notes (p. 351) list **clomiphene**, and MSD calls clomiphene **first-line** for PMOS infertility, with letrozole as an alternative. The **2023 International PCOS Guideline** strongly recommends **letrozole as the first-line drug** for ovulation induction in anovulatory women with PCOS (Teede et al., *Hum Reprod* 2023;38:1655-1679, rec. 5.3.1). It also notes that letrozole is off-label in many countries.
- If the choices include letrozole, it is the best current answer.
- If only clomiphene is offered, choose clomiphene.

**Correction (what letrozole is for):** your Pharmacology High Yield notes (PDF p. 108) list the aromatase inhibitors (letrozole, anastrozole and others) as used "to reduce androgen induced prostate Ca." The FDA label lists letrozole only for **hormone receptor-positive breast cancer in postmenopausal women**: adjuvant, extended adjuvant and advanced disease ([Femara label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2017/020726s033lbl.pdf)). It is not used for prostate cancer. Its other well-known use is ovulation induction in PCOS (off-label, per the guideline above).

## Ovarian tumors (NBME TC pp. 355-359; MSD)
**Four groups:** surface (epithelial), germ cell, stromal, and metastatic (p. 355).

**Surface (epithelial) tumors: about 90%** (p. 356; MSD)
- **Presentation:** fullness, **early satiety** and bloating. **CA-125** is the marker (pp. 355-356).
- **Serous** (watery):
  - Cystadenoma is benign.
  - **Cystadenocarcinoma is often bilateral**, and its papillary cores calcify into **psammoma bodies** (pp. 356, 365).
  - MSD: high-grade serous is the most common epithelial cancer (over 70%), and many start in the **fallopian tube**.
- **Mucinous** (thick mucus): cystadenoma is a multilocular mass. Mucinous cystadenocarcinoma is **unilateral**; your notes link it to gelatinous ascites (pp. 356, 359).
- **Risk factors (MSD):** **BRCA1/2**, nulliparity, early menarche, late menopause.
- **Protective (MSD):** **OCPs**, with about 50% risk reduction after 5 years.

**Germ cell tumors: young women** (p. 357)
| Tumor | Clues |
| --- | --- |
| **Mature cystic teratoma** (benign) | Tissue from all **three germ layers** (hair, teeth). Can cause **ovarian torsion**. **Struma ovarii** = thyroid tissue, causing hyperthyroidism (tachycardia, palpitations). Your notes say to **rule out squamous cell carcinoma or immature tissue** (p. 357) |
| **Yolk sac (endodermal sinus) tumor** | Young patient; **high AFP**; **glomeruloid structures** (p. 357), called Schiller-Duval bodies (standard teaching). **Read more on Radiopaedia:** [Schiller-Duval body](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/schiller-duval-body-histology) |
| **Dysgerminoma** | Female version of **seminoma**. Often makes **LDH** (Bouquet de Jolinière 2014) |
| **Choriocarcinoma** | No villi (only syncytiotrophoblast and cytotrophoblast); **spreads through the blood** (to the lungs) (p. 357). Makes **hCG** (Bouquet de Jolinière 2014) |

- Germ cell tumors usually occur under age 30 (MSD) and are rare over 40 (Bouquet de Jolinière 2014).

**Stromal (sex cord-stromal) tumors** (p. 358)
| Tumor | Hormone | Clues |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Granulosa-theca** | **Estrogen** | **Precocious puberty**, heavy periods, **postmenopausal bleeding**, thick endometrium; **Call-Exner bodies** |
| **Sertoli-Leydig** | **Androgens** | **Rapid virilization** and facial hair; **Reinke crystals** |
| **Fibroma/thecoma** | None (fibroma) | **Meigs syndrome**: fibroma + **ascites + pleural (right) effusion** (p. 358; MSD) |

**Metastatic: Krukenberg tumor** (p. 359)
- Diffuse **gastric cancer** with **signet-ring cells** (mucin pushes the nucleus aside) spreads to **both ovaries**.
- Bilateral vs unilateral is the clue: Krukenberg is bilateral, primary mucinous cancer is unilateral.

**Ligaments: what to tie off** (p. 360)
| Ligament | Connects | Contains |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Infundibulopelvic (suspensory ligament of the ovary)** | Ovary to pelvic wall | **Ovarian vessels**. Ligate it in oophorectomy |
| **Cardinal** | Cervix to pelvic wall | **Uterine vessels** |
| Broad | Uterus, tubes and ovaries to the pelvic wall | |
| Round | Uterine fundus through the inguinal canal to the labia majora | |
| Ovarian ligament | Ovary to the side of the uterus | Not the same as the suspensory ligament |

## Uterus (NBME TC pp. 361-365; MSD)
**Endometriosis**
- **Definition:** endometrial **glands and stroma outside the uterus**. Think of it in a menstruating woman with **cyclic pelvic pain** (p. 361).
- **Theories:** **retrograde menstruation** (the most accepted, per MSD), **metaplasia** of Müllerian cells, and **lymphatic** spread (p. 362).
- **Classic triad (MSD):** **dysmenorrhea, dyspareunia, infertility**. There may also be painful urination or defecation.
- **Where the tissue is, and what it causes (p. 362):**
  - uterosacral ligaments: **palpable nodules**;
  - pouch of Douglas: **pain with defecation**;
  - bladder wall: painful urination;
  - bowel surface: "**gunpowder**" lesions;
  - fallopian tube: **ectopic pregnancy**;
  - ovary: **endometrioma ("chocolate cyst")** (MSD).
- **Exam:** may be normal, or a **fixed, retroverted uterus** (MSD).
- **Diagnosis:** suspected from the typical symptoms. Imaging often misses it, though an endometrioma on ultrasound or MRI is a strong clue. It is confirmed by **direct visualization, usually pelvic laparoscopy**; biopsy is not required but confirms it (MSD).
- **First treatment:** **NSAIDs and hormonal contraceptives** (MSD).

**Other source (endometrial tissue in the uterine wall):** your notes' table lists myometrium under endometriosis, with **heavy periods and an enlarged "boggy" uterus** (p. 362). MSD calls this a separate condition, **adenomyosis** (glands and stroma inside the uterine muscle). It causes heavy, painful periods and a **diffusely (globular) enlarged uterus**, usually no bigger than 12 weeks' size. On the exam, the name for this is **adenomyosis**.

**Anovulatory bleeding** (p. 363)
- No ovulation means no corpus luteum and **no progesterone**. Estrogen keeps building the endometrium until it sheds irregularly.
- It is most common in **adolescents** and in women over 45 (MSD, AUB-O). Your notes' example is a 14-year-old (pp. 363, 369).

**Endometrial hyperplasia and cancer** (pp. 364-365; MSD)
- **Unopposed estrogen risk factors (p. 364):**
  - obesity;
  - nulliparity;
  - **late menopause (after 52)**;
  - PCOS;
  - anovulatory cycles;
  - estrogen-only therapy;
  - estrogen-secreting tumors.
  - MSD adds **tamoxifen** and **Lynch syndrome**.
- **Path to cancer:** hyperplasia, then atypia (dysplasia), then carcinoma.
- **Presentation:** **postmenopausal bleeding** (p. 364). Over 90% of patients have abnormal bleeding (MSD).
- **First test:** **endometrial biopsy** (MSD).

| | Type I (hyperplasia pathway) | Type II (sporadic) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Age | **50-60** | **70-80** |
| Histology | **Endometrioid** | **Serous papillary** (psammoma bodies) |
| Background | Estrogen, hyperplasia | **Atrophic** endometrium, **p53** mutations |
| Behavior | Less aggressive | Aggressive (MSD) |

- **Psammoma bodies** (laminated calcified spheres) appear in "**PSaMMoma**" tumors: **P**apillary thyroid, **S**erous ovary and uterus, **M**eningioma, **M**esothelioma (p. 365).

## Cervix: HPV (NBME TC p. 366; MSD)
- **HPV** is a DNA virus that infects the **transformation zone**. Persistent high-risk infection leads to **CIN**, then **squamous cell carcinoma**. CD8+ T cells clear most infections (p. 366).
- **Strains (your notes):** high-risk **16, 18 and 31**; low-risk **6 and 11** (warts).
- **MSD facts:** types 16 and 18 cause about **70%** of precancer and cancer. Squamous cell carcinoma is **80-85%** of cervical cancers, and nearly all start in the transformation zone.
- **E6** inactivates **p53**: it recruits the E6AP ubiquitin ligase, and **p53 is degraded**, so damaged cells don't die (p. 366; Li 2019).
- **E7** inactivates **Rb**, which frees E2F and pushes the cell from G1 into S phase (p. 366).

**Correction (E7):** your notes (p. 366) say E7 causes "phosphorylation of Rb." E7 actually **binds Rb directly** and targets it for breakdown. This releases E2F, with the same effect as phosphorylation, but E7 does not phosphorylate Rb (Todorovic et al., *J Virol* 2012; Li et al., *Front Microbiol* 2019). Answer "**E7 binds and inactivates Rb**."

## Clinical vignette
A 26-year-old woman has had 4-5 periods a year since menarche. She has acne and coarse hair on her chin and lower abdomen. Her BMI is 33, and she has dark velvety skin on her neck. Her LH is elevated, 17-hydroxyprogesterone and DHEA-S are normal, and a pregnancy test is negative. She is not trying to conceive.

- **Diagnosis:** **PCOS (now called PMOS).** She has ovulatory dysfunction plus clinical hyperandrogenism, which is 2 of the 3 Rotterdam criteria (MSD). High LH fits (NBME TC p. 352).
- **Why not nonclassic CAH:** 17-hydroxyprogesterone is normal. **Why not an androgen tumor:** DHEA-S is normal and the course is gradual (p. 352).
- **Dark neck skin:** acanthosis nigricans, a sign of **insulin resistance** (standard teaching).
- **Treatment:** weight loss and **combined OCPs**, which control her cycles and acne and protect the endometrium. Add **spironolactone** for hirsutism, with contraception (MSD; NBME TC p. 351).
- **Long-term risk:** **endometrial hyperplasia and cancer**, because her estrogen is unopposed. Also diabetes (MSD).

## Common traps
- **Anosmia plus no puberty = Kallmann** (low GnRH, FSH and LH). **Turner and Klinefelter have high FSH/LH** (NBME TC pp. 343, 346).
- **Blind vagina with breasts: check the pubic hair.** Present means Müllerian agenesis (46,XX). Scant means AIS (46,XY), so remove the testes (p. 349).
- **PCOS = high LH, androgens and unopposed estrogen.** It was renamed **PMOS in 2026**; know both names.
- **Ovulation induction in PCOS: letrozole is first-line in the 2023 guideline.** Clomiphene is in your notes and in MSD.
- **Letrozole treats breast cancer (postmenopausal, receptor-positive), not prostate cancer** (FDA label).
- **Ovarian markers:**
  - CA-125 for epithelial tumors;
  - AFP for yolk sac tumors;
  - hCG for choriocarcinoma;
  - LDH for dysgerminoma;
  - estrogen for granulosa tumors;
  - androgens for Sertoli-Leydig tumors.
- **Bilateral ovarian masses with signet-ring cells = Krukenberg (gastric).** Serous cancer is often bilateral; mucinous is unilateral (pp. 356, 359).
- **Fibroma + ascites + pleural effusion = Meigs syndrome** (p. 358; MSD).
- **Oophorectomy bleeding: tie the infundibulopelvic (suspensory) ligament**, which holds the ovarian vessels (p. 360).
- **Endometriosis:** retrograde menstruation; uterosacral nodules; chocolate cyst; confirmed by direct visualization, usually laparoscopy (MSD).
- **Tissue in the uterine muscle = adenomyosis:** enlarged, globular uterus with heavy, painful periods (MSD).
- **Endometrial cancer:** type I is endometrioid, estrogen-driven, from hyperplasia, in women aged 50-60. Type II is serous, p53, atrophic, in women aged 70-80 (p. 365; MSD).
- **HPV:** E6 degrades p53; E7 **binds** Rb (not phosphorylation). Types 16 and 18 cause about 70% (MSD).

## Practice questions
Answer, then check the explanation.

1. A 17-year-old boy has delayed puberty, small testes and sparse pubic hair. He has never been able to smell coffee or smoke. His height is normal for his family. Which set of lab results is expected?
   - **Answer:** **low LH, low FSH and low testosterone.**
   - **Why:** Kallmann syndrome. GnRH neurons fail to migrate from the olfactory area, so all the downstream hormones are low (NBME TC p. 343).
   - **Trap:** **high LH and FSH with low testosterone**, which fits primary testicular failure such as Klinefelter (p. 346).

2. A 15-year-old girl has never had a period. She is 142 cm tall and has a broad chest. Blood pressure is higher in her arms than her legs. Which finding in her ovaries is expected?
   - **Answer:** **streak ovaries**, with high FSH and LH.
   - **Why:** Turner syndrome. Ovarian failure means no estrogen. The arm-leg pressure gap fits coarctation of the aorta (NBME TC pp. 343, 346).
   - **Trap:** **normal ovaries with an absent uterus**, which is Müllerian agenesis.

3. A 17-year-old girl has primary amenorrhea. She has normal breast development but almost no pubic or axillary hair. Exam shows a short blind-ending vagina and firm bilateral masses in the labia. Which is the most appropriate long-term step regarding the masses?
   - **Answer:** **surgical removal of the gonads (testes)** because of cancer risk.
   - **Why:** androgen insensitivity (46,XY). The testes in the labia carry a germ cell tumor risk (your notes: seminoma) (NBME TC p. 349).
   - **Trap:** **leave them in place, because they are benign ovaries**.

4. A 28-year-old woman with PCOS has been trying to conceive for 18 months. She has irregular cycles and no ovulation, and her partner's semen analysis and her tubal study are normal. Which drug is the recommended first-line choice to induce ovulation?
   - **Answer:** **letrozole.**
   - **Why:** the 2023 International PCOS Guideline strongly recommends letrozole as first-line for ovulation induction in anovulatory PCOS (rec. 5.3.1). Your notes (p. 351) and MSD list clomiphene first; the 2023 guideline prefers letrozole.
   - **Trap:** **metformin alone**. Your notes say it can induce ovulation, but MSD says it gives little or no benefit for infertility (see the Other source note).

5. A 25-year-old woman who has taken a combined oral contraceptive for 6 years for PCOS is brought in pale and confused after collapsing at work. CT shows a large hemoperitoneum from a ruptured mass in the right lobe of the liver. Which hemodynamic pattern is expected?
   - **Answer:** **low cardiac output, high systemic vascular resistance and low wedge pressure (PCWP).**
   - **Why:** a hepatocellular adenoma (linked to OCP use) has bled into the abdomen. Blood loss causes hypovolemic shock: less preload, so low PCWP and low output, with reflex vasoconstriction raising SVR (NBME TC pp. 352-353).
   - **Trap:** **high cardiac output with low SVR**, which is distributive (septic) shock (standard teaching).

6. A 39-year-old woman with a BRCA1 mutation has finished having children. She is counseled about risk-reducing surgery, and the surgeon explains that studies of women like her changed our view of where the most common lethal "ovarian" cancer begins. Most high-grade serous carcinomas that present as an ovarian mass arise from which site?
   - **Answer:** **the fallopian tube.**
   - **Why:** MSD says most high-grade serous carcinomas that show up as an ovarian mass or peritoneal disease start in the fallopian tubes, based on risk-reducing surgery in BRCA carriers. That is why the operation is a bilateral **salpingo**-oophorectomy. Serous tumors are surface epithelial tumors in your notes (p. 356).
   - **Trap:** **ovarian stromal (granulosa) cells**, which give sex cord-stromal tumors, not serous carcinoma.

7. A 19-year-old woman has a rapidly enlarging pelvic mass. Serum AFP is markedly elevated, and hCG is normal. Histology shows structures that look like glomeruli. Which is the diagnosis?
   - **Answer:** **yolk sac (endodermal sinus) tumor.**
   - **Why:** a young patient, high AFP and glomeruloid structures (NBME TC p. 357), called Schiller-Duval bodies (standard teaching).
   - **Trap:** **dysgerminoma**, which raises LDH and is the counterpart of seminoma.

8. A 58-year-old woman has vaginal bleeding 6 years after menopause. Ultrasound shows a solid ovarian mass and a thick endometrium. Histology of the mass shows small follicle-like spaces filled with eosinophilic material. Which hormone is the tumor most likely making?
   - **Answer:** **estrogen.**
   - **Why:** a granulosa cell tumor (Call-Exner bodies) makes estrogen, which causes endometrial thickening and postmenopausal bleeding (NBME TC p. 358).
   - **Trap:** **testosterone**, which fits a Sertoli-Leydig tumor with virilization.

9. A 52-year-old woman has bilateral ovarian masses. She has had weight loss and stomach pain. Biopsy of the ovaries shows cells full of mucin with nuclei pushed to one side. Where did the cancer most likely start?
   - **Answer:** **the stomach** (diffuse gastric cancer). This is a Krukenberg tumor.
   - **Why:** signet-ring cells plus bilateral ovarian involvement point to metastasis from diffuse gastric cancer (NBME TC p. 359).
   - **Trap:** **primary mucinous ovarian cystadenocarcinoma**, which is usually unilateral.

10. During a hysterectomy that leaves both ovaries in place, the surgeon clamps the band of tissue running from the cervix to the lateral pelvic wall to control the main blood supply of the uterus. Which ligament is being clamped?
    - **Answer:** **the cardinal ligament**, which carries the uterine vessels.
    - **Why:** your notes' ligament chart: cardinal ligament = cervix to pelvic wall, with the uterine vessels (NBME TC p. 360). The ureter runs just under the uterine artery here, so it is at risk when this ligament is clamped (standard teaching).
    - **Trap:** **the infundibulopelvic (suspensory) ligament**, which carries the ovarian vessels and is tied when the ovary is removed.

11. A 31-year-old woman has painful periods that have worsened over 3 years. She also has deep pain during sex, pain with bowel movements during her period, and 2 years of infertility. Exam shows a fixed, retroverted uterus with tender nodules along the uterosacral ligaments. How is the diagnosis confirmed?
    - **Answer:** **laparoscopy** (direct visualization of implants).
    - **Why:** endometriosis, with the classic triad, dyschezia and uterosacral nodules (NBME TC p. 362). MSD: confirmed by direct visualization, usually pelvic laparoscopy; biopsy is not required but confirms it.
    - **Trap:** **CA-125 level**, which is not diagnostic.

12. A 74-year-old thin woman has postmenopausal bleeding. She never used hormone therapy. Endometrial biopsy shows an aggressive papillary tumor with psammoma bodies, arising in an atrophic endometrium. Which molecular change is most associated with this tumor?
    - **Answer:** **TP53 (p53) mutation.**
    - **Why:** type II (serous) endometrial cancer occurs in older women with atrophic endometrium and is linked to p53 (NBME TC p. 365; MSD).
    - **Trap:** **unopposed estrogen with hyperplasia**, which is the type I (endometrioid) pathway.

13. A 35-year-old woman has a cervical biopsy showing high-grade dysplasia. HPV type 16 is found. Which viral protein action most directly prevents apoptosis of the infected cells?
    - **Answer:** **E6 promoting degradation of p53.**
    - **Why:** E6 recruits E6AP to ubiquitinate p53, so damaged cells don't undergo apoptosis (Li 2019; NBME TC p. 366).
    - **Trap:** **E7 phosphorylating Rb**. E7 binds Rb, releasing E2F to drive the cell cycle; it does not phosphorylate Rb, and it isn't the anti-apoptosis step.
