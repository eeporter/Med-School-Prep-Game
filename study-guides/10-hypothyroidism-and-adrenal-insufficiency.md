## In one line
For any gland, find the level of the problem with the trophic hormone: if the gland is failing, its pituitary hormone (TSH or ACTH) is high; if the pituitary is failing, both are low. In the adrenal, only primary failure loses aldosterone (high potassium) and raises ACTH (dark skin).

## Core mechanism: thyroid
1. Hashimoto thyroiditis (autoimmune) is the most common cause in the US; iodine deficiency is the leading cause worldwide. [StatPearls: Hypothyroidism](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK519536/)
2. Primary hypothyroidism: high TSH, low free T4. Subclinical: high TSH, normal free T4. Central: judge by free T4, because TSH may be normal-looking but inactive. [StatPearls: Hypothyroidism](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK519536/)
3. Thyroid hormone sets the metabolic rate, so lack of it slows everything: cold intolerance, weight gain, constipation, bradycardia, slow relaxation of reflexes, high lipids and high creatine kinase (CK). [StatPearls: Hypothyroidism](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK519536/)
4. **Your notes on thyroid tests and systemic effects (El-Husseiny Endocrinology, pp. 69-71):** small changes in thyroid hormone cause big changes in TSH, so TSH rises before T4 falls and is the single best screening test. High TRH in primary hypothyroidism also releases **prolactin**: galactorrhea and amenorrhea, or more often irregular cycles with heavy periods, in women; infertility and gynecomastia in men. Hypothyroid myopathy gives aches, proximal weakness and cramps with a **high CK**, even without symptoms. Check TSH in anyone with unexplained high CK, high cholesterol or heavy periods.
5. **Hashimoto thyroiditis in your notes (p. 71):** the most common cause where iodine is sufficient; more women than men. Histology: dense lymphocytes and plasma cells with **germinal centers** and large pink **Hürthle cells**. Antibodies: anti-thyroid peroxidase (anti-microsomal) and anti-thyroglobulin. It can be briefly **hyperthyroid early** as follicles rupture, and the gland goes from diffusely enlarged to small and fibrotic. Linked to HLA-DR3 and HLA-DR5 and a higher risk of B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma. NBME Top Concepts adds the same antibodies and the fine-needle picture of lymphocytes with germinal centers and Hürthle cells (p. 131).
6. **Myxedema coma** is the extreme end: altered mental status and hypothermia, usually triggered by infection, cold or sedating drugs in an older woman, mostly in winter. [StatPearls: Myxedema Coma](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK545193/) NBME Top Concepts' version: a patient who has not seen a doctor in years with confusion, dry, cool, yellowish skin, slow reflexes and **non-pitting** edema of the face and limbs, from glycosaminoglycans building up in the tissue (p. 131).

## Core mechanism: adrenal
1. **Primary** (Addison disease): the adrenal itself fails, so cortisol **and** aldosterone are low and ACTH is high. About 90% of cases in the US and Europe are autoimmune; tuberculosis is more common in developing countries. [MSD Manual: Addison Disease](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/adrenal-disorders/addison-disease), [StatPearls: Adrenal Insufficiency](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK441832/)
2. **Secondary** (low ACTH from the pituitary) and **tertiary** (low CRH from the hypothalamus): only cortisol is low. Aldosterone is run by the renin-angiotensin system, so it is preserved: no hyperkalemia, no dehydration, no dark skin. Stopping long-term steroid therapy is the most common cause of tertiary insufficiency. [StatPearls: Adrenal Insufficiency](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK441832/), [StatPearls: ACTH](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500031/)
3. **Your notes (El-Husseiny Endocrinology, pp. 47-48):** primary failure loses glucocorticoid, mineralocorticoid **and** adrenal androgen, so it can bring marked low blood pressure, high potassium and a **hyperchloremic (normal anion gap) metabolic acidosis**. Secondary failure loses only glucocorticoid and adrenal androgen. Tertiary failure follows long-term steroid use stopped suddenly. Your notes' mnemonic: **P**rimary **P**igments the skin, **S**econdary **S**pares the skin, **T**ertiary from **T**reatment.
4. Primary disease can be **acute**, from bleeding into both adrenals: **Waterhouse-Friderichsen syndrome**, usually with *Neisseria meningitidis* sepsis, DIC or endotoxic shock (fever, vomiting, neck stiffness, petechial rash and shock), or **chronic** (Addison disease). Autoimmune adrenalitis attacks all three cortical zones and goes with autoimmune polyglandular syndromes; tuberculosis is the leading cause in developing countries (pp. 47, 50).
5. Dark skin in primary disease comes from high ACTH plus beta-lipotropin, which has melanocyte-stimulating activity; it shows on pressure points, skin folds, scars and the mucosa of the mouth. [MSD Manual](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/adrenal-disorders/addison-disease)

## Compare and contrast: adrenal insufficiency
| Feature | Primary (adrenal) | Secondary / tertiary (pituitary / hypothalamus) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ACTH | High | Low |
| Aldosterone | Low | Normal |
| Potassium | High | Normal |
| Sodium | Low (salt loss + ADH) | Can be low, but dilutional (extra ADH from low cortisol) |
| Skin | Hyperpigmented | Normal |
| Volume | Dehydration, low BP | Usually not dehydrated |
| Typical cause | Autoimmune (90% in US/Europe), TB | Pituitary disease, brain injury; stopping chronic steroids (tertiary) |

[StatPearls: Adrenal Insufficiency](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK441832/), [MSD Manual: Addison Disease](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/adrenal-disorders/addison-disease), [MSD Manual: Secondary Adrenal Insufficiency](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/adrenal-disorders/secondary-adrenal-insufficiency)

## Myxedema coma at a glance
| Area | Finding | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brain | Confusion, coma | Global slowing of metabolism |
| Temperature | Hypothermia | Low heat production |
| Sodium | Low | Less water delivered to the distal nephron; ADH may contribute |
| Glucose | Low | Less glucose production (gluconeogenesis); look for coexisting adrenal insufficiency (standard teaching) |
| Breathing | High CO₂, low O₂, respiratory acidosis | Hypoventilation |
| Heart | Bradycardia, low-voltage ECG (look for pericardial effusion) | Slowed conduction; effusion |
| Muscle | High CK (can mimic a heart attack) | Hypothyroid myopathy |

[StatPearls: Myxedema Coma](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK545193/)

## High-yield facts
- Myxedema coma triggers: infection (most common), cold, sedatives, opioids, anesthetics, amiodarone, lithium, surgery, stopping thyroid medication. [StatPearls: Myxedema Coma](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK545193/)
- Give hydrocortisone **before** levothyroxine: thyroid hormone speeds cortisol breakdown and hypothyroidism can hide a coexisting adrenal insufficiency, so replacing thyroid first can tip the patient into adrenal crisis. [StatPearls: Myxedema Coma](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK545193/)
- Myxedema coma mortality is high, reported around 20-60%. [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK545193/)
- Primary adrenal insufficiency labs: anemia (often masked by dehydration, which raises the hematocrit; MSD lists hematocrit as elevated), low sodium, high potassium. Both primary and secondary disease can cause fasting hypoglycemia, because low cortisol reduces gluconeogenesis ([MSD Manual: Addison Disease](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/adrenal-disorders/addison-disease)); it is more marked when growth hormone is also low, as in combined pituitary failure (standard teaching). [StatPearls: Adrenal Insufficiency](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK441832/)
- Diagnosis, step 1: cosyntropin (synthetic ACTH) stimulation. A normal cortisol rise rules adrenal insufficiency out; a failure to rise **confirms adrenal insufficiency, not its level**. Long-standing secondary disease can also fail, because adrenals that have not been stimulated for a long time shrink (atrophy). [MSD Manual: Secondary Adrenal Insufficiency](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/adrenal-disorders/secondary-adrenal-insufficiency), [MSD Manual: Addison Disease](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/adrenal-disorders/addison-disease)
- Your notes' sequence is the same: low morning or random cortisol, or a poor cortisol peak on ACTH stimulation, means adrenal insufficiency; then a high ACTH means primary and a low ACTH means secondary or tertiary (El-Husseiny Endocrinology, p. 47).
- **Metyrapone test (your notes, pp. 48-49):** metyrapone blocks 11β-hydroxylase, so cortisol falls. A normal axis answers with a big rise in ACTH and 11-deoxycortisol. In primary failure ACTH rises but 11-deoxycortisol does not (the gland cannot respond). In secondary or tertiary failure neither rises (the pituitary cannot respond).
- **Adrenal crisis in your notes (pp. 49-51):** triggers are major stress (surgery, serious infection) in someone with unknown adrenal insufficiency, bleeding into both adrenals, and abrupt withdrawal of long-term steroids. It presents with profound low blood pressure, fever, confusion and coma. **Give hydrocortisone before any testing**: it has enough mineralocorticoid action to save life, and cortisol makes vessels respond to norepinephrine again (a permissive effect), so pressure comes up quickly. Taper steroids given at drug doses for more than about **three weeks**, and give a higher "stress dose" during illness or surgery.
- Diagnosis, step 2: the ACTH level gives the level. High ACTH = primary; low ACTH = secondary or tertiary. [MSD Manual: Secondary Adrenal Insufficiency](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/adrenal-disorders/secondary-adrenal-insufficiency), [MSD Manual: Addison Disease](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/adrenal-disorders/addison-disease)
- Anti-thyroid peroxidase (anti-TPO) antibodies support Hashimoto and predict progression from subclinical disease (progression point is standard teaching). [StatPearls: Hypothyroidism](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK519536/)

## Clinical vignette
A 78-year-old woman with untreated hypothyroidism is found confused in January after a week of cough. Temperature 34.0°C, heart rate 48, sodium 124 mEq/L, glucose 58 mg/dL, PaCO₂ 58 mmHg. ECG shows low voltage. What is the diagnosis, and which drug is given first?

**Answer:** myxedema coma; stress-dose hydrocortisone first, then IV levothyroxine.
**Why:** altered mental status plus hypothermia in a hypothyroid older woman, triggered by infection in winter. Steroids first because levothyroxine can unmask adrenal insufficiency. [StatPearls: Myxedema Coma](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK545193/)
**Trap:** blaming the confusion only on the low sodium; it is one part of global metabolic slowing.

## Common traps
- Central hypothyroidism: TSH can look normal; use free T4.
- A hypothyroid woman with galactorrhea may not have a prolactinoma: high TRH raises prolactin (El-Husseiny Endocrinology, p. 69).
- Hashimoto can start out **hyperthyroid** (El-Husseiny Endocrinology, p. 71).
- Depression can mimic hypothyroidism; constipation and slow reflexes point to the thyroid (NBME Top Concepts, p. 131).
- Primary adrenal failure gives a **normal anion gap** acidosis (loss of aldosterone), not a high gap one (El-Husseiny Endocrinology, p. 47).
- Hyperkalemia and hyperpigmentation point to the **adrenal** (primary). Neither occurs when the pituitary is the problem.
- A patient stopping long-term prednisone has tertiary (CRH-level) insufficiency, not Addison disease.
- High CK and low-voltage ECG in a hypothyroid patient do not automatically mean an MI.

## Practice questions
1. A woman has fatigue, salt craving, darkened palmar creases and gums, BP 92/60 mmHg, Na 129 and K 5.9 mEq/L. ACTH is high. Most likely cause in the US?
   - **Answer:** autoimmune adrenalitis (Addison disease).
   - **Why:** high ACTH with high potassium and pigmentation = primary; about 90% are autoimmune in the US and Europe. [MSD Manual](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/adrenal-disorders/addison-disease)
   - **Trap:** TB is the bigger cause in developing countries.
2. A 50-year-old man with a known pituitary macroadenoma has 2 months of fatigue and dizziness on standing. Morning cortisol and ACTH are both low. Which serum potassium finding is most likely?
   - **Answer:** normal potassium.
   - **Why:** aldosterone is controlled by renin-angiotensin, not ACTH, so it is preserved. [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK441832/), [StatPearls: ACTH](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500031/)
   - **Trap:** assuming all adrenal insufficiency causes hyperkalemia. [MSD Manual: Secondary Adrenal Insufficiency](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/adrenal-disorders/secondary-adrenal-insufficiency) also notes potassium stays relatively normal.
3. TSH 9.2 mIU/L with a normal free T4 and positive anti-TPO antibodies. Diagnosis?
   - **Answer:** subclinical hypothyroidism (Hashimoto).
   - **Why:** high TSH with normal free T4; anti-TPO shows autoimmunity and higher risk of progression (standard teaching). [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK519536/)
   - **Trap:** calling it central hypothyroidism, which has low or inappropriately normal TSH with low free T4.
4. Why does myxedema coma cause hyponatremia?
   - **Answer:** less water reaches the distal nephron for excretion, with possible extra ADH.
   - **Why:** low thyroid hormone reduces free-water excretion. [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK545193/)
   - **Trap:** treating it as primary sodium loss.
