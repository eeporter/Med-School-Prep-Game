# Meninges, CSF, lumbar puncture and meningitis

## In one line
CSF is made by the choroid plexus, flows from the lateral ventricles to the 3rd and 4th ventricles and out into the subarachnoid space, and is absorbed by arachnoid granulations. A lumbar puncture (LP) samples it below the end of the cord (L3-L5). In meningitis, **bacteria** give neutrophils, high protein and **low glucose**; **viruses** give lymphocytes and **normal glucose**.

## Core mechanism
1. **Meninges** (El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 144): **dura** (thick outer layer, from mesoderm), **arachnoid** (middle, web-like, from neural crest) and **pia** (thin, stuck to brain and cord, from neural crest).
2. **CSF flow** (El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 107): choroid plexus → lateral ventricles → foramina of **Monro** → 3rd ventricle → **cerebral aqueduct** (of Sylvius) → 4th ventricle → foramina of **Luschka** (lateral, 2) and **Magendie** (medial) → subarachnoid space → **arachnoid granulations** → dural venous sinuses → internal jugular vein.
3. **Why LP is done low** (El-Husseiny Neurology, pp. 36-37): in adults the cord ends at the L1-L2 junction, so the needle goes in at **L3-L4 or L4-L5**, where only the cauda equina's floating nerve roots lie. The line between the tops of the iliac crests marks **L4**. MSD agrees: "the L3-to-L4 or L4-to-L5 interspace" ([MSD Manual: Lumbar Puncture](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/neurologic-disorders/neurologic-tests-and-procedures/lumbar-puncture)).
4. **Layers the needle crosses** (El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 37): skin → fascia and fat → supraspinous ligament → interspinous ligament → **ligamentum flavum** → **epidural space** (epidural anesthesia stops here) → dura → arachnoid → **subarachnoid space** (CSF is collected here).

## Normal CSF and meningitis patterns
| CSF | Opening pressure | Cells | Protein | Glucose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Normal (MSD) | 100-200 mm H2O | 0-3 WBCs | 20-45 mg/dL | 50-100 mg/dL |
| Bacterial | Up | Up, **neutrophils** | Up | **Down** |
| Fungal or TB | Up | Up, **lymphocytes** | Up | **Down** |
| Viral | Normal or up | Up, **lymphocytes** | Normal or up | **Normal** |

Sources: normal row from [MSD Manual: Lumbar Puncture](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/neurologic-disorders/neurologic-tests-and-procedures/lumbar-puncture); meningitis rows from El-Husseiny Microbiology, p. 316. MSD adds for bacterial meningitis: protein typically 100-500 mg/dL, and "a CSF glucose level of ≤ 18 mg/dL or a CSF:blood glucose ratio of < 0.23 strongly suggests bacterial meningitis" ([MSD Manual: Acute Bacterial Meningitis](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/neurologic-disorders/meningitis/acute-bacterial-meningitis)).

**Other source (fungal and TB pressure):** your table gives opening pressure as up (El-Husseiny Microbiology, p. 316). MSD's CSF table gives "N or ↑" for subacute and chronic meningitis, the row that covers TB and *Cryptococcus* ([MSD table: Cerebrospinal Fluid Abnormalities in Various Disorders](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/multimedia/table/cerebrospinal-fluid-abnormalities-in-various-disorders)).

**Worked example.** CSF glucose 15 mg/dL with blood glucose 100 mg/dL: ratio = 15 / 100 = 0.15, below 0.23, with neutrophils, so this is bacterial meningitis.

## Who causes meningitis, by age
| Age or group | Most common organisms | Empiric treatment (MSD table) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under 3 months | **Group B strep**, then *E. coli* (K1 capsule) and *Listeria* (El-Husseiny Microbiology, p. 144) | Ampicillin + ceftriaxone or cefotaxime, + acyclovir |
| 3 months to 50 years | *S. pneumoniae* and *N. meningitidis* (Microbiology, pp. 60, 99, 144); *H. influenzae* type b was the top cause in children under 5 before the Hib vaccine (p. 108) | Ceftriaxone or cefotaxime + vancomycin, + acyclovir |
| Over 50, or weak cell-mediated immunity | Add ***Listeria*** (most common in kidney transplant patients and adults with cancer; Microbiology, p. 80) and gram-negatives | Ceftriaxone or cefotaxime + **ampicillin** + vancomycin, + acyclovir |
| Viral (aseptic) | **Enteroviruses**, up to 90% of cases (Microbiology, p. 265) | Supportive |

Treatment column: [MSD Manual: Acute Bacterial Meningitis](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/neurologic-disorders/meningitis/acute-bacterial-meningitis). Ampicillin is there to cover *Listeria*, which cephalosporins miss (standard teaching).
- *S. pneumoniae* is the most common bacterial cause in adults of all ages: lancet-shaped gram-positive diplococci on Gram stain (El-Husseiny Microbiology, p. 60).
- *N. meningitidis* spreads by droplets in close quarters (military recruits, college dorms) (Microbiology, p. 99).
- MSD: dexamethasone goes "immediately before or with the initial dose of antibiotics"; the evidence is best for pneumococcal meningitis.

**Recent change (pneumococcal vaccines):** your notes describe PPSV23 for adults over 65 and a 7-valent conjugate (PCV7) for children under 2 (El-Husseiny Microbiology, p. 59). CDC now says to "administer PCV15, PCV20, or PCV21 for all adults 50 years or older" (PPSV23 a year later only after PCV15), and gives children under 5 a 4-dose series of PCV15 or PCV20; PCV7 is no longer listed ([CDC: Pneumococcal Vaccine Recommendations, updated 25 Feb 2026](https://www.cdc.gov/pneumococcal/hcp/vaccine-recommendations/index.html)). The exam idea is unchanged: **conjugate** vaccines link the sugar to a protein to get T-cell help and memory, which plain polysaccharide vaccines cannot (Microbiology, p. 59).

## When not to do an LP first
- Your notes: LP is contraindicated with a mass (tumor, abscess), because suddenly lowering the pressure below can cause **brain herniation** (El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 111).
- MSD: "If papilledema or focal neurologic deficits are present, CT or MRI should be done before lumbar puncture." Other relative contraindications are infection at the puncture site and a bleeding tendency ([MSD Manual: Lumbar Puncture](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/neurologic-disorders/neurologic-tests-and-procedures/lumbar-puncture)).
- If imaging delays the LP, take blood cultures and give antibiotics and dexamethasone first ([MSD Manual: Acute Bacterial Meningitis](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/neurologic-disorders/meningitis/acute-bacterial-meningitis)).
- **Xanthochromia** (yellow CSF from broken-down red cells) points to subarachnoid hemorrhage (El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 166); its absence suggests a traumatic tap (MSD). See [22-intracranial-hemorrhage-and-herniation.md](22-intracranial-hemorrhage-and-herniation.md).
- Post-LP headache happens in about 10% (MSD).

## Hydrocephalus and pseudotumor cerebri (El-Husseiny Neurology, pp. 108-111)
| Type | Cause | Key feature |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Noncommunicating (obstructive) | Block inside the ventricles, usually at the foramen of Monro or the aqueduct (tumor, scarring, malformation) | Aqueductal stenosis: lateral and 3rd ventricles big, **4th normal** |
| Communicating | Arachnoid granulations cannot absorb CSF, after meningitis or subarachnoid/intraventricular hemorrhage; rarely too much CSF from a choroid plexus papilloma | All ventricles big, raised pressure |
| Normal pressure | A communicating type in older adults; the brain shrinks to make room | **Wet, wobbly, wacky**: urge incontinence, magnetic gait, dementia; may improve with LP or shunt. See [25-neurodegenerative-disease-and-guillain-barre.md](25-neurodegenerative-disease-and-guillain-barre.md) |
| Ex vacuo | Brain atrophy (Alzheimer, advanced HIV) | Big ventricles, normal pressure, not a true CSF problem |

- In babies, before the sutures close, hydrocephalus shows as a big head (p. 110). MSD: "Hydrocephalus is the most common cause of abnormally large heads in neonates" ([MSD Manual: Congenital Hydrocephalus](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/congenital-neurologic-anomalies/congenital-hydrocephalus)).
- **Pseudotumor cerebri (idiopathic intracranial hypertension)** (pp. 110-111): a young woman with obesity, headache, **papilledema** and diplopia from a CN 6 palsy, with normal imaging and high opening pressure. Risks: **female TOAD** (Tetracyclines, Obesity, vitamin A excess, Danazol). Treat with weight loss, then **acetazolamide** (less CSF made); repeated LP, shunt or optic nerve sheath fenestration if vision is threatened. Blindness is the main danger.
- MSD adds: opening pressure "> 250 mm H2O", pulsatile tinnitus and brief visual obscurations; topiramate is an alternative to acetazolamide, and venous sinus stenting is another definitive option ([MSD Manual: Idiopathic Intracranial Hypertension](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/neurologic-disorders/headache/idiopathic-intracranial-hypertension)).

**Read more on EyeWiki:** [Pseudotumor Cerebri (Idiopathic Intracranial Hypertension)](https://eyewiki.org/Pseudotumor_Cerebri_(Idiopathic_Intracranial_Hypertension)); [Papilledema](https://eyewiki.org/Papilledema) (links for extra reading only; facts above come from the cited sources)

## Clinical vignette
A 72-year-old man on prednisone for polymyalgia rheumatica has 1 day of fever, headache and a stiff neck. He has no papilledema or focal signs. CSF shows 1,800 WBCs/µL (90% neutrophils), protein 210 mg/dL and glucose 20 mg/dL (blood glucose 120 mg/dL). Which drug must be added to ceftriaxone and vancomycin, and why?

**Answer:** ampicillin, to cover *Listeria monocytogenes*.
**Why:** neutrophils, high protein and low glucose (ratio 20 / 120 = 0.17) mean bacterial meningitis. Over 50 and immunosuppressed, he is at risk of *Listeria*, and MSD's empiric regimen over age 50 adds ampicillin ([MSD Manual: Acute Bacterial Meningitis](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/neurologic-disorders/meningitis/acute-bacterial-meningitis); El-Husseiny Microbiology, p. 80).
**Trap:** stopping at ceftriaxone and vancomycin; cephalosporins do not cover *Listeria* (standard teaching).

## Common traps
- Epidural needle stops **before** the dura; the LP needle goes **through** dura and arachnoid into the subarachnoid space.
- Low CSF glucose = bacterial, fungal or TB; viral glucose is normal.
- Lymphocytes with low glucose = think **TB or fungus** (e.g. *Cryptococcus* in HIV), not virus.
- Papilledema or focal signs → image before LP.
- Aqueductal stenosis spares the 4th ventricle.
- Pseudotumor cerebri: normal scan, high pressure, papilledema, CN 6 palsy.

## Practice questions
1. Which space does the LP needle collect CSF from, and which space does an epidural catheter sit in?
   - **Answer:** subarachnoid space for LP; epidural space for epidural anesthesia.
   - **Why:** the needle passes ligamentum flavum, then epidural space, then dura and arachnoid to reach CSF (El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 37).
   - **Trap:** CSF is not in the subdural or epidural space.
2. A 2-week-old has fever and a bulging fontanelle. CSF shows gram-positive cocci in chains. Most likely organism?
   - **Answer:** group B streptococcus (*S. agalactiae*).
   - **Why:** it is the most common cause of neonatal meningitis, followed by *E. coli* and *Listeria* (El-Husseiny Microbiology, p. 144; [MSD Manual: Acute Bacterial Meningitis](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/neurologic-disorders/meningitis/acute-bacterial-meningitis)).
   - **Trap:** *Listeria* is a gram-positive **rod** (standard teaching).
3. A 19-year-old college student has fever, neck stiffness and a lymphocytic CSF with normal glucose in summer. Most likely cause?
   - **Answer:** an enterovirus (viral or aseptic meningitis).
   - **Why:** lymphocytes with normal glucose is the viral pattern (El-Husseiny Microbiology, p. 316), and enteroviruses cause up to 90% of aseptic meningitis (p. 265).
   - **Trap:** *N. meningitidis* would give neutrophils and low glucose.
4. A 28-year-old woman with obesity has daily headaches, papilledema and horizontal diplopia. MRI is normal; opening pressure is 320 mm H2O with normal CSF contents. First drug?
   - **Answer:** acetazolamide (with weight loss).
   - **Why:** pseudotumor cerebri; acetazolamide lowers CSF production (El-Husseiny Neurology, pp. 110-111). 320 is above MSD's 250 mm H2O cutoff ([MSD Manual: IIH](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/neurologic-disorders/headache/idiopathic-intracranial-hypertension)).
   - **Trap:** the CN 6 palsy is a false localizing sign of raised pressure, not a brainstem lesion.
