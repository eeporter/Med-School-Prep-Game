# Personality disorders

Exam focus: NBME comprehensive (CBSE) and USMLE Step 1 · Study material, not patient care guidance · Auditor: cleared 2026-10-28
Based on your notes: DSM-5-TR (American Psychiatric Association, 2022), the chapter on personality disorders (pp. 733-778). Page numbers are the book's printed page numbers. Treatment facts come from the MSD Manual. DSM criteria are paraphrased here, not copied; check exact wording in the book. Where another source disagrees, a marked note says so.

Abbreviations: **PD** = personality disorder; **OCPD** = obsessive-compulsive personality disorder; **OCD** = obsessive-compulsive disorder; **DBT** = dialectical behavior therapy; **SGA** = second-generation antipsychotic.

## In one line
A personality disorder is a **lifelong, rigid pattern** that started by adolescence or early adulthood and shows up **everywhere** in the person's life. Sort it by cluster: **A = odd or eccentric ("weird")**, **B = dramatic, emotional or erratic ("wild")**, **C = anxious or fearful ("worried")** (p. 734). The memory words are standard teaching.

## General rules (pp. 734-736)
- **Enduring pattern** that deviates from cultural expectations in **2 or more of 4 areas**: thinking (cognition), emotion (affectivity), relationships (interpersonal functioning), impulse control (p. 734).
- **Inflexible and pervasive**, causes distress or impairment, **stable and long-lasting**, with onset traceable to **adolescence or early adulthood**; not due to another mental disorder, a substance or a medical condition such as head injury (pp. 734-735).
- The traits are often **ego-syntonic**: the person does not see them as a problem, so other informants help (p. 735).
- **Under age 18:** a PD can be diagnosed if the features have been present for **at least 1 year**, **except antisocial PD, which cannot be diagnosed before 18** (p. 736).

| **C** | **Dependent** | **Excessive need to be taken care of**, submissive and clinging | **5 of 8** | Can't make everyday decisions without reassurance, needs others to take responsibility, can't disagree for fear of losing support, **urgently seeks a new relationship when one ends**, fears being left alone | p. 768 |
| **C** | **Obsessive-compulsive (OCPD)** | **Orderliness, perfectionism and control** at the cost of flexibility and efficiency | **4 of 8** | Lost in details and rules, **perfectionism that stops tasks being finished**, devoted to work over leisure, rigid about morals, can't discard worthless objects, won't delegate, **miserly**, rigid and stubborn | p. 771 |

**OCPD vs OCD:** OCPD is a pattern of perfectionism and control without true obsessions or compulsions; the traits are often ego-syntonic (p. 735). OCD has intrusive, unwanted obsessions and compulsions that cause distress (p. 265; guide 48).

**Personality change due to another medical condition (pp. 775-776):** a lasting change from the person's previous personality caused directly by a medical condition (for example a **frontal lobe** injury or tumor), not only during delirium.

## Who gets them (DSM-5-TR)
- **Median prevalence** from reviews of epidemiological studies: paranoid 3.2% (p. 739), schizoid 1.3% (p. 743), schizotypal 0.6% (p. 746), **antisocial 3.6%** (p. 750), **borderline 2.7%** (pp. 754-755), histrionic 0.9% (p. 759), narcissistic 1.6% (p. 762), avoidant 2.1% (p. 766), dependent 0.4% (p. 770), **OCPD 4.7%** (p. 774).
- **Antisocial:** **3 times as common in men** (p. 751); may become less evident or remit by about **age 40** (p. 750); above 70% in men with very severe alcohol problems, in substance treatment clinics, and in prisons or similar settings (p. 750).
- **Borderline:** more common in women in **clinical** samples but **equal in community samples**, possibly because women seek help more (p. 755). Many patients go years (1 to 8) without meeting criteria; impulsive behavior gets better soonest and mood symptoms more slowly (p. 755).
- **Schizotypal:** more common in first-degree relatives of people with **schizophrenia**; only a small proportion go on to develop schizophrenia (p. 746).

**Other source (borderline suicide rate):** DSM-5-TR reports that about **6%** of hospitalized patients with borderline PD died by suicide over 24 years (p. 756). MSD says suicide risk is "40 times that of the general population" and about **8-10%** die by suicide ([MSD: Borderline Personality Disorder, updated Jan 2026](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/psychiatric-disorders/personality-disorders/borderline-personality-disorder-bpd)). Remember: **high suicide risk, roughly 1 in 10 to 1 in 20 over the long term.**

## Treatment (MSD)
- **All PDs:** "The gold standard of treatment for personality disorders is psychotherapy." They are "not very responsive to medications"; drugs are for specific symptoms or coexisting conditions such as depression ([MSD: Overview of Personality Disorders, updated Jan 2026](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/psychiatric-disorders/personality-disorders/overview-of-personality-disorders)).
- **Borderline:** "The mainstay of treatment for borderline personality disorder is psychotherapy", especially **DBT** (a form of CBT with individual and group sessions), mentalization-based treatment and others. Drugs are used sparingly: mood stabilizers or SGAs for mood swings, anger and impulsivity; **avoid benzodiazepines and stimulants** (dependence, overdose, disinhibition) (MSD, borderline page).
- **Splitting:** MSD describes the borderline swing from idealization to devaluation as "black-and-white thinking (splitting, polarization of good and bad)".

## Clinical vignette
A 24-year-old woman is admitted after cutting her forearms when her boyfriend of 3 weeks said he needed space. She says he was "perfect" last week and is now "a monster". She has had many intense, short relationships, spends money impulsively, feels "empty inside", and has mood swings lasting a few hours. During stress she sometimes feels unreal and suspects people are plotting against her, which passes within a day. Frantic avoidance of abandonment, idealization and devaluation, impulsivity, self-harm, brief mood swings, emptiness and stress-related paranoia or dissociation give **5 or more of 9** criteria for **borderline PD** (p. 752).

## Common traps
- **Clusters: A odd (paranoid, schizoid, schizotypal); B dramatic (antisocial, borderline, histrionic, narcissistic); C anxious (avoidant, dependent, obsessive-compulsive)** (p. 734).
- **Schizoid = does not want relationships. Avoidant = wants relationships but fears rejection.**
- **Schizotypal = odd beliefs and appearance, but no fixed delusions or sustained psychosis.** Not schizophrenia.
- **Antisocial PD needs age 18 + conduct disorder before 15.** Under 18 → conduct disorder.
- **Any PD under 18 needs features for 1 year; antisocial never under 18.**
- **Borderline: abandonment fear, splitting, self-harm, emptiness.** Treat with DBT; avoid benzodiazepines.
- **Histrionic = seductive and theatrical; narcissistic = entitled and lacks empathy.**
- **Dependent = can't decide alone and quickly finds a new caretaker.**
- **OCPD (perfectionism, ego-syntonic) is not OCD (intrusive, unwanted obsessions).**
- **New personality change in an adult → look for a medical cause (frontal lobe lesion).**

## Practice questions
Answer, then check the explanation.

1. A 48-year-old man refuses to give his home address on a clinic form because "it could be used against me". He believes coworkers are secretly undermining him, still holds a grudge against a neighbor over a fence dispute from 10 years ago, and reacts with anger to harmless jokes. He has no hallucinations, and his beliefs are not fixed delusions. Which is the most likely diagnosis?
   - **Answer:** **paranoid personality disorder**.
   - **Why:** pervasive distrust with 4 or more of 7 features (reluctance to confide, unfounded suspicion, grudges, reading threats into harmless remarks), not only during a psychotic disorder (p. 737).
   - **Trap:** **delusional disorder, persecutory type**, which requires a fixed delusion (p. 104).

2. A 35-year-old night-shift computer technician has always lived alone and has no friends. He says he prefers it that way, has no interest in dating, and shows little emotion when his supervisor praises or criticizes him. He is not anxious about social contact and has no odd beliefs. Which is the most likely diagnosis?
   - **Answer:** **schizoid personality disorder**.
   - **Why:** detachment from relationships he neither wants nor enjoys, solitary activities, little interest in sex, indifference to praise or criticism, emotional coldness (p. 741).
   - **Trap:** **avoidant personality disorder**, in which the person wants relationships but avoids them for fear of rejection (p. 764).

3. A 29-year-old man wears a cape and mismatched clothes to work, believes he can sense other people's thoughts, and thinks songs on the radio are sometimes about him, though he can be talked out of it. He has no close friends and feels uneasy even with people he has known for years. He has never had hallucinations or fixed delusions. Which is the most likely diagnosis?
   - **Answer:** **schizotypal personality disorder**.
   - **Why:** ideas of reference that are not delusional, magical thinking, odd appearance, no close friends, and social anxiety that does not lessen with familiarity (p. 744).
   - **Trap:** **schizophrenia**, which needs delusions, hallucinations or disorganized speech for at least 1 month and 6 months of illness (pp. 113-114).

4. A 16-year-old boy has been arrested twice for car theft, regularly lies to steal money from classmates, has started fights since age 12, and shows no remorse. Which is the most appropriate diagnosis?
   - **Answer:** **conduct disorder**.
   - **Why:** antisocial PD cannot be diagnosed before age 18 (pp. 736, 748); this pattern before 18 is conduct disorder, which is required before 15 for a later antisocial PD diagnosis (p. 748).
   - **Trap:** **antisocial personality disorder**, which requires age 18 or older (p. 748).

5. A 26-year-old woman with borderline personality disorder has made three suicide attempts in a year and cuts herself when she feels abandoned. She is not currently depressed. Which is the most appropriate long-term treatment?
   - **Answer:** **dialectical behavior therapy**.
   - **Why:** psychotherapy is the mainstay of treatment for borderline PD, and DBT is a leading form (MSD).
   - **Trap:** **a long-term benzodiazepine**, which MSD advises against because of dependence, overdose and disinhibition.

6. A 31-year-old woman arrives for a routine visit in a low-cut evening dress, compliments the physician on his looks, calls him her "best friend" after one meeting, and describes her symptoms as "simply awful" but cannot give specific details. She becomes sulky when the physician turns his attention to his notes. Which is the most likely diagnosis?
   - **Answer:** **histrionic personality disorder**.
   - **Why:** discomfort when not the center of attention, seductive behavior, impressionistic speech lacking detail, theatrical emotion and believing relationships are closer than they are (p. 757).
   - **Trap:** **narcissistic personality disorder**, which centers on grandiosity, entitlement and lack of empathy (p. 760).

7. A 45-year-old surgeon insists on being seen only by the department chair because ordinary doctors "can't understand someone at my level". He expects to skip the waiting room, belittles the nurses, and shows no interest when told a colleague's child is ill. Which is the most likely diagnosis?
   - **Answer:** **narcissistic personality disorder**.
   - **Why:** feeling special and only understood by high-status people, entitlement, arrogance and lack of empathy (p. 760).
   - **Trap:** **antisocial personality disorder**, which centers on violating others' rights through lawbreaking, deceit and aggression (p. 748).

8. A 40-year-old woman cannot choose what to wear or what to cook without asking her husband, lets him handle all finances and decisions, and never disagrees with him for fear he will stop supporting her. When a previous partner left her, she moved in with a new boyfriend within 2 weeks. Which is the most likely diagnosis?
   - **Answer:** **dependent personality disorder**.
   - **Why:** needs others to make decisions and take responsibility, can't disagree for fear of losing support, and urgently seeks a new caretaker when a relationship ends (p. 768).
   - **Trap:** **avoidant personality disorder**, which centers on fear of rejection and inadequacy, not on needing to be cared for (p. 764).

9. A 38-year-old accountant has missed every project deadline this year because he redoes reports until they are "perfect". He refuses to delegate, works weekends instead of seeing friends, keeps broken appliances, and is very strict about rules. He sees nothing wrong with his habits and has no intrusive, unwanted thoughts or rituals. Which is the most likely diagnosis?
   - **Answer:** **obsessive-compulsive personality disorder**.
   - **Why:** perfectionism that stops task completion, devotion to work over leisure, inability to discard worthless objects, refusal to delegate and rigidity (p. 771); the traits are ego-syntonic (p. 735).
   - **Trap:** **obsessive-compulsive disorder**, which requires intrusive, unwanted obsessions and/or compulsions (p. 265).

10. A 52-year-old previously reserved bank manager has, over 8 months, become crude, makes sexual jokes to customers, spends money impulsively and seems unconcerned about losing his job. He has no history of psychiatric illness, and his attention is normal. Which is the most appropriate next step?
    - **Answer:** **brain imaging (MRI)** to look for a frontal lobe lesion.
    - **Why:** a new, lasting personality change in middle age points to **personality change due to another medical condition** (pp. 775-776). Personality disorders begin by adolescence or early adulthood (p. 735). Frontal lobe lesions cause disinhibition (standard teaching).
    - **Trap:** **diagnosing a personality disorder**, which cannot start in middle age (p. 735).
