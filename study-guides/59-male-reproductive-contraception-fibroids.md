## In one line
**Scrotum:**
- **Acute pain:** think **torsion first** (surgical emergency), then epididymitis.
- **Painless lump:** shine a light. **Transilluminates** means fluid (hydrocele, spermatocele). **Solid** means cancer until proven otherwise.

**Prostate:**
- **BPH** is a periurethral, DHT-driven overgrowth that blocks urine. Treat it with alpha-1 blockers and 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors.
- **Cancer** is a hard nodule that spreads to bone as **osteoblastic** lesions. Treat it by removing androgen: GnRH drugs, androgen receptor blockers, and the CYP17 inhibitor abiraterone.

**Contraception:**
- **Combined pills** stop ovulation, but estrogen is **off-limits** in smokers 35 and older, migraine with aura, past VTE and the first 3 weeks after delivery.
- The **copper IUD** is the most effective emergency contraceptive.

**Fibroids** are estrogen-responsive smooth muscle tumors that cause heavy bleeding.

## Male reproductive physiology (Osmosis HY Physiology pp. 577-580)
- **LH → Leydig cells → testosterone.**
- **FSH → Sertoli cells:**
  - Sertoli cells make **ABP**, which keeps testosterone high inside the tubules for spermatogenesis.
  - Sertoli cells also make **inhibin**, which feeds back to lower **FSH**.
- **Testosterone** feeds back to lower **GnRH and LH**.
- About 98% of testosterone is protein-bound; about 2% is free and active (p. 579).
- **Testosterone** masculinizes the internal tract in the fetus and helps the testes descend. At puberty it drives muscle, libido, voice and spermatogenesis (p. 579).
- **DHT** is made from testosterone by **5-alpha-reductase** in target tissues. It drives the external genitalia, **prostate growth**, male-pattern baldness and oily skin, which is why **finasteride** treats BPH and baldness (p. 580).
- **Semen** (p. 578):
  - the seminal vesicles add fructose;
  - the prostate adds alkaline fluid that neutralizes vaginal acid;
  - the bladder sphincter closes during ejaculation.

## Scrotal problems
**Spermatic cord contents** (NBME TC p. 155):
- **3 arteries:** testicular, artery to the ductus deferens, cremasteric.
- **3 nerves:** genital branch of the genitofemoral, cremasteric, autonomics (p. 155). The genital branch supplies the cremaster muscle, which is why it carries the cremasteric reflex (standard teaching).
- **3 other structures:** ductus deferens, **pampiniform plexus**, lymphatics.

| Problem | Key clues | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Testicular torsion** | Peaks at **ages 12-18**. A "bell clapper" fixation defect lets the testis twist on its cord. **Sudden severe pain, nausea and vomiting**. The testis is tender and may sit **high and horizontal**. The **cremasteric reflex is usually absent**. Color **Doppler shows reduced flow**, but imaging is only for unclear cases | NBME TC p. 155; MSD |
| **Epididymitis** | Under 35: mostly **gonorrhea or chlamydia**. Over 35: mostly **gram-negative coliforms**, often with catheters or urologic procedures. Pain may radiate to the abdomen, with fever or urinary symptoms. If the findings are not clearly limited to the epididymis, **get an immediate Doppler to rule out torsion**, especially under age 30 | MSD |
| **Mumps orchitis** | Testicular pain plus parotid swelling and a high amylase. The cause is mumps (a paramyxovirus), which the live attenuated vaccine prevents. See the Other source note below on timing | NBME TC p. 173; MSD |
| **Varicocele** | Dilated **pampiniform plexus**, a "**bag of worms**", usually on the **left**. The left testicular vein enters the left renal vein at a 90° angle, so blood backs up. It is bigger standing and with Valsalva and **shrinks lying down** | Osmosis Vol 2 p. 183; MSD |
| **Hydrocele** | Cystic swelling that **transilluminates**; the communicating type is usually congenital. An indirect hernia (failed closure of the processus vaginalis) can form a hydrocele | MSD; Osmosis Vol 2 p. 272 |
| **Spermatocele** | Cyst at the **upper pole** of the testis next to the epididymis; **transilluminates** | MSD |
| **Testicular cancer** | Mass attached to or part of the testis. It is **solid and does not transilluminate**, and is usually painless or a dull ache | MSD |

**Other source (when mumps orchitis appears):** your NBME vignette has testicular pain about 3 weeks before the parotid swelling (p. 173). MSD says unilateral mumps orchitis usually starts **4-7 days after** the parotid swelling. It can also occur **without parotitis**. It affects 20-25% of males with mumps, mostly after puberty, and fertility falls in about a quarter of men after one-sided orchitis. For exams: the usual order is **parotitis first, then orchitis**, but a missing or earlier parotitis does not rule mumps out.

**Torsion is a race against the clock (MSD):**
- Testicular salvage is **80-100% within 6-8 hours** and **near zero at 12 hours**.
- **Manual detorsion** rotates the testis outward ("open the book"). If that fails, operate urgently.
- **Fix both testes**, because the other side shares the defect.

**When a varicocele means something worse:**
- **Left renal vein blocked → new left varicocele.**
  - **Renal cell carcinoma** growing into the left renal vein (Osmosis Vol 2 p. 847; El-Husseiny Nephrology p. 136).
  - **Left renal vein thrombosis** in nephrotic syndrome (El-Husseiny Nephrology p. 88). Your NBME notes link recurrent varicoceles and venous clots in nephrotic syndrome to **loss of antithrombin III** (NBME TC p. 302).
- **Right-sided varicocele, or one that does not shrink lying down,** suggests **inferior vena cava obstruction** (MSD).
- The right testicular vein drains straight into the inferior vena cava, which is why isolated right varicoceles are unusual (standard teaching).

## Undescended testis (cryptorchidism)
- **What it is:** failure of the testis to migrate from the abdomen to the scrotum (El-Husseiny Endocrinology p. 9).
- **How common (MSD):** about **3% of term** and **30% of preterm** infants. About half descend on their own in the first month.
- **Risks (MSD):**
  - **subfertility**;
  - **testicular cancer, mainly in the undescended testis**;
  - torsion of an intra-abdominal testis;
  - inguinal hernia.
- **Treatment:** **orchiopexy, ideally at 6-12 months** (corrected age). Cancer risk falls if surgery is done **before puberty, ideally before age 2** (MSD Cryptorchidism). MSD's testicular cancer page adds that the excess cancer risk is **decreased or eliminated if orchiopexy is done before age 10**.

## Testicular tumors (MSD; NCI)
- **Who:** the most common solid cancer in males **aged 15-35**. **Cryptorchidism** raises the risk **2.5 to 20 times** (MSD).
- **Types:**
  - **Germ cell tumors** are most of them (MSD puts the non-germ-cell types under 5%): **seminoma** about 40%, **nonseminoma** about 60%. Nonseminomas include embryonal carcinoma, yolk sac tumor, choriocarcinoma and teratoma.
  - **Non-germ-cell tumors** (Leydig or Sertoli) are rare, under 5% (MSD). A Leydig cell tumor can make hormones and cause **gynecomastia** (standard teaching).
- **Seminoma** is the male counterpart of **dysgerminoma** (NBME TC p. 357).
- **Mixed tumors:** a tumor counts as a seminoma only if it is **100% seminoma**. Mixed tumors are treated as nonseminomas (NCI).

**Tumor markers**

| Marker | What raises it | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **AFP** | Nonseminomas with **yolk sac**, embryonal or teratoma elements. **Seminomas do not make AFP**, so a "pure seminoma" with high AFP is treated as a mixed (nonseminoma) tumor | NCI; Johns Hopkins |
| **hCG** | Made by tumor syncytiotrophoblast cells, which is why **choriocarcinoma** (syncytio- and cytotrophoblast, no villi) is the classic hCG tumor. **About 14-15% of seminomas** also make some hCG | Johns Hopkins; NCI; NBME TC p. 357; Bouquet de Jolinière 2014 (choriocarcinoma makes hCG) |
| **LDH** | Both types; tracks tumor bulk. In metastatic nonseminoma it predicts survival | NCI; Johns Hopkins |

**Diagnosis and treatment:**
1. **Scrotal ultrasound** confirms the mass is in the testis. Then check AFP, beta-hCG and LDH (MSD).
2. Remove the testis by **radical inguinal orchiectomy**: the cord is clamped high through a groin incision before the testis is handled (MSD; NCI).
3. **Never biopsy through the scrotum.** Transscrotal surgery raises local recurrence (2.9% vs 0.4%) by seeding the scrotum and inguinal nodes (NCI).
4. **Staging:** the testis drains to the **retroperitoneal (para-aortic) nodes**, not the inguinal nodes (standard teaching). So staging CT looks at the retroperitoneal nodes (NCI), and seminoma radiation, when used, is aimed at the para-aortic region (MSD). For stage I disease, MSD says **single-dose carboplatin has largely replaced radiation**, because radiation carries long-term heart risks and second cancers.
5. **Chemotherapy is platinum-based**, for example bleomycin, etoposide and cisplatin (MSD).
6. **Cure:** 5-year survival is **over 95%** when the cancer is limited to the testis (MSD).

## Benign prostatic hyperplasia (MSD; your notes)
- **What it is:** a non-cancerous overgrowth of the **periurethral** prostate glands, which pushes the rest of the gland outward (MSD). Exam books call this area the transition zone (standard teaching).
- **Why:** prostate growth depends on **DHT** (Osmosis HY Physiology p. 580).
- **Symptoms (MSD):** frequency, **nocturia**, hesitancy, **weak stream**, incomplete emptying and terminal dribbling.
- **DRE (MSD):** enlarged, **nontender, rubbery**, often with the middle groove lost. **Hard areas suggest cancer.** PSA can be raised in BPH, so a raised PSA still needs a cancer workup.
- **Complications:**
  - urinary retention, UTI and stones (MSD);
  - **postrenal azotemia** from blocked outflow (Osmosis Vol 2 p. 764);
  - **hydronephrosis** (p. 771);
  - overflow incontinence (p. 859).
- **Drugs that tip a man with BPH into retention (MSD):**
  - **anticholinergics** (for example, sedating antihistamines);
  - **sympathomimetics** such as OTC decongestants;
  - opioids, anesthesia and alcohol.

**BPH treatment**

| Drug | How it works | Key points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Alpha-1 blockers**: prazosin, terazosin, doxazosin, tamsulosin, silodosin, alfuzosin | Block **alpha-1A receptors in the bladder neck and prostate**, relaxing smooth muscle | Fast relief of symptoms. Blocking **alpha-1B on blood vessels** causes **postural hypotension** (Pharm HY PDF 21) |
| **5-alpha-reductase inhibitors**: finasteride, dutasteride | Lower **DHT** | **Shrink the prostate** over **months**, best in larger glands (over 30 mL) (MSD; Pharm HY PDF 21; Osmosis HY Physiology p. 580) |
| **Combination** | Both of the above | Better than either alone (MSD) |
| **Tadalafil** | PDE5 inhibitor | Helps when ED is also present (MSD) |
| **TURP** (surgery) | Removes the obstructing tissue | For men who fail drugs or have complications; about 10% need it again within 10 years (MSD) |

## Prostate cancer (MSD; your Pharm HY notes)
- **Histology:** almost always **adenocarcinoma**. It usually arises in the **peripheral zone**, which a DRE can reach (standard teaching).
- **Risk factors:** age (median age at diagnosis is 72), **Black men** and **inherited genes**:
  - BRCA1/2 mutations;
  - Lynch syndrome;
  - a family history of hereditary breast and ovarian cancer.
- **DRE:** **rock-hard induration or nodules**, though the exam is often normal.
- **PSA:** a level of **4 ng/mL or more** usually leads to biopsy. Infection, BPH and handling the prostate can also raise PSA.
- **Grading:** the Gleason score, reported as WHO grade groups 1-5.
- **Spread:** to bone, as **osteoblastic** (bone-forming) lesions in the **pelvis, ribs and vertebrae**.
- **Screening:** for men aged **55-69**, the USPSTF (US Preventive Services Task Force) says PSA screening is an **individual decision** after discussing benefits and harms.

**Androgen deprivation therapy**
- **GnRH agonists ("-relin"):** leuprolide, goserelin, triptorelin and others.
  - **Pulsatile** dosing stimulates the pituitary and is used for **infertility**.
  - **Continuous** dosing shuts the pituitary down and is used for prostate cancer, endometriosis and precocious puberty (Pharm HY PDF 108-109).
  - They cause an **initial flare** (Pharm HY PDF 108), seen as a **transient PSA rise** (MSD).
- **GnRH antagonists ("-relix"):** degarelix and others. **No initial flare** (Pharm HY PDF 108).
- **Side effects of both** (Pharm HY PDF 108): hot flushes, **loss of libido, impotence**, muscle loss and **osteoporosis**.
- **Androgen receptor blockers:** flutamide, bicalutamide, nilutamide and enzalutamide. They block androgens (testosterone and DHT) at the receptor (Pharm HY PDF 109; MSD).
- **Abiraterone (with prednisone):** blocks androgen **synthesis** in the tumor, testes and adrenal glands (MSD).
- **Aromatase inhibitors** are not prostate cancer drugs: see the letrozole Correction in guide 58.

**Correction (what abiraterone is):** your Pharm HY notes (PDF p. 109) list abiraterone with flutamide and the other drugs as a "DHT receptor blocker." Abiraterone does **not** block the receptor.
- **What it does:** it is an **androgen synthesis inhibitor** that blocks the enzyme **CYP17** (17α-hydroxylase/C17,20-lyase) (Zytiga prescribing information, section 12.1; UK product information).
- **Why the side effects:** CYP17 is also needed for adrenal steroids, so blocking it **raises adrenal mineralocorticoid output**. That causes **hypertension, hypokalemia and fluid retention**.
- **Why prednisone is given with it:** the corticosteroid **suppresses ACTH drive**, which lowers the mineralocorticoid excess.
- **Liver:** check liver enzymes, because serious liver injury can occur (UK product information).
- **For exams:** flutamide, bicalutamide, nilutamide and enzalutamide block the **receptor**. Abiraterone blocks **synthesis** (CYP17).

## Erectile dysfunction drugs (NBME TC p. 156; your El-Husseiny and Pharm HY notes)
- **How erection works:** NO makes cGMP, which relaxes smooth muscle. **PDE5** breaks cGMP down.
- **Sildenafil** inhibits PDE5, so **cGMP builds up** and NO's vasodilation lasts longer (NBME TC p. 156; El-Husseiny Respiratory p. 131). Sildenafil also treats **pulmonary hypertension** (El-Husseiny Cardiovascular p. 201; Respiratory p. 131; Pharm HY PDF 162).
- **Never with nitrates.** Both raise cGMP, so together they cause **profound systemic hypotension**. The combination is **absolutely contraindicated** (El-Husseiny Cardiovascular p. 201). Your Pharm HY notes call the result "severe hypotensive shock" (PDF 71; also PDF 72).
- **Alprostadil** (a prostaglandin E1 analog) is another ED treatment (Pharm HY PDF 165).
- **Other causes of ED in your notes:** GnRH drugs (Pharm HY PDF 108). See the endocrine guides for prolactinoma and diabetes.

## Contraception (MSD; CDC; your notes)
**Combined pills (estrogen + progestin)** (MSD)
- **How they work:**
  - suppress hypothalamic **GnRH**, so the pituitary signal for ovulation is blocked;
  - **thicken cervical mucus**;
  - make the endometrium less receptive.
- **Do not use (unacceptable risk):**
  - **smoking at age 35 or older**;
  - **migraine with aura**;
  - **past or current VTE**, or a clotting disorder;
  - **current breast cancer**;
  - severe hypertension (160/100 mm Hg or more) or hypertension with vascular disease;
  - **under 21 days after delivery**;
  - decompensated cirrhosis, **hepatocellular adenoma** or liver cancer;
  - acute viral hepatitis.
- **Benefits:**
  - **ovarian cancer** risk down about 50% after 5 years;
  - **endometrial cancer** risk down about 60% after 10 years;
  - fewer ovarian cysts, less painful periods and less iron deficiency.
- **Drug interactions:**
  - **CYP450 inducers** cause pill failure: **rifampin**, rifabutin, phenytoin, carbamazepine, St. John's wort and some HIV drugs (MSD; Pharm HY PDF 38: add a barrier method).
  - A LNG IUD is not affected.
  - Pills also speed **lamotrigine** clearance.

**Progestin-only pills** (MSD)
- Work mainly by **thickening cervical mucus**.
- **Take at the same time every day.** If more than 27 hours pass between doses, use backup for 7 days.
- Safe **right after delivery**.
- Not for women with current breast cancer.

**After delivery**
- **Your notes:** do not use combined methods while **breastfeeding**, because estrogen reduces milk. In women who are not breastfeeding, start them **after 3 weeks**, once the clotting risk of pregnancy has eased (El-Husseiny Obstetrics p. 131).
- **Your notes:** breastfeeding causes temporary anovulation, so contraception "may be deferred for 3 months", with a definite method used after that (El-Husseiny Obstetrics p. 131).

**Other source (relying on breastfeeding):** MSD says women who are **exclusively** breastfeeding may rely on lactational amenorrhea for up to **6 months** after delivery, and those leaving that method earlier should start a contraceptive. Healthdirect Australia (Aug 2026) lists all three conditions for it to work (up to 98%): **exclusive breastfeeding, baby under 6 months, and no return of periods**. For exams: breastfeeding protects only while **all three** hold. Partial breastfeeding or a returned period means she needs a method now, whatever the month.

**Other source (combined methods while breastfeeding):** the CDC's 2024 eligibility criteria (US MEC, Appendix D) rate combined methods in breastfeeding women as follows:
- **under 21 days:** category 4 (do not use);
- **21 to under 30 days:** category 3;
- **30-42 days:** category 2 (benefits usually outweigh risks) if there are no other VTE risk factors;
- **after 42 days:** category 2.

The CDC says studies on breastfeeding effects conflict and finds no consistent harm to infant growth or health. For exams:
- **The first 3 weeks after delivery are off-limits for everyone.**
- If a breastfeeding mother needs contraception early, **progestin-only methods or an IUD** are the safe answer.

**IUDs** (MSD table)
- **Copper IUD (lasts 10 years):**
  - Works through **copper ions**, which cause a sterile inflammatory reaction that damages sperm.
  - Contains no hormones.
  - Causes **heavier periods and worse cramps**.
- **LNG IUDs (last 3-8 years):**
  - Work by **thickening cervical mucus**.
  - Bleeding becomes **lighter** and irregular, and some women stop having periods.
- **Infection:** *Actinomyces israelii* can cause PID (pelvic inflammatory disease) in IUD users (El-Husseiny Microbiology p. 86).

**Emergency contraception** (MSD; Pharm HY PDF 107)

| Method | Time limit | Pregnancy rate | Points |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Copper IUD** | Within **5 days** | About **0.1%** (best) | Can stay in as ongoing contraception. **Preferred if BMI is over 30** |
| **LNG 52-mg IUD** | 5 days in MSD's text (its Key Points say 7) | About 0.3% | As good as copper (MSD) |
| **Ulipristal 30 mg** (an SPRM) | Within **120 hours** (5 days) | About 1.5% | Prescription only. **Wait 6 days** before starting hormonal birth control, then use backup for 7 days |
| **Oral LNG 1.5 mg** | MSD: within 120 hours | 2-3% | Sold without a prescription. Less effective than ulipristal if BMI is over 30 |

- **How they work:** the pills mainly **delay or prevent ovulation** (MSD).
- **Medical abortion** is a different thing: **mifepristone** (a progesterone receptor antagonist) followed by **misoprostol** (a prostaglandin E1 analog) (El-Husseiny Obstetrics p. 32; Pharm HY PDF 165).

## Uterine fibroids (leiomyomas) (MSD; Pharm HY PDF 107)
- **What they are:** **benign smooth muscle tumors** of the uterus and the most common pelvic tumor.
- **How common:** by age 50, about 70% of White and **80% of Black** women in the US have fibroids.
- **Risk factors:** early menarche, obesity and hypertension raise risk. Three or more births lower it.
- **Hormones:** they respond to **estrogen**. They grow in the reproductive years and usually **shrink after menopause**.
- **Location matters:**
  - **submucosal** fibroids are most linked to **infertility**;
  - submucosal, intramural or subserosal fibroids can all cause abnormal bleeding.
- **Symptoms:**
  - **heavy or between-period bleeding**, which can cause **iron deficiency anemia**;
  - pelvic pressure, urinary or bowel symptoms;
  - pregnancy problems and postpartum hemorrhage.
- **Degeneration:** several types; **red degeneration happens mainly in pregnancy**.
- **Cancer risk:** sarcomatous change occurs in **under 1%**. Use MRI if leiomyosarcoma is a concern, and avoid morcellation if cancer is suspected.
- **Diagnosis:** **transvaginal ultrasound** first. Saline sonography, hysteroscopy or MRI are used for submucosal fibroids or before myomectomy.
- **Treatment:**
  - **Less bleeding, no shrinkage:** tranexamic acid, NSAIDs, combined pills, progestins and the **LNG IUD**.
  - **Shrinkage:** **GnRH agonists or antagonists** (oral elagolix or relugolix), usually short-term because fibroids **regrow**. Low-dose add-back hormone limits menopause-like effects.
  - **Uterus-sparing procedures:** **uterine artery embolization**, MRI-guided focused ultrasound and radiofrequency ablation.
  - **Surgery:** **myomectomy** keeps fertility (about 25% need a hysterectomy 4-8 years later). **Hysterectomy** is the definitive treatment.
- **Your notes:** SPRMs (asoprisnil, telapristone) are listed for fibroids and endometriosis (Pharm HY PDF 107).

**Recent change (SPRMs for fibroids):** on **13 November 2020** the EMA restricted **5-mg ulipristal** (Esmya and generics), an SPRM sold for fibroids, after cases of **serious liver injury**, some needing liver transplants.
- It may now be used only in **premenopausal** women for whom **surgery is unsuitable or has failed**.
- The **single-dose 30-mg ulipristal** for emergency contraception is **not affected**.
- MSD's current fibroid treatment list does not include ulipristal.
- **For exams:** the drugs that shrink fibroids are **GnRH agonists or antagonists**. Ulipristal is best known as an **emergency contraceptive**.

## Clinical vignette
A 67-year-old man has had 2 years of getting up 3 times a night to urinate, a weak stream and dribbling. He takes no medicines. DRE shows a symmetrically enlarged, smooth, rubbery, nontender prostate. Urinalysis is normal, creatinine is normal, and PSA is mildly raised.

- **Diagnosis:** **BPH.** He has outlet-obstruction symptoms and a rubbery, nontender gland (MSD).
- **Why not cancer on exam:** cancer feels rock-hard or nodular (MSD). But PSA can rise in BPH, so a raised PSA still needs evaluation for cancer (MSD).
- **Mechanism:** **DHT-driven** periurethral overgrowth narrows the urethra (MSD; Osmosis HY Physiology p. 580).
- **Treatment:**
  - an **alpha-1 blocker** for quick symptom relief;
  - add a **5-alpha-reductase inhibitor** to shrink a large gland over months;
  - the combination works better than either alone (MSD).
- **Warn him:**
  - **standing up slowly** on an alpha blocker (postural hypotension, Pharm HY PDF 21);
  - **avoid OTC decongestants and sedating antihistamines**, which can cause acute retention (MSD).
- **Untreated risks:** retention, infection, stones, **hydronephrosis and postrenal kidney injury** (MSD; Osmosis Vol 2 pp. 764, 771).

## Common traps
- **Acute scrotal pain in a teenager is torsion until proven otherwise.**
  - The cremasteric reflex is usually absent.
  - Don't let imaging delay surgery when the diagnosis is clear.
  - Salvage is near zero at 12 hours (MSD).
- **Epididymitis by age (MSD):**
  - under 35: gonorrhea or chlamydia;
  - over 35: coliforms, often with a catheter or urologic procedure.
- **Transilluminates = fluid** (hydrocele, spermatocele). **Solid = cancer workup** (MSD).
- **Left varicocele:**
  - The left testicular vein drains into the **left renal vein**.
  - A new left varicocele in an adult can mean renal cell carcinoma (Osmosis Vol 2 p. 847).
  - A right-sided varicocele, or one that stays when lying down, suggests IVC obstruction (MSD).
- **Testicular mass: inguinal orchiectomy, never a scrotal biopsy** (NCI).
- **Seminomas never make AFP.** A high AFP means a nonseminoma component (NCI; Johns Hopkins).
- **Undescended testis:**
  - orchiopexy at 6-12 months;
  - the cancer risk is mainly in the undescended testis;
  - orchiopexy before age 10 decreases or eliminates the excess cancer risk (MSD).
- **Alpha-1A (prostate and bladder) relieves symptoms. Alpha-1B (vessels) drops blood pressure** (Pharm HY PDF 21).
- **5-alpha-reductase inhibitors shrink the prostate; alpha blockers do not** (MSD; Pharm HY PDF 21).
- **Prostate cancer bone metastases are osteoblastic** (MSD).
- **GnRH drugs:**
  - **"-relin" agonists cause an initial flare;** "-relix" antagonists don't (Pharm HY PDF 108).
  - **Pulsatile** GnRH is for infertility, **continuous** for suppression (PDF 108-109).
- **Abiraterone = CYP17 synthesis blocker, not a receptor blocker.** Give it with prednisone because of mineralocorticoid excess (Zytiga product information).
- **Sildenafil + nitrate = profound hypotension** (El-Husseiny Cardiovascular p. 201).
- **No estrogen:**
  - smokers aged 35 or older;
  - migraine with aura;
  - VTE;
  - under 21 days postpartum (MSD; CDC).
- **Rifampin makes combined pills fail** (Pharm HY PDF 38).
- **Most effective emergency contraception = copper IUD.** It is also preferred when BMI is over 30 (MSD).
- **Fibroids:**
  - tranexamic acid, NSAIDs and pills reduce bleeding but don't shrink them;
  - GnRH drugs shrink them;
  - myomectomy keeps fertility (MSD).

Answer, then check the explanation.

1. A 14-year-old boy wakes at 5 a.m. with sudden, severe right scrotal pain and vomits twice. He is not sexually active. The right testis is exquisitely tender and lies higher than the left, with a horizontal lie. Stroking the inner thigh does not raise the testis. The pain began 2 hours ago. Which is the most appropriate next step?
   - **Answer:** **urgent surgical exploration** (manual detorsion can be tried while surgery is arranged).
   - **Why:** this is classic torsion: the right age, sudden pain, vomiting, a high horizontal testis and an absent cremasteric reflex. Salvage is 80-100% within 6-8 hours and near zero at 12 hours. Imaging is for unclear cases only (MSD; NBME TC p. 155).
   - **Trap:** **urinalysis and oral antibiotics for epididymitis**, which wastes the salvage window.

2. A 24-year-old man has 3 days of increasing pain at the back of his left testis, burning with urination and a clear urethral discharge. He has had two new sexual partners this month. Exam shows a tender, swollen epididymis; the testis itself is normal in position. Which organisms are the most likely cause?
   - **Answer:** ***Chlamydia trachomatis* or *Neisseria gonorrhoeae*.**
   - **Why:** in men under 35, most epididymitis comes from a sexually transmitted pathogen. Doxycycline is preferred when these are suspected, and a single dose of ceftriaxone is added when gonorrhea is suspected (MSD).
   - **Trap:** **gram-negative coliforms such as *E. coli***, the usual cause in men over 35 with catheters or recent urologic procedures.

3. A 61-year-old man who smokes notices a new swelling in the left side of his scrotum. It feels like a bag of worms and does not get smaller when he lies down. He has had 2 episodes of painless blood in his urine, and a left flank mass is palpable. Into which vessel does the vein that is backing up normally drain?
   - **Answer:** **the left renal vein.**
   - **Why:** the left testicular vein empties into the left renal vein. A renal cell carcinoma growing into that vein blocks drainage, causing a new left varicocele that doesn't shrink lying down (Osmosis Vol 2 pp. 183, 847; El-Husseiny Nephrology p. 136; MSD).
   - **Trap:** **the inferior vena cava**, where the **right** testicular vein drains (standard teaching).

4. A 3-month-old boy has a soft, painless swelling of the right scrotum that is smaller in the morning and larger after crying. It glows when a penlight is held against it, and the testis is felt separately. Failure of which developmental process explains this?
   - **Answer:** **closure (obliteration) of the processus vaginalis.**
   - **Why:** a communicating hydrocele lets peritoneal fluid flow into the scrotum. It transilluminates and is usually congenital (MSD). Failed closure of the processus vaginalis also causes indirect inguinal hernias, which can form hydroceles (Osmosis Vol 2 p. 272).
   - **Trap:** **descent of the testis from the abdomen** (cryptorchidism). Here the testis is in the scrotum.

5. A 29-year-old man finds a hard, painless lump in his left testis. Ultrasound shows a solid intratesticular mass. Blood is drawn for tumor markers. Which is the most appropriate way to obtain tissue?
   - **Answer:** **radical inguinal orchiectomy**, with the spermatic cord clamped before the testis is handled.
   - **Why:** this is both the diagnosis and the first treatment (MSD; NCI). The testis drains to the retroperitoneal nodes (standard teaching).
   - **Trap:** **transscrotal needle biopsy**. NCI calls it inappropriate because it can seed the scrotum and inguinal nodes, raising local recurrence.

6. A 31-year-old man has an orchiectomy for a testicular mass. The pathologist's sections show only seminoma. Before surgery his AFP was clearly elevated, and his liver tests are normal. How should the tumor be managed?
   - **Answer:** **as a nonseminoma (mixed germ cell tumor).**
   - **Why:** seminomas do not make AFP. A raised AFP with "pure seminoma" on the slides means an unsampled nonseminoma part, so NCI advises treating it as a mixed tumor, unless something like liver disease explains the AFP (NCI; Johns Hopkins).
   - **Trap:** **"seminoma that secretes AFP"**. By definition, seminoma does not secrete AFP.

7. A 9-month-old boy, born at term, has had an empty left scrotum since birth. The left testis can be felt in the inguinal canal but cannot be brought into the scrotum. The right testis is normal. What is the best management?
   - **Answer:** **orchiopexy now.**
   - **Why:** spontaneous descent after the first months is rare. Surgery is ideal at 6-12 months (corrected age). The cancer risk sits mainly in the undescended testis and is decreased or eliminated if orchiopexy is done before age 10. Undescended testes also cause subfertility (MSD).
   - **Trap:** **wait until puberty**. Cancer risk is lower when surgery happens before puberty, ideally before age 2.

8. A 70-year-old man with BPH starts terazosin. On the second morning he feels light-headed and nearly faints when he gets out of bed. Blockade of receptors in which location best explains this effect?
   - **Answer:** **alpha-1B receptors on blood vessels**, causing vasodilation and **postural hypotension**.
   - **Why:** the urinary benefit comes from alpha-1A receptors in the bladder neck and prostate. The fall in blood pressure comes from alpha-1B receptors on vessels (Pharm HY PDF 21).
   - **Trap:** **alpha-1A receptors in the prostate**. Blocking those relieves symptoms; they are not the cause of the fainting.

9. A 72-year-old man with prostate cancer and new osteoblastic lesions in his spine starts monthly leuprolide injections. Over the first 10 days his back pain worsens and his PSA rises. Which drug would have avoided this initial effect?
   - **Answer:** **degarelix**, a GnRH antagonist ("-relix").
   - **Why:** GnRH agonists ("-relin") first stimulate the pituitary, causing a testosterone flare, before continuous dosing shuts it down. Antagonists block the receptor at once, with no flare (Pharm HY PDF 108; MSD: transient PSA rise with agonists).
   - **Trap:** **goserelin**, another agonist with the same flare.

10. A 68-year-old man with metastatic prostate cancer is started on a drug that lowers testosterone made by the testes, adrenal glands and tumor. It must be taken with prednisone. Without enough prednisone he develops hypertension, a potassium of 3.0 mEq/L and ankle edema. What is the drug's mechanism?
    - **Answer:** **inhibition of CYP17 (17α-hydroxylase/C17,20-lyase)**. The drug is **abiraterone**.
    - **Why:** blocking CYP17 stops androgen synthesis but also raises adrenal mineralocorticoid output. That causes hypertension, hypokalemia and fluid retention. Prednisone suppresses the ACTH drive (Zytiga product information).
    - **Trap:** **androgen receptor blockade** like flutamide. Your Pharm HY notes list abiraterone as a receptor blocker; that's the error the Correction fixes.

11. A 63-year-old man with stable angina uses sublingual nitroglycerin about once a week. He asks for a pill for erectile dysfunction that his friend uses. What is the main danger of combining these two drugs?
    - **Answer:** **profound systemic hypotension.**
    - **Why:** nitrates give NO, which raises cGMP. PDE5 inhibitors stop cGMP from being broken down. Together they relax vascular smooth muscle excessively, so the combination is absolutely contraindicated (El-Husseiny Cardiovascular p. 201; NBME TC p. 156; Pharm HY PDF 71-72).
    - **Trap:** **reflex bradycardia**. The main danger is the drop in pressure itself.

12. A 37-year-old woman who smokes a pack of cigarettes a day asks to start "the pill" for contraception. Her blood pressure is 118/76 mm Hg, and she has no other conditions. Which method should she not be given?
    - **Answer:** **a combined estrogen-progestin pill.**
    - **Why:** smoking at age 35 or older is an unacceptable health risk for combined pills because of clotting and cardiovascular risk. Offer a progestin-only method or an IUD instead (MSD).
    - **Trap:** **"combined pills are fine because her blood pressure is normal"**. Her age plus smoking alone rules them out.

13. A 23-year-old woman with a BMI of 36 had unprotected intercourse 4 days ago. She wants the most effective emergency option and would like long-term contraception afterward. Which is the best choice?
    - **Answer:** **a copper IUD.**
    - **Why:** it works up to 5 days after intercourse, has the lowest failure rate (about 0.1%), is preferred in obesity and can stay in for years (MSD).
    - **Trap:** **oral levonorgestrel**, which is less effective when BMI is over 30 and has a 2-3% failure rate (MSD).

14. A 42-year-old Black woman has periods that soak a pad every hour for 3 days, and her hemoglobin is 8.9 g/dL with low ferritin. Her uterus is enlarged and irregular but not tender. She is scheduled for myomectomy in 3 months and wants to shrink the masses first. Which treatment will reduce their size?
    - **Answer:** **a GnRH agonist** (for example, leuprolide) or an oral GnRH antagonist (elagolix or relugolix) with add-back hormone.
    - **Why:** these are fibroids, which depend on estrogen. Cutting estrogen shrinks them for a while, though they regrow after the drug stops (MSD).
    - **Trap:** **tranexamic acid**, which reduces bleeding but does not shrink fibroids (MSD).
