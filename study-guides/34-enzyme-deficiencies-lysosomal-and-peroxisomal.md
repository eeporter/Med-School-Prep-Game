## In one line
Each lysosomal storage disease is one missing lysosomal enzyme, so its substrate piles up in the cells that handle it most: neurons (Tay-Sachs), macrophages (Gaucher, Niemann-Pick), myelin (Krabbe, metachromatic leukodystrophy) or blood vessels and kidneys (Fabry). Almost all are **autosomal recessive**; **Fabry and Hunter are X-linked**.

## Core mechanism
1. Lysosomal enzymes are tagged with **mannose-6-phosphate** in the Golgi so they reach the lysosome. In **I-cell disease** that tagging enzyme (N-acetylglucosaminyl-1-phosphotransferase) is missing, so the enzymes are secreted instead: coarse face, clouded corneas, stiff joints and **high plasma lysosomal enzymes**; often fatal in childhood (El-Husseiny Biochemistry, p. 44).
2. Tay-Sachs, Niemann-Pick and some Gaucher forms are more common in **Ashkenazi Jewish** people (p. 199).
3. Inheritance: for the mucopolysaccharidoses, MSD says "Inheritance is usually autosomal recessive (except for MPS type II)" ([MSD Manual: Overview of Lysosomal Storage Disorders](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/inherited-disorders-of-metabolism/overview-of-lysosomal-storage-disorders)); El-Husseiny gives Fabry and Hunter as X-linked recessive (pp. 203-204).

## Sphingolipidoses
| Disease | Missing enzyme | What builds up | Clues |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Tay-Sachs** | **Hexosaminidase A** ("TAy-SaX") | GM2 ganglioside | Regression, **cherry-red macula**, big head, "onion skin" lysosomes, **no** big liver or spleen; death by 2-5 years (p. 199) |
| **Niemann-Pick** (A) | **Sphingomyelinase** | Sphingomyelin | Regression, cherry-red macula **plus massive hepatosplenomegaly**, **foamy histiocytes**; death before 3. "No man picks his nose with his sphinger" (p. 200) |
| **Gaucher** | **Glucocerebrosidase** | Glucocerebroside | The **most common**; hepatosplenomegaly, pancytopenia, bone crises, **avascular necrosis of the femur**, "**crumpled tissue paper**" macrophages; treat with recombinant enzyme (p. 202) |
| **Fabry** (X-linked) | **Alpha-galactosidase A** | Ceramide trihexoside (globotriaosylceramide) | Early: burning hand and foot pain, **angiokeratomas** between umbilicus and knees, **little sweating**; late: kidney failure and heart disease (p. 203) |
| **Krabbe** | **Galactocerebrosidase** | Galactocerebroside, psychosine | Peripheral neuropathy, loss of oligodendrocytes, optic atrophy, **globoid cells** (p. 204) |
| **Metachromatic leukodystrophy** | **Arylsulfatase A** | Cerebroside sulfate (sulfatide) | Central **and** peripheral demyelination, ataxia, dementia (p. 202) |
| Farber | Ceramidase | Ceramide | Hoarse cry, skin nodules, joint deformity (your table; MSD) |

Sources: El-Husseiny Biochemistry, pp. 199-204; your enzyme table; [MSD Manual: Overview of Lysosomal Storage Disorders](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/inherited-disorders-of-metabolism/overview-of-lysosomal-storage-disorders)

- **Why a cherry-red spot:** ganglioside fills the retinal ganglion cells, turning the macula white; the fovea has no ganglion cells, so the red choroid shows through (El-Husseiny Biochemistry, p. 199).
- MSD treatments: Gaucher has **enzyme replacement** (imiglucerase) and **substrate reduction** (eliglustat, miglustat); Fabry has enzyme replacement (agalsidase beta); Hurler has laronidase and stem cell transplant; Hunter has idursulfase.

**Read more on EyeWiki:** [Cherry-Red Spot](https://eyewiki.org/Cherry-Red_Spot) (links for extra reading only; facts above come from the cited sources)

**Correction (your table's enzyme names):** the table gives Gaucher disease's enzyme as "B-galactosidase." It is **glucocerebrosidase** (El-Husseiny Biochemistry, p. 202; MSD: "glucosylceramide beta-glucosidase"). The table's Krabbe enzyme, "β-galactosidase", is correct only in the narrow sense: the specific enzyme is **galactocerebrosidase** (galactosylceramide beta-galactosidase, MSD). The table also shortens "hexosaminidase A" (Tay-Sachs) and "arylsulfatase A" (metachromatic leukodystrophy) by dropping the "A."

**Recent change (metachromatic leukodystrophy):** your notes give no treatment. In March 2024 the FDA approved the first gene therapy for children with MLD, **atidarsagene autotemcel (Lenmeldy)**: the child's own stem cells are "modified by adding a functional copy of the ARSA gene" ([FDA press release, 18 Mar 2024](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-gene-therapy-children-metachromatic-leukodystrophy)). MSD (March 2024) still describes gene therapy as under investigation.

## Peroxisomal disorders (El-Husseiny Biochemistry, p. 195)
Peroxisomes do beta-oxidation of **very-long-chain fatty acids (VLCFA)**, **alpha-oxidation** (only in peroxisomes) and make **plasmalogens** for myelin.
| Disorder | Defect | Clues |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Zellweger syndrome** | No working peroxisomes (**PEX** genes); autosomal recessive | Floppy newborn, seizures, big liver, wide sutures and fontanelle; death within months |
| **Refsum disease** | Faulty **alpha-oxidation**, so **phytanic acid** builds up | Scaly skin (ichthyosis), ataxia, night blindness, cataracts, short 4th toe; avoid chlorophyll-containing foods; plasmapheresis |
| **Adrenoleukodystrophy** | **ABCD1** (X-linked recessive); VLCFA cannot enter peroxisomes | VLCFA builds up in **adrenal glands**, brain white matter and testes; adrenal crisis, coma, death |

- MSD adds: Refsum's enzyme is **phytanoyl-CoA hydroxylase**, and it causes peripheral neuropathy, **retinitis pigmentosa**, hearing loss and ichthyosis; diet limits phytanic acid to under 10 mg a day. X-linked ALD is suspected from raised VLCFA and confirmed by gene sequencing; the cerebral form starts at ages 4-8 and affects 40%; a milder **adrenomyeloneuropathy** starts in the 20s or 30s; any form can cause adrenal insufficiency ([MSD Manual: Peroxisomal Disorders](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/inherited-disorders-of-metabolism/peroxisomal-disorders)).

**Correction (your table's Refsum row):** the table repeats the fatty acid oxidation phrase "reduced fatty acid oxidation and ketogenesis with hypoglycemia." Refsum disease does not cause hypoglycemia; it is phytanic acid buildup from a peroxisomal alpha-oxidation defect, with neuropathy, retinitis pigmentosa and ichthyosis (El-Husseiny Biochemistry, p. 195; [MSD Manual: Peroxisomal Disorders](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/inherited-disorders-of-metabolism/peroxisomal-disorders)).

## Clinical vignette
A 6-month-old of Ashkenazi Jewish descent is losing head control and startles strongly to noise. Eye exam shows cherry-red spots in both maculae. The liver and spleen are normal size. Which enzyme is missing?

**Answer:** hexosaminidase A (Tay-Sachs disease).
**Why:** GM2 ganglioside fills neurons, giving regression and cherry-red spots; the liver and spleen stay normal because macrophages are not loaded (El-Husseiny Biochemistry, p. 199; [MSD Manual](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/inherited-disorders-of-metabolism/overview-of-lysosomal-storage-disorders)).
**Trap:** Niemann-Pick also has a cherry-red spot but adds **massive hepatosplenomegaly**. The exaggerated startle is standard teaching.

## Common traps
- Cherry-red spot **without** big organs = Tay-Sachs; **with** big organs = Niemann-Pick.
- Gaucher = bone pain, avascular necrosis and "crumpled tissue paper" cells.
- Fabry = burning feet, angiokeratomas, little sweating, kidney failure; X-linked.
- Hunter = no corneal clouding, aggressive, X-linked; Hurler = clouded corneas.
- Peripheral **and** central demyelination = Krabbe (globoid cells) or metachromatic leukodystrophy.
- Adrenal insufficiency plus brain white-matter disease in a boy = adrenoleukodystrophy (VLCFA).

## Practice questions
1. A 35-year-old woman of Ashkenazi descent has bone pain, a big spleen and low platelets. Bone marrow shows macrophages that look like crumpled tissue paper. Missing enzyme and first-line treatment?
   - **Answer:** glucocerebrosidase (Gaucher disease); enzyme replacement (imiglucerase) or substrate reduction (eliglustat).
   - **Why:** glucocerebroside builds up in macrophages (El-Husseiny Biochemistry, p. 202; [MSD Manual](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/inherited-disorders-of-metabolism/overview-of-lysosomal-storage-disorders)).
   - **Trap:** choosing "beta-galactosidase", the error in the table.
2. A 14-year-old boy has burning pain in his hands and feet in hot weather, dark red papules around his belly button, and protein in his urine. Which enzyme and which inheritance?
   - **Answer:** alpha-galactosidase A; X-linked recessive (Fabry disease).
   - **Why:** ceramide trihexoside builds up in nerves, skin vessels and kidney cells (El-Husseiny Biochemistry, p. 203).
   - **Trap:** choosing an autosomal recessive sphingolipidosis.
3. A 6-year-old boy has new behavior problems and falling grades. MRI shows white-matter disease at the back of the brain, and he has darkening skin and low cortisol. Which substance is high in his blood?
   - **Answer:** very-long-chain fatty acids (X-linked adrenoleukodystrophy).
   - **Why:** ABCD1 mutation blocks VLCFA entry into peroxisomes; they build up in adrenal glands and white matter (El-Husseiny Biochemistry, p. 195; [MSD Manual: Peroxisomal Disorders](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/inherited-disorders-of-metabolism/peroxisomal-disorders)). The back-of-brain MRI pattern and skin darkening (from high ACTH) are standard teaching.
   - **Trap:** metachromatic leukodystrophy does not cause adrenal failure.
4. A 5-year-old boy has coarse facial features, stiff joints and clear corneas, and is very aggressive. Diagnosis?
   - **Answer:** Hunter syndrome (iduronate-2-sulfatase deficiency).
   - **Why:** milder Hurler picture with no corneal clouding and aggression (El-Husseiny Biochemistry, p. 204).
   - **Trap:** Hurler has clouded corneas.
