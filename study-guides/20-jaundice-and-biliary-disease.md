# Jaundice and bile duct disease: bilirubin, liver tests, gallstones, PBC and PSC

## In one line
First ask whether the extra bilirubin is **unconjugated** (too much made, or the liver can't take it up or conjugate it) or **conjugated** (the liver can't get it out into bile, or the bile can't drain). Then use the liver enzymes: high aminotransferases (ALT and AST) mean liver cell injury; high alkaline phosphatase (ALP) means cholestasis.

## Core mechanism
1. Macrophages take up hemoglobin from old red cells and turn it into **biliverdin**, then unconjugated bilirubin (Osmosis Physiology, p. 332); the enzyme names, heme oxygenase and biliverdin reductase, are standard teaching. Gut bacteria make urobilinogen; some is reabsorbed and leaves in urine as urobilin, and the rest leaves in stool as **stercobilin** (Osmosis Physiology, p. 332). Hemoglobin breakdown makes **unconjugated** bilirubin, which is carried to the liver bound to albumin. Liver cells conjugate it with glucuronic acid, which makes it water soluble, and excrete it in bile into the duodenum. Gut bacteria turn it into urobilinogen; some is reabsorbed and re-excreted in bile. [MSD Manual: Jaundice](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/approach-to-the-patient-with-liver-disease/jaundice)
2. The enzyme that conjugates bilirubin is glucuronyl transferase, coded by the *UGT1A1* gene. [MSD Manual: Inborn Metabolic Disorders Causing Hyperbilirubinemia](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/approach-to-the-patient-with-liver-disease/inborn-metabolic-disorders-causing-hyperbilirubinemia)
3. **Why dark urine means conjugated bilirubin:** MSD: "conjugated bilirubin is excreted in urine; unconjugated bilirubin is not." Unconjugated bilirubin is not water soluble and travels bound to albumin. [MSD Manual: Jaundice](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/approach-to-the-patient-with-liver-disease/jaundice)
4. Jaundice becomes visible at a bilirubin of about **2 to 3 mg/dL**. [MSD Manual: Jaundice](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/approach-to-the-patient-with-liver-disease/jaundice)
5. **Causes by mechanism.** Unconjugated: more production (for example hemolysis), less uptake by the liver, or less conjugation. Conjugated: liver cell dysfunction, intrahepatic cholestasis or extrahepatic cholestasis (a blocked duct). [MSD Manual: Jaundice](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/approach-to-the-patient-with-liver-disease/jaundice)

## Reading the liver tests
| Test | What a high (or low) value means |
| --- | --- |
| ALT and AST | They "leak from damaged hepatocytes." ALT is more liver specific; AST also comes from heart, skeletal muscle, kidney, red cells and pancreas. Values above 500 IU/L mean acute liver cell injury: acute viral hepatitis, toxin- or drug-induced hepatitis, or ischemic hepatitis |
| AST:ALT above 2 | Characteristic of alcohol-related liver disease, because pyridoxal-5'-phosphate, which ALT synthesis needs, is often deficient with heavy alcohol use |
| ALP | Suggests cholestasis; rises to 4 or more times normal 1-2 days after the bile duct is blocked. Not liver specific (also bone, placenta, intestine) |
| GGT (gamma-glutamyl transferase) | Confirms that a high ALP comes from the liver; alcohol and some drugs can raise it on their own |
| Albumin (low) and PT/INR (prolonged) | Show how well the liver makes proteins. Albumin's half-life is about 20 days, so it changes slowly. A cholestatic patient can also have a long PT/INR because vitamin K is poorly absorbed |
| Bilirubin fractions | Unconjugated (indirect) if the indirect part is above 85%; conjugated (direct) if the direct part is above 50% |

Source for the whole table: [MSD Manual: Laboratory Tests of the Liver and Gallbladder](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/testing-for-hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/laboratory-tests-of-the-liver-and-gallbladder)

**Worked example.** Total bilirubin 6.0 mg/dL, direct 4.2 mg/dL. Direct fraction = 4.2 / 6.0 = 70%, which is above 50%, so this is conjugated hyperbilirubinemia; look for cholestasis or liver cell disease, not hemolysis.

## Inherited causes of jaundice
| Syndrome | Defect | Bilirubin | Key feature | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gilbert | Variants in *UGT1A1*, the glucuronyl transferase gene; your notes: a **promoter** mutation, so the enzyme is normal but less is made (Osmosis GI, p. 94) | Unconjugated, mild | Affects 3-10% of people; rises with **fasting**, illness, dehydration, overexertion and menses; normal liver histology | None needed |
| Crigler-Najjar type 1 | **Complete** loss of glucuronyl transferase (a coding-region mutation; autosomal recessive) | Unconjugated, severe: 20-50 mg/dL in your notes | Usually fatal from kernicterus by age 1 without treatment; pale stools with low fecal urobilinogen | Phototherapy, liver transplant (the definitive treatment in your notes) |
| Crigler-Najjar type 2 | **Partial** loss of the enzyme | Unconjugated, less severe: below 20 mg/dL in your notes | Usually reach adulthood without brain damage; normal stool color | **Phenobarbital**, which induces the remaining enzyme |
| Dubin-Johnson | Impaired excretion of bilirubin glucuronides; your notes name the **MRP2** gene (Osmosis GI, p. 93) | Conjugated, without cholestasis | Liver is **deeply pigmented** (pigment in lysosomes); autosomal recessive; jaundice shows with illness, pregnancy or oral contraceptives; the gallbladder may not show on oral cholecystogram; urinary coproporphyrin is mostly type I | None needed |
| Rotor | Inherited; your notes: *SLCO1B1*/*SLCO1B3* mutations that impair the liver's **re-uptake** of bilirubin (Osmosis GI, p. 94) | Conjugated, without cholestasis | Liver is **not** pigmented; the gallbladder fills normally on oral cholecystogram | None needed |

Source: [MSD Manual: Inborn Metabolic Disorders Causing Hyperbilirubinemia](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/approach-to-the-patient-with-liver-disease/inborn-metabolic-disorders-causing-hyperbilirubinemia). ## Gallstones and their complications
| Condition | Where the stone is | Pain | Labs | Key step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Biliary colic | Briefly blocking the cystic duct | Right upper quadrant (RUQ), often to the right shoulder blade; builds for about 15 minutes, plateaus, gone within 6 hours; often after a meal or at night (Osmosis GI, p. 4) | Normal liver enzymes (Osmosis GI summary table, p. 9) | Ultrasound; laparoscopic cholecystectomy if symptomatic |
| Acute cholecystitis | **Impacted** in the cystic duct | RUQ pain **over 6 hours**, often low-grade fever, **Murphy sign** | Liver tests normal or slightly high; bilirubin up to about 4 mg/dL from inflammation | Ultrasound first; cholescintigraphy (HIDA) if unclear: the gallbladder does not fill |
| Choledocholithiasis and cholangitis | In the **common bile duct** | Charcot triad: pain, jaundice, fever or chills | High bilirubin and especially high ALP (obstruction) | Antibiotics plus early decompression, usually ERCP |

**Other source (cholecystitis labs):** your notes list **raised ALP and white count** in acute cholecystitis (Osmosis GI, p. 6); MSD says liver tests are normal or only slightly high. Both fit: a raised white count is expected, and ALP rises only a little unless a stone reaches the common bile duct.

Your notes add (Osmosis GI, pp. 2-6): cholangitis bacteria are *E. coli*, *Klebsiella*, *Enterobacter* and *Enterococcus*; gallstones are the most common cause. About 90% of acute cholecystitis settles within a month as the stone falls back. Acalculous cholecystitis is 5-10% of cases, in critically ill or post-surgical patients, from gallbladder ischemia. Chronic cholecystitis can give Rokitansky-Aschoff sinuses and a calcified **porcelain gallbladder**, which raises the risk of gallbladder cancer (p. 7).

Sources: [MSD Manual: Cholelithiasis](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/gallbladder-and-bile-duct-disorders/cholelithiasis), [MSD Manual: Acute Cholecystitis](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/gallbladder-and-bile-duct-disorders/acute-cholecystitis), [MSD Manual: Choledocholithiasis and Cholangitis](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/gallbladder-and-bile-duct-disorders/choledocholithiasis-and-cholangitis)

- **Murphy sign:** "deep inspiration exacerbates the pain during palpation of the right upper quadrant and halts inspiration." [MSD Manual: Acute Cholecystitis](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/gallbladder-and-bile-duct-disorders/acute-cholecystitis)
- **Reynolds pentad:** Charcot triad plus confusion and hypotension. Cholangitis is an emergency; the usual organisms are gram-negative bacteria. A stone that blocks the ampulla of Vater causes **gallstone pancreatitis**, the most common cause of acute pancreatitis. [MSD Manual: Choledocholithiasis and Cholangitis](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/gallbladder-and-bile-duct-disorders/choledocholithiasis-and-cholangitis)
- **Acalculous cholecystitis** (no stone) occurs in critically ill patients and with prolonged fasting or intravenous feeding; cholescintigraphy is the most reliable test. [MSD Manual: Acute Cholecystitis](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/gallbladder-and-bile-duct-disorders/acute-cholecystitis)
- **Stone types:** cholesterol stones are over 85% in the Western world (bile supersaturated with cholesterol). **Other source:** your notes say cholesterol stones are "most common, 80%" (Osmosis GI, p. 10); either way they are the most common type. Your notes add that cholesterol stones are **radiolucent** and pigment stones **radiopaque**, and the risk-factor mnemonic **Fat, Female, Fertile, Forty** (plus fatty food intolerance and flatulence), with oral contraceptives, rapid weight loss and prolonged TPN (pp. 10-11). **Black** pigment stones come with chronic hemolysis and alcohol-related liver disease. **Brown** pigment stones form with infection and parasites such as liver flukes (your notes: *E. coli*, *Ascaris lumbricoides*, *Clonorchis sinensis*; more common in Asian populations, Osmosis GI, p. 10). Risk factors include female sex, obesity, older age and family history. Asymptomatic stones are usually not treated; 1-4% per year become symptomatic. [MSD Manual: Cholelithiasis](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/gallbladder-and-bile-duct-disorders/cholelithiasis)

## Jaundice in newborns
- **Physiologic jaundice** (unconjugated) comes from three things: red cells that live a shorter time, immature conjugation, and more enterohepatic recycling. Bilirubin usually rises over the first 3-4 days of life, then falls. [MSD Manual: Neonatal Hyperbilirubinemia](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/metabolic-electrolyte-and-toxic-disorders-in-neonates/neonatal-hyperbilirubinemia)
- **Suspect a problem** when jaundice appears in the first 24 hours, after the first week, or lasts more than 2 weeks, or when conjugated bilirubin is above 1 mg/dL (with total below 5 mg/dL). [MSD Manual: Neonatal Hyperbilirubinemia](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/metabolic-electrolyte-and-toxic-disorders-in-neonates/neonatal-hyperbilirubinemia)
- **Breastfeeding jaundice** (first week) comes from low milk intake, which increases enterohepatic recycling. **Breast milk jaundice** starts after days 5-7, peaks around 2 weeks, and is thought to come from beta-glucuronidase in the milk. [MSD Manual: Neonatal Hyperbilirubinemia](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/metabolic-electrolyte-and-toxic-disorders-in-neonates/neonatal-hyperbilirubinemia)
- **Kernicterus** (now called chronic bilirubin encephalopathy) is brain damage from unconjugated bilirubin deposited in the basal ganglia and brainstem nuclei. **Phototherapy** photoisomerizes unconjugated bilirubin into more water-soluble forms that can be excreted without conjugation. [MSD Manual: Neonatal Hyperbilirubinemia](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/metabolic-electrolyte-and-toxic-disorders-in-neonates/neonatal-hyperbilirubinemia)
- **Biliary atresia:** progressive sclerosis of the extrahepatic bile duct. It shows up several weeks to months after birth with jaundice, **dark urine** (conjugated bilirubin), **acholic (pale) stools** and a large liver and spleen. Total and direct bilirubin are both high. Kasai portoenterostomy works best in the first month of life; if cholestasis persists 3 months after surgery, the child is referred for liver transplant. Your notes add: babies look well at birth and stools gradually turn clay-colored; ultrasound can show an absent common bile duct and a "**triangular cord**" sign; an intraoperative cholangiogram is the gold standard; some cases come with laterality defects (situs inversus, polysplenia) (Osmosis GI, pp. 90-91). [MSD Manual: Biliary Atresia](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/congenital-gastrointestinal-anomalies/biliary-atresia)

## Compare: primary biliary cholangitis (PBC) vs primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC)
**Recent change (name):** your notes title it "Primary Biliary Cirrhosis" (Osmosis GI, p. 120). In 2015 the name was changed to **primary biliary cholangitis** [Beuers et al., Changing nomenclature for PBC: from 'cirrhosis' to 'cholangitis', Clin Res Hepatol Gastroenterol 2015;39:e57-e59](https://pure.amsterdamumc.nl/en/publications/changing-nomenclature-for-pbc-from-cirrhosis-to-cholangitis/); most patients are diagnosed long before they have cirrhosis (standard teaching). Your notes give the new name as "AKA".
| Feature | PBC | PSC |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who | 95% women, age 40-70 | About two-thirds men, peak age 25-45 |
| What is damaged | Autoimmune destruction of small **intrahepatic** bile ducts | Patchy inflammation, fibrosis and strictures of **intrahepatic and extrahepatic** ducts |
| Antibody | **Antimitochondrial antibody** in 90-95% (against the E2 part of mitochondrial pyruvate dehydrogenase, Osmosis GI, p. 120); IgM raised | Antinuclear antibody and **p-ANCA** usually positive (p-ANCA in 80%, Osmosis GI, p. 13) |
| Associations | Sjögren syndrome, systemic sclerosis and CREST, autoimmune thyroiditis | **Inflammatory bowel disease** in 60-90% (ulcerative colitis and Crohn disease, Osmosis GI, p. 12; mostly ulcerative colitis, standard teaching); HLA-B8, HLA-DR3 |
| Diagnosis | Cholestatic labs (ALP usually above 1.5 times normal) plus antimitochondrial antibody | Cholangiography, starting with magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP): multiple strictures and dilations |
| Cancer risk | — | **Cholangiocarcinoma** in 10-20%; colorectal cancer risk with IBD, so colonoscopy **every year** from PSC diagnosis |
| Biopsy | Bile duct destruction with lymphocytes and **periductal granulomas** (Osmosis GI, p. 121) | **"Onion-skin" fibrosis** around ducts (Osmosis GI, p. 13) |
| Complications | Osteoporosis, high cholesterol (xanthelasma), fat-soluble vitamin deficiency (Osmosis GI, p. 120) | Cirrhosis, cholangiocarcinoma, gallbladder cancer, hepatocellular carcinoma (Osmosis GI, p. 12) |
| Treatment | **Ursodeoxycholic acid** first line; cholestyramine for itching (Osmosis GI, p. 121) | No effective drug; liver transplant improves survival (Osmosis GI, p. 13) |

Sources: [MSD Manual: Primary Biliary Cholangitis](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/fibrosis-and-cirrhosis/primary-biliary-cholangitis-pbc), [MSD Manual: Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/gallbladder-and-bile-duct-disorders/primary-sclerosing-cholangitis-psc). PSC bile ducts look **"beaded"**: narrowed segments next to dilated ones (Osmosis GI, p. 12; MSD says "multiple strictures and dilations").

## High-yield facts
- **Urine clues:** hemolysis gives "no bilirubin in urine", while bile duct obstruction puts conjugated bilirubin in the urine [MSD Manual: Jaundice](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/approach-to-the-patient-with-liver-disease/jaundice). Urine urobilinogen goes **up** in hemolysis (more bilirubin reaches the gut) and **down or absent** in complete obstruction (no bilirubin reaches the gut) (standard teaching).
- Up to 80% of patients with PBC have itching (pruritus) and fatigue; about half have no symptoms at diagnosis. [MSD Manual: Primary Biliary Cholangitis](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/fibrosis-and-cirrhosis/primary-biliary-cholangitis-pbc)
- Severe cholestasis can give clay-colored stools and steatorrhea. [MSD Manual: Jaundice](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/approach-to-the-patient-with-liver-disease/jaundice)
- Ultrasound is the imaging test of choice for gallstones (sensitivity and specificity about 95%). [MSD Manual: Cholelithiasis](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/gallbladder-and-bile-duct-disorders/cholelithiasis)
- Charcot triad is specific but not very sensitive, so its absence does not rule out cholangitis. [MSD Manual: Choledocholithiasis and Cholangitis](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/gallbladder-and-bile-duct-disorders/choledocholithiasis-and-cholangitis)

## Clinical vignette
A 52-year-old woman has 6 months of fatigue and itching. She also has dry eyes and dry mouth. Alkaline phosphatase is 3 times normal, GGT is high, ALT is mildly high, and ultrasound shows normal bile ducts. Antimitochondrial antibody is positive. What is the diagnosis, and what is first-line treatment?

**Answer:** primary biliary cholangitis; ursodeoxycholic acid.
**Why:** a middle-aged woman with cholestatic labs (high ALP confirmed as liver by GGT), normal ducts on ultrasound and antimitochondrial antibody (positive in 90-95%) has PBC; the dry eyes and mouth suggest associated Sjögren syndrome. [MSD Manual: Primary Biliary Cholangitis](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/fibrosis-and-cirrhosis/primary-biliary-cholangitis-pbc), [MSD Manual: Laboratory Tests](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/testing-for-hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/laboratory-tests-of-the-liver-and-gallbladder)
**Trap:** choosing PSC. PSC is mostly in younger men with ulcerative colitis and p-ANCA, and it shows strictures on MRCP.

## Common traps
- Gilbert = unconjugated, mild, worse with fasting, normal liver. Dubin-Johnson = conjugated, **black** liver. Rotor = conjugated, normal-colored liver.
- Crigler-Najjar type 2 responds to phenobarbital; type 1 does not have enzyme to induce (standard teaching), so it needs phototherapy or a transplant.
- AST:ALT above 2 points to alcohol. ALT is the more liver-specific enzyme.
- High ALP alone could be bone; a high GGT says it is liver.
- Cholecystitis usually has normal or only slightly high liver tests; jaundice with fever and RUQ pain points to the common bile duct (cholangitis).
- Black pigment stones = hemolysis; brown pigment stones = infection or parasites.

## Practice questions
1. A 22-year-old has yellow eyes after 2 days of fasting with the flu. Total bilirubin 3.0 mg/dL, direct 0.3 mg/dL. Other liver tests and the blood count are normal. What is the defect?
   - **Answer:** variants in *UGT1A1*, the glucuronyl transferase gene (Gilbert syndrome).
   - **Why:** indirect fraction = 2.7 / 3.0 = 90%, above 85%, so it is unconjugated. With no hemolysis and normal liver enzymes, a mild rise with fasting is Gilbert syndrome. [MSD Manual: Inborn Metabolic Disorders](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/approach-to-the-patient-with-liver-disease/inborn-metabolic-disorders-causing-hyperbilirubinemia), [MSD Manual: Laboratory Tests](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/testing-for-hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/laboratory-tests-of-the-liver-and-gallbladder)
   - **Trap:** ordering a liver biopsy; histology is normal and no treatment is needed.
2. A healthy 25-year-old has mild conjugated hyperbilirubinemia with normal ALP. A liver biopsy is black but otherwise normal. Diagnosis?
   - **Answer:** Dubin-Johnson syndrome.
   - **Why:** impaired excretion of bilirubin glucuronides gives conjugated bilirubin without cholestasis, and a melanin-like pigment darkens the liver. [MSD Manual: Inborn Metabolic Disorders](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/approach-to-the-patient-with-liver-disease/inborn-metabolic-disorders-causing-hyperbilirubinemia)
   - **Trap:** Rotor syndrome looks the same in the blood, but the liver is not pigmented.
3. A 68-year-old with known gallstones has RUQ pain, jaundice and a fever of 39 °C. Now she is confused and her blood pressure is 82/50. Bilirubin and ALP are high. Diagnosis and key step?
   - **Answer:** acute cholangitis with Reynolds pentad; antibiotics, support and urgent bile duct decompression (usually ERCP).
   - **Why:** a common bile duct stone blocks drainage, and gram-negative infection follows. Charcot triad plus confusion and hypotension is Reynolds pentad. [MSD Manual: Choledocholithiasis and Cholangitis](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/gallbladder-and-bile-duct-disorders/choledocholithiasis-and-cholangitis)
   - **Trap:** treating it as cholecystitis and waiting; a blocked, infected duct needs drainage.
4. A man who drinks heavily has AST 240 IU/L and ALT 100 IU/L. Why is AST higher than ALT?
   - **Answer:** low pyridoxal-5'-phosphate holds ALT down, giving the alcohol-related pattern of AST:ALT above 2 (here 240 / 100 = 2.4).
   - **Why:** ALT synthesis needs pyridoxal-5'-phosphate (the active form of vitamin B6, standard teaching), which is often deficient with heavy alcohol use, so ALT rises less than AST. [MSD Manual: Laboratory Tests of the Liver and Gallbladder](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/testing-for-hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/laboratory-tests-of-the-liver-and-gallbladder)
   - **Trap:** reading the ratio as heart muscle injury; ALT is the liver-specific enzyme, and the pattern with heavy drinking is the clue.
