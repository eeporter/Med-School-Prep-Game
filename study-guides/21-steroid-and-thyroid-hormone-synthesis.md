# How steroid and thyroid hormones are made, and the drugs and defects that block them

Exam focus: USMLE Step 1 and NBME · Study material, not patient care guidance · Auditor: cleared 2026-10-27

## In one line
Adrenal steroids are built from **cholesterol**, and each zone of the cortex has its own enzymes, so a single blocked enzyme starves one product and floods the others. Thyroid hormone is built on **thyroglobulin** by **thyroid peroxidase**, and T4 is mostly a prohormone that tissues convert into active T3.

## Part 1: adrenal steroids

### Core mechanism
1. **First step:** the StAR protein (steroidogenic acute regulatory protein) moves cholesterol from the outer to the inner mitochondrial membrane. There, CYP11A1 (side-chain cleavage enzyme, "desmolase" in review books) makes **pregnenolone**. Two different steps get called "rate-limiting": Baranowski gives the label to the **enzyme step, CYP11A1 making pregnenolone** ("the rate-limiting step of all steroid hormone biosynthesis"), while many review books give it to **StAR moving cholesterol into the mitochondria** (standard teaching). If a question says "rate-limiting enzyme", the answer is CYP11A1 (desmolase). [Baranowski, Arlt and Idkowiak 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6067656/)
2. **Zona glomerulosa (outer layer), mineralocorticoids:** 3β-HSD2 (3β-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase) makes progesterone, CYP21A2 (21-hydroxylase) hydroxylates it, and **CYP11B2 (aldosterone synthase)** finishes aldosterone. CYP11B1 is not expressed in this zone. This branch is driven by the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system. [Baranowski 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6067656/) Potassium as a second driver of aldosterone is standard teaching.
3. **Zona fasciculata (middle layer), glucocorticoids:** CYP17A1 adds a hydroxyl group at carbon 17 (17α-hydroxylase). Then 21-hydroxylase and finally **CYP11B1 (11β-hydroxylase)**, which turns 11-deoxycortisol into cortisol. This branch is driven by ACTH (adrenocorticotropic hormone). [Baranowski 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6067656/)
4. **Zona reticularis (inner layer), androgen precursors:** the 17,20-lyase activity of the same CYP17A1 enzyme makes the sex-steroid precursors. It needs cytochrome b5, which is only expressed in the reticularis. [Baranowski 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6067656/) The adrenal androgens are mainly DHEA (dehydroepiandrosterone) and androstenedione, which act mostly after conversion to testosterone and dihydrotestosterone. [MSD Manual: Overview of Adrenal Function](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/adrenal-disorders/overview-of-adrenal-function)
5. **Why a block causes hyperplasia:** when cortisol can't be made, ACTH "normally suppressed by cortisol, is excessive", and the precursors made before the block "accumulate and are shunted into adrenal androgen synthesis". All forms of congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH) are autosomal recessive. [MSD Manual: Overview of CAH](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/endocrine-disorders-in-children/overview-of-congenital-adrenal-hyperplasia) That constant ACTH drive enlarges the glands (standard teaching).

**Memory hook from your notes (El-Husseiny Endocrinology, p. 36):** the zones spell **GFR** from outside in, and they make **salt, sugar, sex**: glomerulosa (aldosterone), fasciculata (cortisol), reticularis (androgens); "the deeper you go, the sweeter it gets." Your notes add that the glomerulosa answers to renin-angiotensin while the fasciculata and reticularis answer to ACTH, which has no major effect on aldosterone; the cortex comes from mesoderm and the medulla from neural crest.

**Your Osmosis notes (HY Physiology, pp. 276-277):** cholesterol desmolase (side-chain cleavage), present in all three zones, is the **rate-limiting step**, is driven by ACTH and turns cholesterol into pregnenolone. Aldosterone synthase is found only in the glomerulosa; 17,20-lyase makes the androgens DHEA and androstenedione, which are weak and mostly act after conversion.
- **Correction:** Osmosis says aldosterone synthase "converts cortisone → aldosterone" (p. 277). It actually works on 11-deoxycorticosterone, turning it into corticosterone, then 18-hydroxycorticosterone, then aldosterone. [Baranowski 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6067656/) Cortisone is the inactive form of cortisol, not an aldosterone precursor (standard teaching).
- **Correction:** Osmosis says 17,20-lyase converts "glucocorticoids into androgens" (p. 277). It cuts the 17-hydroxylated **precursors** (mainly 17-hydroxypregnenolone) to make DHEA and androstenedione, before any cortisol is made. [Baranowski 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6067656/) The same page writes "21β-hydroxylase"; the enzyme is 21-hydroxylase.

### Enzyme blocks at a glance
| Block | Precursor that rises | Aldosterone side | Cortisol | Androgens | Classic picture |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| StAR (lipoid CAH) | None made; cholesterol builds up in the gland (standard teaching) | Low | Low | Low | All steroid synthesis disrupted; severe form gives a salt-wasting adrenal crisis in newborns |
| 3β-HSD2 | **DHEA** and 17α-hydroxypregnenolone | Low | Low | Low in the adrenal, but DHEA is converted elsewhere | Rare; salt-wasting crisis with high-renin low blood pressure in infancy. **46,XY** babies are undermasculinized; **46,XX** babies can be **virilized**, because tissues convert the excess DHEA |
| 17α-hydroxylase (CYP17A1) | Mineralocorticoid precursors such as DOC (standard teaching) | High mineralocorticoid effect | Low | Low | **Hypertension with low potassium and low renin**; 46,XY babies are undervirilized |
| 21-hydroxylase (CYP21A2) | **17-hydroxyprogesterone** | Low | Low | **High** | Most common CAH; salt wasting; virilized females. See guide 08 |
| 11β-hydroxylase (CYP11B1) | **11-deoxycortisol** (standard teaching) and **DOC** (11-deoxycorticosterone; cited in guide 08) | Aldosterone low, but DOC acts as a mineralocorticoid | Low | **High** | Hypertension plus virilization. See guide 08 |

Sources: [Baranowski 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6067656/) (StAR, 3β-HSD2 including the DHEA and genital findings, and 17α-hydroxylase rows), [MSD Manual: 21-Hydroxylase Deficiency](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/endocrine-disorders-in-children/congenital-adrenal-hyperplasia-caused-by-21-hydroxylase-deficiency) (17-hydroxyprogesterone), [MSD Manual: Overview of CAH](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/endocrine-disorders-in-children/overview-of-congenital-adrenal-hyperplasia) (21- and 11β-hydroxylase are the most common forms and virilize females; the less common forms impair androgen synthesis, so males are undervirilized). The 21- and 11β-hydroxylase details are cited in guide 08.

### Worked logic: 17α-hydroxylase deficiency
The block sits at the entrance to both the cortisol and the androgen branches. Pregnenolone can only flow down the mineralocorticoid branch, so mineralocorticoid precursors pile up, sodium is held and potassium is lost. Blood pressure rises, which switches renin off. With no androgens, a 46,XY fetus is undervirilized. [Baranowski 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6067656/)

## Part 2: thyroid hormone

### Synthesis, step by step
1. **Trap iodide:** the thyroid actively concentrates iodide. The transporter is the sodium-iodide symporter (NIS) on the basolateral side of the follicular cell, an energy-using step called "iodine trapping" (El-Husseiny Endocrinology, pp. 62, 148). Perchlorate and pertechnetate compete for the same carrier, so perchlorate lowers radioactive iodine uptake, and potassium iodide can protect the thyroid from radioactive iodine (p. 148).
2. **Oxidize and organify:** **thyroid peroxidase (TPO)** turns iodide into organic iodine.
3. **Iodinate:** tyrosines on **thyroglobulin** get one iodine (monoiodotyrosine, MIT) or two (diiodotyrosine, DIT).
4. **Couple:** DIT + DIT = **T4**; DIT + MIT = **T3**. Your notes say TPO does all three jobs: oxidizing iodide, making MIT and DIT (organification), and coupling them (El-Husseiny Endocrinology, pp. 63, 147).
5. **Store:** the hormones stay attached to thyroglobulin in the colloid of the follicle.
6. **Release:** follicular cells take up colloid, cut T3 and T4 off thyroglobulin, and release them into the blood.

Source for steps 1-6: [MSD Manual: Overview of Thyroid Function](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/thyroid-disorders/overview-of-thyroid-function)

### In the blood and in the tissues
- **Your notes (El-Husseiny Endocrinology, pp. 64-65):** over 99% of thyroid hormone is protein-bound, mainly to TBG. TBG falls in liver failure and nephrotic syndrome and rises with estrogen (pregnancy, the pill, hormone replacement), which raises total T4 while free T4 stays normal. A hypothyroid woman on levothyroxine who starts estrogen usually needs a **higher dose**, because she cannot make extra hormone to fill the new TBG. T3 has about four times the activity of T4; there is about 50 times more T4 than T3; half-lives are about 7 days (T4), 1 day (T3) and under a day (reverse T3). Peripheral conversion of T4 to T3 is blocked by **glucocorticoids, beta blockers and PTU**. Liothyronine (T3) is not used for routine replacement because its short half-life makes levels swing.
- Only one fifth of circulating T3 is secreted by the thyroid; most is made in tissues by removing one iodine from T4. T4 can also become **reverse T3**, which is inactive. [MSD Manual: Overview of Thyroid Function](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/thyroid-disorders/overview-of-thyroid-function) The enzymes are the deiodinases: type 1 and type 2 make T3 from T4, and type 3 makes reverse T3 (standard teaching; MSD does not name them).
- **T3 is the active hormone.** It binds nuclear receptors and changes gene expression. T4 has minimal activity and acts as a reservoir with a half-life of about 8 days. [MSD Manual: Overview of Thyroid Function](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/thyroid-disorders/overview-of-thyroid-function)
- Thyroxine-binding globulin (TBG) carries about 75% of bound hormone. Only free hormone acts: about 0.3% of T3 and 0.03% of T4 is free. [MSD Manual: Overview of Thyroid Function](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/thyroid-disorders/overview-of-thyroid-function)
- **Feedback:** hypothalamic TRH (thyrotropin-releasing hormone) stimulates pituitary TSH (thyroid-stimulating hormone), and TSH drives every step of synthesis and release. Rising free T4 and T3 suppress TSH and TRH. [MSD Manual: Overview of Thyroid Function](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/thyroid-disorders/overview-of-thyroid-function)

### Drugs and antibodies that act on these steps
| Agent | Step it hits | Exam point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Methimazole | Blocks TPO, so less organification | Default antithyroid drug |
| Propylthiouracil (PTU) | Blocks TPO; at high doses also blocks T4 to T3 conversion in tissues | Preferred in the **first trimester** and in **thyroid storm**; can cause severe liver failure |
| Both thionamides | — | Reversible agranulocytosis in about 0.1% (your notes: about 0.5%); check a white count with differential in any patient on either drug who gets fever or sore throat, and stop the drug. Your notes also list rash and aplastic anemia, liver toxicity with PTU, and **aplasia cutis** with methimazole (El-Husseiny Endocrinology, p. 147) |
| Iodide (high dose) | Blocks **release** of T3 and T4 within hours; blocks organification only for days to a week | Give a thionamide **first**, then iodide. Before surgery that means methimazole; in thyroid storm MSD's table gives **PTU before iodine** |
| Propranolol and other beta-blockers | Block adrenergic symptoms (fast heart rate, tremor, anxiety) | Symptom control only |
| Radioactive iodine-131 | Taken up and destroys thyroid tissue (standard teaching) | Most common US treatment; avoid in pregnancy and lactation |
| Graves antibody (TSI) | **Stimulates** the TSH receptor | Drives continuous excess T4 and T3 |

Source: [MSD Manual: Hyperthyroidism](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/thyroid-disorders/hyperthyroidism). Your notes name the **Wolff-Chaikoff effect**: a large iodine load temporarily inhibits TPO, so organification and T3/T4 output fall (El-Husseiny Endocrinology, p. 74). The opposite, **Jod-Basedow**, is hyperthyroidism when an iodine-deficient patient with an autonomous nodule gets iodine, for example IV contrast or amiodarone (p. 78).

**Thyroid storm in your notes (El-Husseiny Endocrinology, p. 77):** poorly treated hyperthyroidism plus a stress (infection, trauma, surgery) gives agitation, delirium, fever, diarrhea, coma and fast arrhythmias (the usual cause of death), sometimes with a high alkaline phosphatase. Treat with the **4 Ps**: **P**ropranolol, **P**TU, **P**rednisolone (glucocorticoid) and **P**otassium iodide (Lugol), giving the iodine at least an hour after PTU so it is not built into new hormone.

**Other source (beta blockers and T4 to T3):** your notes say beta blockers (propranolol) also slow peripheral T4-to-T3 conversion (El-Husseiny Endocrinology, pp. 65, 77). MSD does not say this; it names PTU and dexamethasone for blocking conversion. [MSD Manual: Hyperthyroidism](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/thyroid-disorders/hyperthyroidism)

**Recent change (PTU in pregnancy):** your notes call PTU "the drug of choice in pregnancy" (El-Husseiny Endocrinology, p. 147). Current guidance limits PTU to the **first trimester**, because methimazole is linked to rare birth defects early on, and switches to methimazole afterwards because PTU can cause severe liver failure. [MSD Manual: Hyperthyroidism](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/thyroid-disorders/hyperthyroidism)

## High-yield facts
- 21-hydroxylase and 11β-hydroxylase deficiency are the most common forms of CAH. [MSD Manual: Overview of CAH](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/endocrine-disorders-in-children/overview-of-congenital-adrenal-hyperplasia)
- Cortisol raises gluconeogenesis in the liver; aldosterone makes the kidney keep sodium "in exchange for potassium". [MSD Manual: Overview of Adrenal Function](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/adrenal-disorders/overview-of-adrenal-function)
- The adrenal medulla is separate: chromaffin cells make catecholamines, mainly epinephrine. [MSD Manual: Overview of Adrenal Function](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/adrenal-disorders/overview-of-adrenal-function)
- Methimazole is avoided in early pregnancy because of rare scalp and gut defects in the baby; PTU is avoided otherwise because of liver failure. [MSD Manual: Hyperthyroidism](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/thyroid-disorders/hyperthyroidism)

## Clinical vignette
A 16-year-old raised as a girl has no breast development or periods. Blood pressure is 160/100 mmHg, potassium is low and renin is low. Karyotype is 46,XY. Which enzyme is deficient?

**Answer:** 17α-hydroxylase (CYP17A1).
**Why:** without 17α-hydroxylase, neither cortisol nor sex steroids can be made, so precursors flow into the mineralocorticoid branch. That gives low-renin hypertension with low potassium, and no androgens means a 46,XY fetus is undervirilized. [Baranowski 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6067656/)
**Trap:** choosing 11β-hydroxylase deficiency. It also causes hypertension, but androgens are **high**, so it virilizes.

## Common traps
- Hypertension in CAH means 11β or 17α. High androgens point to 11β; low androgens point to 17α.
- CYP17A1 is one enzyme with two jobs: 17α-hydroxylase (cortisol and sex branches) and 17,20-lyase (sex branch).
- StAR and side-chain cleavage come first, so a block there shuts down every steroid.
- T4 is the main product of the gland, but T3 is the active hormone.
- PTU blocks peripheral conversion of T4; methimazole does not. Use PTU in the first trimester and in thyroid storm. Your notes' hook: "**P**TU blocks **P**eripheral conversion, used in **P**regnancy" (El-Husseiny Endocrinology, p. 147); remember the pregnancy part now means the first trimester only.
- A woman on levothyroxine who starts the pill or gets pregnant needs a higher dose (El-Husseiny Endocrinology, p. 64).
- Iodine can both shut the thyroid down (Wolff-Chaikoff) and switch on a nodule (Jod-Basedow) (El-Husseiny Endocrinology, pp. 74, 78).
- In thyroid storm, give PTU before iodide [MSD Manual: Hyperthyroidism](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/thyroid-disorders/hyperthyroidism) (the reason, that iodide given first can feed new hormone synthesis, is standard teaching).

## Practice questions
1. A newborn girl has ambiguous genitalia, and at 2 weeks she has vomiting, low sodium and high potassium. Which steroid builds up, and why are her genitals virilized?
   - **Answer:** 17-hydroxyprogesterone builds up behind a 21-hydroxylase block, and the precursors are shunted into androgens.
   - **Why:** low cortisol takes away feedback, so ACTH rises; precursors made before the block are shunted to androgens. [MSD Manual: Overview of CAH](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/endocrine-disorders-in-children/overview-of-congenital-adrenal-hyperplasia), [MSD Manual: 21-Hydroxylase Deficiency](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/endocrine-disorders-in-children/congenital-adrenal-hyperplasia-caused-by-21-hydroxylase-deficiency).
   - **Trap:** 11β-hydroxylase deficiency also virilizes, but DOC buildup gives hypertension, not salt wasting.
2. Which enzyme, expressed in the zona glomerulosa but absent from the fasciculata, makes the final step to aldosterone?
   - **Answer:** CYP11B2, aldosterone synthase.
   - **Why:** the glomerulosa has aldosterone synthase and lacks CYP11B1; the fasciculata uses CYP11B1 to make cortisol. [Baranowski 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6067656/)
   - **Trap:** 11β-hydroxylase (CYP11B1) is the cortisol enzyme.
3. A woman 9 weeks pregnant has Graves disease and needs an antithyroid drug. Which drug, and which step does it block?
   - **Answer:** propylthiouracil; it blocks thyroid peroxidase, so iodide is not organified.
   - **Why:** these drugs "block thyroid peroxidase, decreasing the organification of iodide", "and impair the coupling reaction". PTU is preferred in the first trimester because methimazole is linked to rare birth defects. [MSD Manual: Hyperthyroidism](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/thyroid-disorders/hyperthyroidism)
   - **Trap:** radioactive iodine, which is avoided in pregnancy.
4. Free T4 is 0.03% of total T4. If total T4 is 5 µg/dL, how much is free, and which form acts on tissues?
   - **Answer:** 5 µg/dL × 0.0003 = 0.0015 µg/dL, which is 1.5 ng/dL. Only this free fraction acts, mostly after conversion to T3.
   - **Why:** [MSD Manual: Overview of Thyroid Function](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/thyroid-disorders/overview-of-thyroid-function) gives the free fraction and says only free hormone acts on tissues.
   - **Trap:** thinking a high total T4 always means hyperthyroidism. TBG rises in pregnancy and with estrogen or combined oral contraceptives, and falls with illnesses that reduce the liver's protein synthesis, nephrotic syndrome, anabolic steroids and high-dose glucocorticoids. These changes move total T4, but free T4 stays the same. [MSD Manual: Overview of Thyroid Function](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/thyroid-disorders/overview-of-thyroid-function)
