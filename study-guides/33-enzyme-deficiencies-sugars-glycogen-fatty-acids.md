## In one line
Ask **where** the block is and **what piles up**. A liver block (von Gierke, fructose or galactose, MCAD) causes **fasting hypoglycemia**; a muscle block (McArdle, Tarui) causes **cramps with exercise**; a red cell block (G6PD, pyruvate kinase) causes **hemolysis**; a fat-burning block causes **hypoketotic hypoglycemia**.

## Glycogen storage diseases
| Type | Enzyme | Organ | Hallmarks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **I, von Gierke** | **Glucose-6-phosphatase** | Liver, kidney | Severe **fasting hypoglycemia**, **lactic acidosis**, **hyperuricemia** (gout), hyperlipidemia with xanthomas, big liver and kidneys; glucagon and epinephrine do not raise glucose. Treat with frequent glucose or cornstarch; avoid fructose and galactose (El-Husseiny Biochemistry, p. 166) |
| **II, Pompe** | Lysosomal **acid alpha-glucosidase** (acid maltase) | Heart, muscle, liver | Infant with **massive cardiomegaly**, floppy muscles, big tongue; **normal glucose**; glycogen inside lysosomes. "Pompe trashes the Pump" (p. 167). MSD: enzyme replacement with alglucosidase alfa |
| **III, Cori** | **Debranching enzyme** (alpha-1,6-glucosidase) | Liver ± muscle | Milder von Gierke with **normal lactate** (gluconeogenesis works); short outer branches (limit dextrin) build up (p. 167) |
| **IV, Andersen** | **Branching enzyme** | Liver | Long unbranched glycogen; **cirrhosis** and death in early childhood (your table; MSD) |
| **V, McArdle** | **Muscle glycogen phosphorylase** (myophosphorylase) | Muscle | Exercise **cramps**, **myoglobinuria**, **second wind** after 10-15 minutes; **flat venous lactate** with a normal ammonia rise on exercise testing; normal blood glucose (p. 168) |
| VI, Hers | Liver phosphorylase | Liver | Mild hypoglycemia, big liver; benign (your table; MSD) |
| VII, Tarui | Phosphofructokinase-1 in muscle and red cells | Muscle, red cells | Like McArdle **plus hemolysis** (your table; MSD) |

Sources: El-Husseiny Biochemistry, pp. 166-168; your enzyme table; [MSD Manual: Glycogen Storage Diseases](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/inherited-disorders-of-metabolism/glycogen-storage-diseases). Types I, II, III and V are autosomal recessive (p. 166).

- **MSD adds:** type Ib (glucose-6-phosphate translocase) also causes **neutropenia** with recurrent infections; von Gierke later causes **hepatic adenomas**.
- **Recent change (naming):** your table lists "Type VIII: liver phosphorylase kinase, X-linked." MSD says the former type VIII "is now included in IXa" (phosphorylase kinase deficiency, X-linked).

## Galactose and fructose
| Disorder | Enzyme | What builds up | Picture |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Classic galactosemia** | **Galactose-1-phosphate uridyltransferase** | Galactose-1-phosphate, galactitol | Days after milk starts: vomiting, jaundice, big liver, failure to thrive, **cataracts**, kidney tubule damage, **E. coli sepsis**; later cognitive problems and **premature ovarian failure** (El-Husseiny Biochemistry, p. 140; [MSD Manual: Galactosemia](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/inherited-disorders-of-metabolism/galactosemia)) |
| **Galactokinase deficiency** | Galactokinase | Galactitol only | Mild: galactose in blood and urine and **infantile cataracts** (failure to track objects) (p. 139) |
| **Hereditary fructose intolerance** | **Aldolase B** | Fructose-1-phosphate, which traps phosphate | Healthy until fruit, juice or table sugar is introduced; then **vomiting and hypoglycemia** 20-30 minutes later, jaundice, cirrhosis, proximal kidney damage (pp. 143-144; [MSD Manual: Fructose Metabolism Disorders](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/inherited-disorders-of-metabolism/fructose-metabolism-disorders)) |
| **Essential fructosuria** | **Fructokinase** | Fructose in blood and urine | Harmless; found by chance as a non-glucose reducing sugar in urine (p. 143; MSD) |

- **Why galactose gives cataracts and fructose does not:** aldose reductase in the lens turns galactose (an aldose) into **galactitol**, which pulls in water. Fructose is a ketose, so it is not a substrate (El-Husseiny Biochemistry, pp. 139, 143).
- **Why hereditary fructose intolerance causes hypoglycemia:** fructose-1-phosphate piles up and uses up the cell's phosphate, which blocks glycogen breakdown and gluconeogenesis (p. 144).
- **Urine tests:** a dipstick (glucose oxidase) is **negative** for fructose and galactose; a reducing-sugar test (Clinitest) is positive (p. 144).
- Treatment: remove galactose and lactose (galactosemia) or fructose, sucrose and sorbitol (fructose intolerance) (p. 140; MSD). MSD: every US state screens newborns for the uridyltransferase defect.

**Correction (your table's galactosemia rows):** the table labels galactokinase deficiency "classic galactosemia type 2" but gives it the severe classic picture (liver failure, acidosis). El-Husseiny calls galactokinase deficiency "relatively mild" with cataracts as the main problem (p. 139), and MSD says these patients "develop cataracts from overproduction of galactitol." The table also labels the epimerase deficiency "type 2"; it is the **third** form (MSD lists transferase, galactokinase and epimerase). The table says affected infants are "termed lactose intolerant"; galactosemia is a block in galactose metabolism, not lactase deficiency (standard teaching).

## Red cell enzymes
- **G6PD deficiency** (El-Husseiny Biochemistry, pp. 172-173): X-linked recessive; the most common human enzyme deficiency. Red cells get NADPH only from the HMP shunt, and they need it to keep glutathione reduced. Without it, oxidant stress forms **Heinz bodies** (precipitated hemoglobin), which the spleen bites out (**bite cells**: "Bite into some Heinz ketchup"). Triggers: **infection** (most common), fava beans, sulfonamides, nitrofurantoin, primaquine, antituberculosis drugs.
- MSD adds: it affects more than 10% of African American males; dapsone and **rasburicase** are also triggers; and the G6PD assay can be **falsely normal during and right after** a hemolytic episode (young red cells have more enzyme), so repeat it weeks later ([MSD Manual: G6PD Deficiency](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/hematology-and-oncology/anemias-caused-by-hemolysis/glucose-6-phosphate-dehydrogenase-g6pd-deficiency)).
- **Pyruvate kinase deficiency** (p. 135): the second most common enzyme cause of hemolytic anemia; chronic hemolysis, **raised 2,3-BPG** (oxygen curve shifts right) and **no Heinz bodies**.

## Mitochondria and pyruvate
- **Pyruvate dehydrogenase deficiency** (El-Husseiny Biochemistry, p. 147): X-linked; pyruvate is shunted to **lactate** and **alanine**, giving lactic acidosis and neurologic problems from infancy. Treat with a **ketogenic** diet (high fat, low carbohydrate, more lysine and leucine, which are purely ketogenic).
- PDH needs five cofactors: thiamine (B1), lipoic acid, CoA (B5), FAD (B2) and NAD (B3) (p. 147).
- **Mitochondrial DNA diseases** pass **only from the mother** to all her children (pp. 72-73); they hit nerve and muscle and cause lactic acidosis, with **ragged red fibers** on muscle biopsy. Severity varies with **heteroplasmy** (the mix of normal and mutant mitochondria).

**Correction (MELAS inheritance):** your table lists "mitochondrial myopathy, encephalopathy, lactic acidosis and stroke" as autosomal recessive. El-Husseiny lists MELAS among diseases caused by mutations in mitochondrial DNA or in nuclear genes for mitochondrial proteins, and explains that mitochondrial DNA is "inherited exclusively through females" (pp. 72-73). For exams, MELAS is the classic **maternally inherited** (mitochondrial DNA) disease (standard teaching).

## Fatty acid oxidation
| Defect | Key point |
| --- | --- |
| **Carnitine shuttle** (primary carnitine deficiency, CPT-I) | Long-chain fatty acids cannot enter mitochondria → muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy and **hypoketotic hypoglycemia** (El-Husseiny Biochemistry, p. 192). MSD: primary carnitine deficiency is treated with L-carnitine |
| **CPT-II** | Your table: muscle weakness, necrosis and **myoglobinuria**. MSD: the classic form appears in adults as muscle pain and myoglobinuria after exercise or fasting |
| **MCAD deficiency** | The most common beta-oxidation defect; after fasting (as little as 12 hours) or illness from age 2-3 months: vomiting, lethargy, seizures, coma, even sudden infant death; **hypoketotic hypoglycemia** with raised C8-C10 acylcarnitines; in US newborn screening; prevent with frequent high-carbohydrate meals and no fasting (p. 192; [MSD Manual: Beta-Oxidation Cycle Disorders](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/inherited-disorders-of-metabolism/beta-oxidation-cycle-disorders)) |
| **LCHAD deficiency** | The second most common; adds cardiomyopathy, rhabdomyolysis, neuropathy. Your table links it to **acute fatty liver of pregnancy**; MSD notes the mother of an affected fetus often has **HELLP** syndrome |

- **Why "hypoketotic":** no beta-oxidation means no acetyl-CoA for ketones, and low acetyl-CoA also fails to switch on pyruvate carboxylase for gluconeogenesis (El-Husseiny Biochemistry, p. 192).

**Correction (Jamaican vomiting sickness):** your table lists it as "Autosomal Recessive." It is a **poisoning**, not an inherited disease: eating unripe ackee fruit, whose toxin hypoglycin A blocks fatty acid transport and oxidation, so hypoglycemia sets in once glycogen runs out ([Katibi et al., Am J Trop Med Hyg 2015;93:1122-1123](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4703256)).

## Clinical vignette
A 4-month-old has a big liver, a doll-like face and a fasting glucose of 32 mg/dL. Lactate, uric acid and triglycerides are high. Glucagon does not raise the glucose. Which enzyme is missing?

**Answer:** glucose-6-phosphatase (von Gierke disease, type I).
**Why:** the liver can make glucose-6-phosphate from glycogen or gluconeogenesis but cannot release free glucose, so fasting hypoglycemia, lactic acidosis, hyperuricemia and hyperlipidemia follow, and glucagon cannot help (El-Husseiny Biochemistry, p. 166; [MSD Manual: Glycogen Storage Diseases](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/inherited-disorders-of-metabolism/glycogen-storage-diseases)).
**Trap:** Cori disease (debranching enzyme) has **normal lactate**.

## Common traps
- Hypoglycemia soon **after** fruit, juice or table sugar = hereditary fructose intolerance; hypoglycemia **without** ketones after **fasting** = fatty acid oxidation defect (MCAD).
- Cataracts = galactose pathway (galactitol), never fructose.
- Pompe: heart and muscle, normal glucose, lysosomal enzyme.
- McArdle: no lactate rise with exercise; second wind.
- G6PD test during hemolysis can look normal.
- Pyruvate kinase deficiency: hemolysis without Heinz bodies.
- MELAS is maternal; Jamaican vomiting sickness is a toxin.

## Practice questions
1. A 6-month-old started on fruit juice has vomiting, sweating and a glucose of 38 mg/dL 30 minutes after drinking. Urine dipstick is negative for glucose, but a reducing-sugar test is positive. Which enzyme?
   - **Answer:** aldolase B (hereditary fructose intolerance).
   - **Why:** fructose-1-phosphate accumulates and depletes phosphate, blocking glycogen breakdown and gluconeogenesis; the dipstick only detects glucose (El-Husseiny Biochemistry, p. 144; [MSD Manual: Fructose Metabolism Disorders](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/inherited-disorders-of-metabolism/fructose-metabolism-disorders)).
   - **Trap:** fructokinase deficiency is harmless.
2. A 20-year-old has painful cramps and dark urine after sprinting, which ease if he keeps going slowly. Exercise testing shows no rise in venous lactate. Which enzyme?
   - **Answer:** muscle glycogen phosphorylase (McArdle disease).
   - **Why:** muscle cannot break down its own glycogen, so lactate does not rise; extra blood flow brings glucose after 10-15 minutes (second wind) (El-Husseiny Biochemistry, p. 168).
   - **Trap:** Tarui disease looks similar but adds hemolysis.
3. A 9-month-old has vomiting and lethargy after 14 hours without food during a stomach bug. Glucose is 35 mg/dL and urine ketones are absent. Plasma C8 acylcarnitine is high. Diagnosis and prevention?
   - **Answer:** MCAD deficiency; avoid fasting with frequent high-carbohydrate meals.
   - **Why:** without medium-chain beta-oxidation the liver cannot make ketones or fuel gluconeogenesis (p. 192; [MSD Manual: Beta-Oxidation Cycle Disorders](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/inherited-disorders-of-metabolism/beta-oxidation-cycle-disorders)).
   - **Trap:** hypoglycemia **with** ketones points away from a fat-burning defect (standard teaching).
4. A Black man develops dark urine and anemia 2 days after starting primaquine. A G6PD assay done that day is normal. Next step?
   - **Answer:** repeat the G6PD assay several weeks later.
   - **Why:** old, enzyme-poor cells are destroyed first, and the surviving young cells have more enzyme, so the test can be falsely normal ([MSD Manual: G6PD Deficiency](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/hematology-and-oncology/anemias-caused-by-hemolysis/glucose-6-phosphate-dehydrogenase-g6pd-deficiency); the young-cell explanation is standard teaching).
   - **Trap:** ruling out G6PD deficiency on that first result.
