# Multiple endocrine neoplasia (MEN) 1, 2A and 2B

Exam focus: USMLE Step 1 and NBME · Study material, not patient care guidance · Auditor: cleared 2026-10-27
From Elissa's question log: "diagnostic criteria and features distinguishing MEN type 1, 2A, and 2B".
Based on your notes: El-Husseiny's Essentials of Endocrinology (MEN syndromes pp. 121-122). Each fact is also checked against StatPearls and the MSD Manual; where a document is wrong, a marked note says so.

## In one line
MEN1 is the "3 Ps" (parathyroid, pituitary, pancreas) from a lost tumor suppressor (El-Husseiny Endocrinology, p. 121); MEN2 is a RET gain of function: medullary thyroid cancer in nearly all, pheochromocytoma in up to half, plus parathyroid disease (2A) or mucosal neuromas and a marfanoid build (2B).

## Core mechanism
1. All MEN syndromes are **autosomal dominant**. [StatPearls: MEN1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK536980/), [StatPearls: MEN2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK519054/)
2. **MEN1:** inactivating germline mutations in the *MEN1* (menin) gene on chromosome 11. Losing menin lets endocrine cells in several glands grow into tumors. [StatPearls: MEN1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK536980/)
3. **MEN2:** gain-of-function mutations in the *RET* proto-oncogene (a receptor tyrosine kinase) switch growth signaling on in neural crest-derived cells, such as thyroid C cells and adrenal medulla. [StatPearls: MEN2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK519054/)

4. **Why MEN2 hits the thyroid and the adrenal together (your notes, p. 122):** the germline *RET* mutation is in every cell, and the thyroid C cells and the adrenal medulla both come from **neural crest**, so one inherited defect gives medullary thyroid cancer and pheochromocytoma. The C cells reach the thyroid from the 4th pharyngeal pouch region; the adrenal cortex comes from mesoderm instead.
- **Correction:** p. 122 says neural crest cells "differentiate into ... the four pharyngeal pouches". The pouches are lined by **endoderm**, as your own notes say on p. 2 ("Pouches = endoderm"); neural crest cells migrate into the pharyngeal **arches**, not the pouches (standard teaching).

## Your notes' version (El-Husseiny Endocrinology, p. 121)
| | MEN1 = 3 Ps | MEN2A = 2 Ps | MEN2B = 1 P |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Parts | Parathyroid tumors; pituitary tumors (prolactin or growth hormone); pancreatic endocrine tumors (Zollinger-Ellison syndrome, insulinoma, VIPoma, rarely glucagonoma) | Parathyroid hyperplasia, pheochromocytoma, medullary thyroid cancer | Pheochromocytoma, medullary thyroid cancer, mucosal neuromas (skin-colored bumps on lips and tongue made of thickened nerve tissue), marfanoid habitus (tall, thin, long limbs and fingers) |
| Gene | *MEN1* (menin, a tumor suppressor) | *RET* (receptor tyrosine kinase) | *RET* |

The "Ps" count only the P-named glands: MEN2A's medullary thyroid cancer and MEN2B's thyroid cancer are present in both even though the mnemonic does not name them.

## Compare and contrast

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gene | *MEN1* (menin), inactivating | *RET*, gain of function | *RET*, gain of function |
| Parathyroid | Hyperparathyroidism in 95% or more; usually the first sign | Hyperparathyroidism in about 10-25% (MSD: 10-20%), often mild | Not a feature (under 1%) |
| Pituitary | Adenoma in about 40% (most often prolactin) | — | — |
| Pancreas / duodenum | Neuroendocrine tumors in about 60% (gastrinoma most common functioning; insulinoma 10-30%) | — | — |
| Medullary thyroid cancer (MTC) | — | Nearly 100% | Nearly 100% |
| Pheochromocytoma | — | Up to 50% | Up to 50% |

## High-yield facts
- MEN1 clinical diagnosis: 2 of the 3 main tumor types, or 1 tumor plus a first-degree relative with MEN1, or a *MEN1* mutation. [StatPearls: MEN1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK536980/)
- About 20-25% of patients with gastrinoma have MEN1 (up to a third in some series; standard teaching), so recurrent peptic ulcers plus high calcium should make you think MEN1. [StatPearls: MEN1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK536980/)
- MTC arises from parafollicular C cells and secretes calcitonin. [StatPearls: MEN2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK519054/)
- In *RET* carriers, prophylactic thyroidectomy is the goal; its timing depends on the specific mutation. [StatPearls: MEN2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK519054/)
- Always rule out pheochromocytoma (plasma free metanephrines) before thyroid or any other surgery in MEN2, even without symptoms. [StatPearls: MEN2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK519054/)

## Clinical vignette
A 32-year-old man has kidney stones, recurrent duodenal ulcers despite treatment, and milky nipple discharge. Calcium is high and gastrin is markedly elevated. Which gene is mutated?

**Answer:** *MEN1* (menin), MEN type 1.
**Why:** hyperparathyroidism (stones, high calcium), gastrinoma (refractory ulcers, high gastrin) and a prolactinoma (galactorrhea) are the 3 Ps. [StatPearls: MEN1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK536980/)
**Trap:** choosing *RET*; MEN2 never involves the pituitary or pancreas.

## Common traps
- MEN1 gene = tumor suppressor (loss of function; El-Husseiny Endocrinology, p. 121); RET = proto-oncogene (gain of function).
- The mnemonic counts Ps, not tumors: medullary thyroid cancer is in **both** MEN2 types even though it is not a P (El-Husseiny Endocrinology, p. 121).
- Parathyroid: MEN1 (common, first sign) and MEN2A (about 10-25%), not MEN2B.
- Marfanoid habitus and bumpy lips or tongue = MEN2B.
- Check for pheochromocytoma before any operation in MEN2, or the patient can have a hypertensive crisis on the table.

## Practice questions
1. A 20-year-old with a tall, thin build, nodules on the tongue and lips, and a thyroid nodule has a high calcitonin. Diagnosis?
   - **Answer:** MEN2B with medullary thyroid carcinoma.
   - **Why:** mucosal neuromas and marfanoid habitus are MEN2B markers; calcitonin comes from C-cell MTC. [StatPearls: MEN2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK519054/), [MSD Manual: MEN 2B](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/oncology/multiple-endocrine-neoplasia-men-syndromes/multiple-endocrine-neoplasia-type-2b-men-2b)
   - **Trap:** Marfan syndrome itself does not cause neuromas or MTC.
2. A patient with MEN2A is scheduled for thyroidectomy. Which test must come first?
   - **Answer:** plasma free metanephrines to exclude pheochromocytoma.
   - **Why:** up to 50% develop pheochromocytoma; surgery with an unrecognized one is dangerous. [StatPearls: MEN2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK519054/); MSD gives 40-50% and advises removing the pheochromocytoma first: [MSD Manual: MEN 2A](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/multiple-endocrine-neoplasia-men-syndromes/multiple-endocrine-neoplasia-type-2a-men-2a)
   - **Trap:** checking only calcitonin.
3. What is usually the first manifestation of MEN1?
   - **Answer:** primary hyperparathyroidism.
   - **Why:** it occurs in 95% or more and is usually the first sign; peak onset is in the 20s to 40s, and MSD advises considering MEN1 in anyone with hyperparathyroidism before age 30 ([MSD Manual: MEN 1](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/multiple-endocrine-neoplasia-men-syndromes/multiple-endocrine-neoplasia-type-1-men-1)). [StatPearls: MEN1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK536980/)
   - **Trap:** gastrinoma is the most common *functioning* pancreatic tumor, not the first sign overall.
4. Which MEN type has medullary thyroid cancer, pheochromocytoma and parathyroid hyperplasia?
   - **Answer:** MEN2A.
   - **Why:** MEN2A combines MTC (nearly 100%), pheochromocytoma (up to 50%) and hyperparathyroidism (about 10-25%). [StatPearls: MEN2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK519054/), [MSD Manual: MEN 2A](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/multiple-endocrine-neoplasia-men-syndromes/multiple-endocrine-neoplasia-type-2a-men-2a)
   - **Trap:** MEN2B lacks the parathyroid component.
