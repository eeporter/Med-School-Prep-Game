# Neural tube defects and brain malformations

## In one line
The neural tube closes in week 4. If the **top** end (rostral neuropore) stays open you get anencephaly; if the **bottom** end (caudal neuropore) stays open you get open spina bifida. **Open** defects leak alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) **and** acetylcholinesterase (AChE) into amniotic fluid, and folate before conception prevents most of them.

## Core mechanism
1. The **notochord** (mesoderm) releases **sonic hedgehog**, which makes the overlying ectoderm thicken into the neural plate. The plate folds into a groove, and the folds fuse into the **neural tube**. Cells at the fold edges become the **neural crest**. The notochord later becomes the nucleus pulposus (El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 3).
2. What each part becomes (El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 3):
   - **Neural tube:** CNS neurons, ependymal cells, oligodendrocytes, astrocytes.
   - **Neural crest:** PNS neurons, Schwann cells, melanocytes, adrenal medulla.
   - **Mesoderm:** microglia.
3. Timing in your notes: the middle of the tube closes on **day 23**, the rostral neuropore on **day 25**, the caudal neuropore on **day 27** (El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 3). So NTDs arise in **week 4**, often before a pregnancy is known, which is why folate has to start before conception (standard teaching).
4. Once the tube closes, it induces the vertebral arches. Spina bifida is either a failure to form the arches (occulta, cystica) or a failure of the tube itself to close (myeloschisis) (El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 6).
5. Risk factors: **folate deficiency**, **maternal diabetes** and **valproate** (El-Husseiny Neurology, pp. 3, 8; [MSD Manual: Spina Bifida](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/congenital-neurologic-anomalies/spina-bifida)). Your Osmosis notes add other anticonvulsants and obesity (Osmosis Neurology, p. 50).

## Compare: types of spina bifida
| Type | What sticks out | Skin covering | AFP | AChE | Key feature |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Spina bifida occulta | Nothing; only the spinous process is missing | Normal skin, often a **tuft of hair** or dimple, lumbosacral | Normal | Normal | Mildest; usually no symptoms; missed on prenatal ultrasound (El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 7) |
| Meningocele | Sac of meninges and CSF only | Variable | Raised | See note below | Usually no nerve damage (standard teaching) |
| Myelomeningocele | Meninges **plus spinal cord** | Usually **exposed** (MSD: "the meninges are usually exposed or, rarely, are covered by a thin layer of skin") | Raised | Raised when open | Leg weakness and sensory loss below the lesion, neurogenic bladder; goes with **Chiari II** and hydrocephalus |
| Myeloschisis (myelocele) | Flat, exposed neural tissue, no covering | None | Raised | Raised | Rarest and most severe (El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 8) |

Sources: El-Husseiny Neurology, pp. 6-8; Osmosis Neurology, pp. 50-51; [MSD Manual: Spina Bifida](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/congenital-neurologic-anomalies/spina-bifida)

**Correction (AChE in myelomeningocele):** your notes say a meningomyelocele raises AFP but **not** AChE "because this is not an open neural tube defect" (El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 7). MSD says that in myelomeningocele "the meninges are usually exposed", which makes it an open defect, and MSD's screening page says "Elevated alpha-fetoprotein plus presence of acetylcholinesterase in amniotic fluid is nearly 100% sensitive for anencephaly and 90 to 96% sensitive for open spina bifida" ([MSD Manual: Noninvasive Prenatal Fetal Screening Tests](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/gynecology-and-obstetrics/prenatal-genetic-counseling-and-evaluation/noninvasive-prenatal-fetal-screening-tests)). So for exams: an open myelomeningocele raises **both** AFP and AChE; only a fully skin-covered (closed) defect leaves AChE normal (standard teaching).

## AFP vs AChE (your notes, El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 8)
- Any **open** NTD (anencephaly or open spina bifida) raises AFP and AChE in amniotic fluid or maternal serum.
- **AFP is not specific**: it also rises with ventral body wall defects (omphalocele, gastroschisis).
- **AChE is the specific one** for open neural tube defects. MSD: "An elevated MSAFP level suggests open spina bifida, anencephaly, or abdominal wall defects" ([MSD Manual: Noninvasive Prenatal Fetal Screening Tests](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/gynecology-and-obstetrics/prenatal-genetic-counseling-and-evaluation/noninvasive-prenatal-fetal-screening-tests)), so a high maternal serum AFP (MSAFP) alone does not tell them apart.
- Prenatal screening = fetal ultrasound plus maternal serum AFP ([MSD Manual: Spina Bifida](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/congenital-neurologic-anomalies/spina-bifida)).

## Brain malformations
| Malformation | What went wrong | Clues |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Anencephaly** | Rostral neuropore fails to close; no forebrain, open skull | Raised AFP and AChE; **polyhydramnios** because the fetus cannot swallow (El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 4). Fatal: "Infants are stillborn or die within hours or days" ([MSD Manual: Anencephaly](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/congenital-neurologic-anomalies/anencephaly)) |
| **Holoprosencephaly** | The forebrain (prosencephalon) does not split into two hemispheres | Sonic hedgehog mutations; midline face defects from cleft lip to **cyclopia**; trisomy 13 (Patau) and severe fetal alcohol syndrome; MRI shows one ventricle (El-Husseiny Neurology, pp. 4-5; [MSD Manual: Malformed Cerebral Hemispheres](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/congenital-neurologic-anomalies/malformed-cerebral-hemispheres)) |
| **Lissencephaly** | Neurons fail to migrate: "smooth brain" without gyri | Intellectual disability and seizures, often infantile spasms (El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 5; [MSD Manual: Malformed Cerebral Hemispheres](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/congenital-neurologic-anomalies/malformed-cerebral-hemispheres)) |
| **Chiari I** | Cerebellar **tonsils** (1 structure) down through the foramen magnum | Most common type; usually silent in childhood, then headaches and cerebellar signs in adults; goes with **syringomyelia** (El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 8) |
| **Chiari II** | Cerebellar **vermis and tonsils** (2 structures) down | Lumbosacral **myelomeningocele**; aqueductal stenosis and noncommunicating hydrocephalus; presents early (El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 9). MSD: "Hydrocephalus is common because many children with spina bifida have a Chiari II type malformation" ([MSD Manual: Spina Bifida](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/congenital-neurologic-anomalies/spina-bifida)) |
| **Dandy-Walker** | Absent or small cerebellar **vermis** with a **cystic, enlarged 4th ventricle** filling a big posterior fossa | Foramina of Luschka and Magendie fail to open, so noncommunicating hydrocephalus (El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 9). Mnemonic: Dilated 4th ventricle, Water on the brain, Small vermis (Osmosis Neurology, p. 45) |

## High-yield facts
- **Folate prevents NTDs.** MSD: 400 to 800 mcg daily for women who could become pregnant; **4 mg** daily after a previously affected pregnancy ([MSD Manual: Spina Bifida](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/congenital-neurologic-anomalies/spina-bifida)). Your notes: folate in early pregnancy lowers the rate of NTDs (El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 8).
- **Valproate** is the antiseizure drug most linked to NTDs (standard teaching). Your notes explain it as blocking intestinal folate absorption (El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 8); the exact mechanism is debated (standard teaching). Methotrexate and other folate antagonists also harm the dividing neural tube cells (p. 8).
- **Latex allergy** is common in children with spina bifida, which matters for surgery (Osmosis Neurology, p. 50).
- Spina bifida complications: neurogenic bladder and bowel, tethered cord, hydrocephalus, clubfoot, scoliosis, hip dysplasia (Osmosis Neurology, p. 50).
- The spinal cord ends at the lower border of **L1** in adults (El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 12).

## Clinical vignette
A 26-year-old woman with epilepsy on valproate has a second-trimester scan showing a lumbosacral sac containing neural tissue. The fetal head shows a small posterior fossa and enlarged lateral ventricles. Amniotic fluid AFP and AChE are both high. What is the defect, and what brain malformation goes with it?

**Answer:** myelomeningocele (open spina bifida) with a Chiari II malformation.
**Why:** the sac holds neural tissue, and both AFP and AChE are raised, so it is an open defect. Chiari II pulls the vermis and tonsils down, blocks CSF outflow and causes hydrocephalus (El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 9; [MSD Manual: Spina Bifida](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/congenital-neurologic-anomalies/spina-bifida)). Valproate is a known risk factor (MSD).
**Trap:** calling it a meningocele; a meningocele holds only meninges and CSF.

## Common traps
- Rostral = anencephaly; caudal = spina bifida.
- AFP is high in body wall defects too; **AChE** points to an open NTD.
- Spina bifida occulta: normal AFP, tuft of hair, no symptoms.
- Chiari I = tonsils, adults, syringomyelia. Chiari II = vermis + tonsils, babies, myelomeningocele, hydrocephalus.
- Dandy-Walker = missing vermis + big cystic 4th ventricle; Chiari II = small posterior fossa with herniation.
- Holoprosencephaly goes with **trisomy 13** and sonic hedgehog; it shows on the face (cleft lip to cyclopia).

## Practice questions
1. A newborn has a tuft of hair over L5 and a normal neurologic exam. Maternal AFP was normal. Diagnosis?
   - **Answer:** spina bifida occulta.
   - **Why:** the arch failed to form, but the defect is covered by normal skin, so AFP is normal and there are no deficits (El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 7).
   - **Trap:** meningocele would show a sac and raised AFP.
2. A fetus has absent cerebral hemispheres and the mother has polyhydramnios. Which neuropore failed to close, and why the extra fluid?
   - **Answer:** the rostral (cranial) neuropore; the fetus cannot swallow amniotic fluid.
   - **Why:** anencephaly leaves no forebrain and no swallowing reflex (El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 4; [MSD Manual: Anencephaly](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/congenital-neurologic-anomalies/anencephaly)).
   - **Trap:** the caudal neuropore gives spina bifida.
3. At 18 weeks, maternal serum AFP is high and amniotic AChE is normal. Ultrasound shows a normal spine and skull, and loops of bowel floating free in the amniotic fluid beside a normally inserted umbilical cord. Which defect fits best?
   - **Answer:** a ventral body wall defect (gastroschisis).
   - **Why:** MSD: an elevated MSAFP "suggests open spina bifida, anencephaly, or abdominal wall defects" ([MSD Manual: Noninvasive Prenatal Fetal Screening Tests](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/gynecology-and-obstetrics/prenatal-genetic-counseling-and-evaluation/noninvasive-prenatal-fetal-screening-tests)); AChE is specific for open neural tube defects (El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 8), and the scan shows the wall defect. Bowel with no covering sac beside a normal cord insertion is gastroschisis (standard teaching).
   - **Trap:** choosing an open NTD, which would raise AChE.
4. A woman whose first baby had spina bifida plans another pregnancy. What folate dose?
   - **Answer:** 4 mg (4,000 mcg) daily, which is 10 times the usual 400 mcg (4,000 / 400 = 10).
   - **Why:** MSD recommends 4 mg daily after a previously affected pregnancy and 400-800 mcg for others ([MSD Manual: Spina Bifida](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/congenital-neurologic-anomalies/spina-bifida)).
   - **Trap:** giving the routine 400 mcg dose.
5. An infant has a big head, a small cerebellar vermis and a cyst-like 4th ventricle filling a large posterior fossa. Diagnosis?
   - **Answer:** Dandy-Walker malformation.
   - **Why:** vermis agenesis with cystic dilation of the 4th ventricle; the foramina of Luschka and Magendie fail to open, causing hydrocephalus (El-Husseiny Neurology, p. 9; [MSD Manual: Congenital Hydrocephalus](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/congenital-neurologic-anomalies/congenital-hydrocephalus)).
   - **Trap:** Chiari II has a **small** posterior fossa ("insufficient posterior fossa growth", Osmosis Neurology, p. 42).
