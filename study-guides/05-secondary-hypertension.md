# Secondary hypertension: young vs elderly

Exam focus: USMLE Step 1 and NBME · Study material, not patient care guidance · Auditor: cleared 2026-10-27
From the question log: "differential diagnosis and diagnostic workup for secondary hypertension in young versus elderly patients".
Based on your notes: El-Husseiny's Essentials of Cardiovascular (hypertension pp. 116-122) and Endocrinology (hyperaldosteronism pp. 52-54, pheochromocytoma pp. 59-60), and Osmosis HY Cardiovascular ("Hypertension"). Each fact is also checked against StatPearls and the MSD Manual; where a different source disagrees with your notes, or newer guidance has changed, a marked note says so.

## In one line
Suspect a secondary cause when hypertension is early, sudden, severe, resistant to 3 drugs, or comes with low potassium; then let the patient's age and one clue (potassium, episodes, arm-leg difference, bruit) pick the cause.

## Core mechanism
Most secondary causes raise blood pressure through one of four routes:
1. **Too much renin-angiotensin**: renal artery stenosis makes the kidney think it is underperfused. Renin high, aldosterone high. [StatPearls: Renovascular Hypertension](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK551587/)
2. **Too much aldosterone on its own**: primary aldosteronism retains sodium and wastes potassium. Aldosterone high, renin **suppressed**. [StatPearls: Hyperaldosteronism](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK499983/), [MSD Manual: Primary Aldosteronism](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/adrenal-disorders/primary-aldosteronism)
3. **Too much catecholamine or cortisol or thyroid hormone**: pheochromocytoma, Cushing syndrome, hyperthyroidism. [StatPearls: Secondary Hypertension](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK544305/)
4. **Structural, renal and other**: coarctation of the aorta, kidney disease; sleep apnea (sympathetic activation from repeated low oxygen at night) and drugs. [StatPearls: Secondary Hypertension](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK544305/)

## Your notes' framework
- Your notes define hypertension as a persistent systolic pressure of **130** or more and/or diastolic of **80** or more. About **90%** is primary (essential); secondary hypertension has an identifiable cause and makes up under 5% of cases (El-Husseiny Cardiovascular, p. 119).
- Causes listed in your notes: renal artery stenosis, primary hyperaldosteronism (Conn syndrome), pheochromocytoma, Cushing disease, coarctation of the aorta, oral contraceptives, acromegaly, congenital adrenal enzyme defects and almost any chronic kidney disease (El-Husseiny Cardiovascular, p. 119).
- Osmosis mnemonic **RHNECCK**: **R**enovascular, primary **H**yperaldosteronism, **N**SAIDs, pre-**E**clampsia/eclampsia, **C**oarctation, **C**ushing syndrome, chronic **K**idney disease. NSAIDs block renal prostaglandin E2, so the afferent arteriole constricts; coarctation lowers pressure to the kidneys and switches on renin (Osmosis HY Cardiovascular, "Hypertension").
- **Recent change (definition):** Osmosis defines hypertension as above 140/90. The 2025 AHA/ACC guideline (published 14 August 2025) keeps the lower cutoffs your El-Husseiny notes use: normal below 120/80; elevated 120-129 systolic with diastolic below 80; stage 1 130-139 or 80-89; stage 2 140 or more, or 90 or more. [AHA: Top Things to Know, 2025 High Blood Pressure Guideline](https://professional.heart.org/en/science-news/2025-high-blood-pressure-guideline/top-things-to-know) Pregnancy has its own cutoffs.
- **Severe hypertension in your notes:** urgency is 180/120 or more without acute organ damage; emergency is severe hypertension **with** acute organ damage (encephalopathy, stroke, retinal bleeding or papilledema, MI, heart failure, aortic dissection, kidney injury, microangiopathic hemolytic anemia, eclampsia) (El-Husseiny Cardiovascular, p. 122).

## When to suspect a secondary cause
- Resistant hypertension: above goal on 3 drugs of different classes, one a diuretic (definition is standard teaching). MSD recommends screening these patients for primary aldosteronism. [MSD Manual: Hypertension](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/cardiovascular-disorders/hypertension/hypertension)
- Onset before age 40 without risk factors, or a sudden worsening of previously controlled pressure (standard teaching).
- Severe hypertension (MSD defines it as 180/120 mmHg or higher). MSD: "The more severe the hypertension and the younger the patient, the more extensive is the evaluation." [MSD Manual: Hypertension](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/cardiovascular-disorders/hypertension/hypertension)
- Low potassium not explained by a diuretic (think mineralocorticoid excess); MSD says these patients are evaluated for primary aldosteronism. [MSD Manual: Hypertension](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/cardiovascular-disorders/hypertension/hypertension) Metabolic alkalosis as a clue is standard teaching.
- **Recent change:** the 2025 Endocrine Society guideline suggests that everyone diagnosed with hypertension have aldosterone, renin and potassium checked, not just patients with resistant hypertension or low potassium. It estimates primary aldosteronism in 5% to 14% of people with high blood pressure in primary care and up to 30% in referral centers (not core Step 1). [Endocrine Society press release, 14 July 2025](https://www.endocrine.org/news-and-advocacy/news-room/2025/endocrine-society-guideline-calls-for-increased-screening-for-common-cause-of-high-blood-pressure)

[StatPearls: Secondary Hypertension](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK544305/)

## Most likely cause by age
| Age group | Leading secondary causes |
| --- | --- |
| Children under 12 | Kidney (renal parenchymal) disease; 70-85% of childhood hypertension is secondary |
| Young adults | Primary aldosteronism (the most common identifiable cause in adults; [MSD Manual: Hypertension](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/cardiovascular-disorders/hypertension/hypertension); your notes give renal artery stenosis instead, see below), renovascular disease (fibromuscular dysplasia in young women), kidney disease, drugs, pheochromocytoma; coarctation of the aorta |
| Over 65 | Atherosclerotic renal artery stenosis, primary aldosteronism, chronic kidney disease |

MSD names primary aldosteronism as the most common identifiable cause, and the 2025 Endocrine Society guideline calls it "a common hormonal cause of high blood pressure" found in 5-14% of patients in primary care. | Low potassium, metabolic alkalosis (but potassium is normal in most patients) | Primary aldosteronism | Aldosterone-to-renin ratio (ARR) above 20 (aldosterone ng/dL ÷ plasma renin activity ng/mL/h) with aldosterone above 15 ng/dL; cutoffs depend on the assay |
| Creatinine jumps after ACE inhibitor or ARB, abdominal bruit, flash pulmonary edema, unequal kidney sizes | Renal artery stenosis | Renal artery duplex ultrasound |

## Hypertension + low potassium: use renin and aldosterone
| Renin | Aldosterone | Think of |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low | **High** | Primary aldosteronism (the adrenal makes aldosterone on its own) |
| High | High | Secondary aldosteronism, e.g. renal artery stenosis, renin-secreting tumor |
| Low | **Low** | A non-aldosterone mineralocorticoid effect: **Liddle syndrome** (overactive ENaC sodium channel in the collecting duct; treat with amiloride, spironolactone does not work), **licorice** (pseudoaldosteronism), **Cushing syndrome**, **11β-hydroxylase CAH** (deoxycorticosterone excess). Also apparent mineralocorticoid excess (11β-HSD2 loss, which licorice mimics) and 17α-hydroxylase CAH (standard teaching). |

MSD: "Low levels of both plasma renin activity and aldosterone suggest nonaldosterone mineralocorticoid excess." ## Your notes on the main causes
- **Primary hyperaldosteronism (Conn syndrome):** the adrenal makes aldosterone on its own, so renin is low. Aldosterone saves sodium and dumps potassium and hydrogen ions in the distal tubule, giving hypertension, hypokalemia (weakness, paresthesias) and metabolic alkalosis. The usual electrolyte pattern asked is **normal sodium, low potassium, high bicarbonate**. Most patients have **no edema** because of "aldosterone escape": more volume raises GFR and releases atrial natriuretic peptide, which pushes sodium back out. Treatment: remove the tumor, or spironolactone (El-Husseiny Endocrinology, pp. 52-53).
- **Secondary hyperaldosteronism:** the kidney senses low volume and drives renin up: renovascular hypertension, renin-secreting juxtaglomerular cell tumors, and edematous states (cirrhosis, heart failure, nephrotic syndrome). Aldosterone signs **plus high renin** = secondary; **plus low renin** = primary (El-Husseiny Endocrinology, p. 54).
- **Pheochromocytoma:** episodic hypertension with headache, sweating and palpitations; the **rule of 10s** (10% malignant, bilateral, extra-adrenal, calcified, in children); raised catecholamines and metanephrines in urine and plasma. Treat with phenoxybenzamine (irreversible alpha blocker) **before** a beta blocker, then surgery; a beta blocker first leaves alpha effects unopposed and can cause a hypertensive crisis (El-Husseiny Endocrinology, pp. 59-60).
- **Coarctation of the aorta (adult, postductal type):** high arm pressure, low leg pressure with weak, delayed femoral pulses; headaches and nosebleeds; notching of ribs 3-8 on chest X-ray from enlarged intercostal collaterals. The infantile (preductal) type goes with a patent ductus and **Turner syndrome**. Adults with coarctation are at risk of heart failure, aortic dissection and brain bleeds, including ruptured berry aneurysms (El-Husseiny Cardiovascular, pp. 116-117).
- **Renal artery stenosis:** atherosclerosis in older male smokers, fibromuscular dysplasia ("string of beads") in young or middle-aged women; resistant hypertension, unequal kidney sizes and a flank or epigastric bruit (El-Husseiny Cardiovascular, pp. 120-121). See [03-ace-inhibitors-renal-artery-stenosis.md](03-ace-inhibitors-renal-artery-stenosis.md).

## High-yield facts
- Primary aldosteronism: about 60% bilateral adrenal hyperplasia, about 30% a single adrenal adenoma (Conn syndrome), per [StatPearls: Hyperaldosteronism](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK499983/). Your notes name both a unilateral adenoma and bilateral hyperplasia as the main causes without ranking them (El-Husseiny Endocrinology, p. 52). Sources differ here: older sources and MSD list a Conn adenoma as the most common cause and bilateral hyperplasia as less common [MSD Manual: Primary Aldosteronism](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/adrenal-disorders/primary-aldosteronism). The 60/30 split is the usual Step 1 answer (standard teaching).
- Renin tells primary from secondary aldosteronism: low renin = primary (the adrenal is acting on its own); high renin = secondary (for example renal artery stenosis). [StatPearls: Hyperaldosteronism](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK499983/), [MSD Manual: Primary Aldosteronism](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/adrenal-disorders/primary-aldosteronism)
- Low potassium is classic for primary aldosteronism but present in only a minority, so a normal potassium does not rule it out. [StatPearls: Secondary Hypertension](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK544305/)
- Fibromuscular dysplasia (young women) vs atherosclerosis (older adults) is the age split for renovascular hypertension. See [03-ace-inhibitors-renal-artery-stenosis.md](03-ace-inhibitors-renal-artery-stenosis.md).

## Clinical vignette
A 34-year-old woman has blood pressure 168/102 mmHg despite three drugs including a diuretic. Potassium is 3.1 mEq/L and bicarbonate 31 mEq/L. Plasma renin is low and aldosterone is high. What is the most likely cause?

**Answer:** primary aldosteronism (most often bilateral adrenal hyperplasia, next a Conn adenoma).
**Why:** high aldosterone with suppressed renin means the adrenal is secreting on its own. Aldosterone retains sodium (hypertension) and wastes potassium and hydrogen ions (hypokalemia, metabolic alkalosis). [StatPearls: Hyperaldosteronism](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK499983/), [MSD Manual: Primary Aldosteronism](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/adrenal-disorders/primary-aldosteronism)
**Trap:** renal artery stenosis also causes high aldosterone, but renin is **high**.

## Common traps
- "Most common cause of secondary hypertension" depends on age: kidney disease in children, aldosteronism and renovascular disease in adults.
- Edema is uncommon in primary aldosteronism, even with sodium retention. [MSD Manual: Primary Aldosteronism](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/adrenal-disorders/primary-aldosteronism)
- Hypertension + low potassium does **not** always mean primary aldosteronism. Check renin **and** aldosterone (see the box above).
- Pheochromocytoma screening uses metanephrines, not a single catecholamine level.
- In pheochromocytoma, the alpha blocker goes **first**, then the beta blocker (El-Husseiny Endocrinology, p. 60).
- Hypertension with no edema does not argue against aldosteronism; aldosterone escape limits the volume gain (El-Husseiny Endocrinology, p. 52).
- Coarctation: compare arm and leg pressure. Pressure is high in the arms and low in the legs.

## Practice questions
1. A 19-year-old man has arm pressure 160/95 mmHg, weak femoral pulses, and a systolic murmur heard over his back. Diagnosis?
   - **Answer:** coarctation of the aorta.
   - **Why:** narrowing past the arm vessels raises pressure in the arms and lowers it in the legs. [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK544305/)
   - **Trap:** choosing renal artery stenosis; it does not cause an arm-leg pressure difference.
2. A 45-year-old woman has paroxysmal headaches, sweating and palpitations with blood pressure spikes. Best screening test?
   - **Answer:** plasma free metanephrines (or 24-hour urine fractionated metanephrines).
   - **Why:** pheochromocytoma; metanephrine tests have sensitivity above 95%. [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK544305/)
   - **Trap:** ordering imaging first; confirm biochemically, then localize with CT or MRI.
3. A 75-year-old with coronary disease has new resistant hypertension and an abdominal bruit. Most likely secondary cause?
   - **Answer:** atherosclerotic renal artery stenosis.
   - **Why:** the leading secondary cause in older adults with generalized atherosclerosis. [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK544305/)
   - **Trap:** fibromuscular dysplasia is the young-woman cause.
4. An 8-year-old has hypertension. What category of cause is most likely?
   - **Answer:** kidney (renal parenchymal) disease.
   - **Why:** 70-85% of hypertension in children under 12 is secondary, mostly renal. [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK544305/)
   - **Trap:** assuming primary (essential) hypertension, which is the adult default.
