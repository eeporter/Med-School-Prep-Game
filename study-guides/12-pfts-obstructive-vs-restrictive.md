# Pulmonary function tests: obstructive vs restrictive, plus ABGs

Exam focus: USMLE Step 1 and NBME · Study material, not patient care guidance · Auditor: cleared 2026-10-27
From Elissa's question log: "How are restrictive and obstructive lung diseases differentiated using pulmonary function tests (PFTs) and arterial blood gas (ABG) analysis?"
Based on your notes: El-Husseiny's Essentials of Respiratory System (PFTs pp. 19-24, diffusion p. 41, alveolar gas equation p. 43, A-a gradient p. 59, COPD pp. 89-90, restrictive disease p. 97). Each fact is also checked against StatPearls and the MSD Manual; where a different source disagrees with your notes, a marked note says so.

## In one line
Obstruction traps air (low FEV1/FVC, normal or high lung volumes); restriction shrinks the lungs (low TLC, normal or high FEV1/FVC); then DLCO tells you whether the gas-exchange membrane itself is damaged.

## Core mechanism
1. **FEV1** is the air blown out in the first second; **FVC** is the total blown out. Narrow or floppy airways slow the first second more than the total, so FEV1/FVC falls. Exams use below 0.70 to define obstruction; the 2022 ERS/ATS standard instead grades results by z-score (lower limit of normal, z below −1.645), and the ATS recommends race-neutral GLI equations ([MSD Manual: Airflow, Lung Volumes, and Flow-Volume Loop](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pulmonary-disorders/tests-of-pulmonary-function-pft/airflow-lung-volumes-and-flow-volume-loop)). [StatPearls: Pulmonary Function Tests](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK482339/)
2. Stiff lungs or a weak or rigid chest wall cannot fill, so all volumes shrink. **Total lung capacity (TLC) below 80% predicted** defines restriction on exams (MSD and the 2022 ERS/ATS standard use TLC below the lower limit of normal; [MSD Manual: Airflow, Lung Volumes, and Flow-Volume Loop](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pulmonary-disorders/tests-of-pulmonary-function-pft/airflow-lung-volumes-and-flow-volume-loop)). FEV1 and FVC fall together, so the ratio is normal or high. [StatPearls: PFTs](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK482339/), [MSD Manual table](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/multimedia/table/characteristic-physiologic-changes-associated-with-pulmonary-disorders)
3. In obstruction, air stays behind, so residual volume (RV) and TLC are normal or high (hyperinflation). [MSD Manual table](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/multimedia/table/characteristic-physiologic-changes-associated-with-pulmonary-disorders)
4. **DLCO** (diffusing capacity for carbon monoxide) measures how well gas crosses from alveoli into blood. It falls when alveolar surface or capillaries are lost (emphysema, pulmonary fibrosis, pulmonary vascular disease). [MSD Manual: Gas Exchange](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pulmonary-disorders/tests-of-pulmonary-function-pft/measurement-of-gas-exchange)

5. **Your notes' version (El-Husseiny Respiratory, pp. 19-24):** normal FEV1/FVC is about 0.8. In obstruction (chronic bronchitis, asthma, emphysema) TLC is normal or high, but from TLC a smaller volume comes out slowly. In restriction (ARDS, sarcoidosis, idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis) all volumes fall, but the smaller volume comes out fast and completely, so FEV1/FVC is often **high**. A low FEV1/FVC means obstruction; a normal or high ratio suggests restriction, but a **low TLC is what proves it**. FVC falls whenever function is badly impaired.
6. **Why the ratio is high in fibrosis (p. 24):** stiff fibrotic tissue pulls the airways open (radial traction), so resistance falls and flow is supernormal for the lung volume. On a flow-volume loop, obstruction starts and ends at high volumes with a "scooped" downslope; restriction starts and ends at low volumes, with flow a bit higher than normal at any given volume (pp. 23-24).
7. **Aging (p. 21):** the chest wall stiffens (rib calcification) while the lung loses recoil, so RV rises, FVC falls a little and TLC stays about the same.

## Compare and contrast
| Measure | Obstructive | Restrictive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FEV1/FVC | Low (below 0.70) | Normal or high |
| FEV1 | Low | Low, normal or high (MSD: "decreased, normal, or increased") |
| FVC | Low or normal | Low |
| TLC | Normal or high | Low (below 80%) |
| RV | Normal or high | Low |

[MSD Manual table](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/multimedia/table/characteristic-physiologic-changes-associated-with-pulmonary-disorders), [StatPearls: PFTs](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK482339/)

## DLCO sorts diseases within each pattern
| Pattern + DLCO | Think of |
| --- | --- |
| Obstruction + low DLCO | Emphysema (alveolar walls destroyed) |
| Obstruction + normal or high DLCO, reverses with bronchodilator | Asthma (DLCO can be high) |
| Restriction + low DLCO | Pulmonary fibrosis / interstitial lung disease |
| Restriction + normal DLCO | Problem outside the lung, with a normal A-a gradient: poor muscle effort (polio, myasthenia gravis, Guillain-Barré) or a poor frame (scoliosis, morbid obesity) (El-Husseiny Respiratory, p. 97) |
| Normal spirometry + low DLCO | Pulmonary vascular disease (pulmonary hypertension, emboli), early ILD, **anemia** (corrects when adjusted for hemoglobin) |

[StatPearls: PFTs](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK482339/), [MSD Manual: Gas Exchange](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pulmonary-disorders/tests-of-pulmonary-function-pft/measurement-of-gas-exchange)

Your notes add: **chronic bronchitis** usually has a **normal DLCO** (the alveoli are intact; Reid index above 50%), while **emphysema** lowers DLCO by destroying alveoli and their capillaries (El-Husseiny Respiratory, pp. 89-90). Interstitial lung diseases (idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, pneumoconioses, hypersensitivity pneumonitis, ARDS, neonatal RDS, sarcoidosis) are the restrictive group with low DLCO and a high A-a gradient (p. 97). Diffusion falls with less surface area (emphysema, lobe removal) or a thicker membrane (fibrosis, edema); CO2 is about 20 times more soluble than O2, so it diffuses faster (p. 41). Obesity mainly lowers expiratory reserve volume and FRC; RV changes little (p. 97).

## ABGs and the A-a gradient
- The alveolar-arterial (A-a) oxygen gradient is the difference between alveolar O₂ (PAO₂) and arterial O₂ (PaO₂); normal is under about 20 mmHg and rises with age (estimate: age/4 + 4, standard teaching). [MSD Manual: Gas Exchange](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pulmonary-disorders/tests-of-pulmonary-function-pft/measurement-of-gas-exchange)
- **Other source (normal A-a gradient):** your notes say a normal A-a gradient "does not exceed 10-15 mmHg", with a normal PAO₂ of about 100 and PaO₂ above 92 mmHg (El-Husseiny Respiratory, p. 59). MSD uses under about 20 and notes it rises with age. A young patient with a gradient of 25 is abnormal by either rule.
- Your notes: **high altitude** and **hypoventilation** are the two causes of hypoxemia with a normal A-a gradient; PaCO₂ tells them apart (low at altitude from hyperventilation, high with hypoventilation). Supplemental O₂ corrects both (El-Husseiny Respiratory, p. 59). The alveolar gas equation depends on atmospheric pressure, inspired O₂ fraction (normally 0.21) and alveolar CO₂ (normally 40) (p. 43).
- Hypoxemia with a **normal** A-a gradient: hypoventilation (for example opioids, neuromuscular disease) or low inspired O₂ (altitude). [MSD Manual](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pulmonary-disorders/tests-of-pulmonary-function-pft/measurement-of-gas-exchange)
- Hypoxemia with a **high** A-a gradient: V/Q mismatch, right-to-left shunt, or severe diffusion impairment, which covers most lung diseases. [MSD Manual](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pulmonary-disorders/tests-of-pulmonary-function-pft/measurement-of-gas-exchange)
- How the ABG separates restriction types: interstitial lung disease gives low PaO₂ with a **high** A-a gradient, and usually a low PaCO₂ because the patient breathes fast. Neuromuscular or chest-wall restriction gives hypoventilation: **high** PaCO₂ with a **normal** A-a gradient. The A-a rules are from [MSD Manual: Gas Exchange](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pulmonary-disorders/tests-of-pulmonary-function-pft/measurement-of-gas-exchange); the PaCO₂ patterns are standard teaching.
- Chronic obstruction with CO₂ retention gives chronic respiratory acidosis with renal compensation. See [01-acid-base.md](01-acid-base.md).

## Clinical vignette
A 62-year-old with a 40 pack-year smoking history has dyspnea. FEV1/FVC 0.55, TLC 120% predicted, RV increased, DLCO 45% predicted, little change after albuterol. Diagnosis?

**Answer:** emphysema (COPD).
**Why:** low ratio = obstruction; high TLC and RV = air trapping; low DLCO = loss of alveolar surface; no bronchodilator reversal points away from asthma. [StatPearls: PFTs](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK482339/)
**Trap:** choosing asthma; asthma reverses with bronchodilator and has normal or high DLCO.

## Common traps
- Restriction is defined by TLC, not by FVC alone; a low FVC can occur in obstruction too.
- A normal FEV1/FVC does not mean normal lungs; check TLC and DLCO.
- In restriction the FEV1/FVC is normal or **high** because elastic recoil pushes air out fast.
- Hypoventilation raises PaCO₂ but leaves the A-a gradient normal.
- Chronic bronchitis has a **normal** DLCO; only emphysema lowers it (El-Husseiny Respiratory, pp. 89-90).
- Low TLC, not a high FEV1/FVC, is what diagnoses restriction (El-Husseiny Respiratory, p. 21).

## Practice questions
1. A 55-year-old with dry crackles has FEV1/FVC 0.88, TLC 62% predicted and low DLCO. Pattern and likely disease?
   - **Answer:** restrictive, from pulmonary fibrosis (interstitial lung disease).
   - **Why:** low TLC = restriction; low DLCO = parenchymal damage to the gas-exchange surface. [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK482339/), [MSD Manual](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pulmonary-disorders/tests-of-pulmonary-function-pft/measurement-of-gas-exchange)
   - **Trap:** the high ratio is not "better than normal"; it is typical of restriction.
2. A patient with an opioid overdose has PaO₂ 60 and PaCO₂ 70 mmHg on room air. What is the A-a gradient likely to be?
   - **Answer:** normal.
   - **Why:** hypoventilation lowers alveolar O₂ and arterial O₂ equally. At sea level, PAO₂ ≈ 150 − PaCO₂/0.8 = 150 − 87.5 = 62.5, so A-a ≈ 2.5. [MSD Manual](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pulmonary-disorders/tests-of-pulmonary-function-pft/measurement-of-gas-exchange)
   - **Trap:** assuming every hypoxemia means lung disease. (The simplified alveolar gas equation with 150 and R = 0.8 is standard teaching; the MSD page lists the inputs but shows the equation as an image.)
3. A young woman with episodic wheeze has FEV1/FVC 0.62 that rises to 0.78 after albuterol, and a slightly high DLCO. Diagnosis?
   - **Answer:** asthma.
   - **Why:** obstruction that normalizes after bronchodilator; DLCO can be high in asthma. [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK482339/), [MSD Manual: Gas Exchange](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pulmonary-disorders/tests-of-pulmonary-function-pft/measurement-of-gas-exchange)
   - **Trap:** emphysema has low DLCO and little reversibility.
4. A patient with normal spirometry and lung volumes has an isolated low DLCO. What should you think of?
   - **Answer:** pulmonary vascular disease (pulmonary hypertension or chronic emboli), or early ILD.
   - **Why:** the airways and volumes are fine, but blood-gas transfer is impaired. [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK482339/), [MSD Manual](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pulmonary-disorders/tests-of-pulmonary-function-pft/measurement-of-gas-exchange)
   - **Trap:** assuming normal spirometry rules out lung pathology.
