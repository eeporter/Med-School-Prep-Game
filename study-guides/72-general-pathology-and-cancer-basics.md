# General pathology and cancer basics

Exam focus: USMLE Step 1 and NBME · Study material, not patient care guidance · Auditor: cleared 2026-11-02
Based on your notes: El-Husseiny's Essentials: General Principles (cell death and injury pp. 2-10, inflammation and repair pp. 11-17, adaptation and neoplasia pp. 18-28). Each fact is also checked against StatPearls, the MSD Manual, the National Cancer Institute and the American Cancer Society; where a newer or different source disagrees with your notes, a marked note says so.

## In one line
Injured cells either adapt, recover or die. **Necrosis** is messy and inflamed; **apoptosis** is tidy and needs ATP. **Reversible injury** is mainly swelling; **membrane breakdown** marks the point of no return. Cancer is a stepwise series of DNA mutations that turn on **oncogenes** (one hit needed) and turn off **tumor suppressors** (two hits needed).

## Core mechanism
1. **Reversible injury:** less ATP means the Na+/K+ and calcium pumps fail, so the cell swells. Swelling is the earliest visible sign (El-Husseiny General Principles, p. 7).
2. **Irreversible injury:** the plasma membrane breaks, enzymes such as troponin leak out, calcium enters, mitochondria fail and lysosomes rupture (p. 7).
3. **Nuclear death:** pyknosis (condensing), then karyorrhexis (fragmenting), then karyolysis (dissolving) (p. 7).
4. **Free radicals** damage cells through lipid peroxidation, protein changes and DNA breaks. Catalase, superoxide dismutase and glutathione peroxidase remove them, as do vitamins A, C and E (p. 10).
5. **Neoplasia** moves from normal cells through dysplasia to carcinoma in situ, invasive carcinoma and metastasis. Invasion means crossing the basement membrane (p. 19).

## High-yield facts

### Necrosis patterns (pp. 2-4)
| Type | What it looks like | Classic setting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coagulative | Firm; cell outlines kept, nuclei gone | Infarct of any organ except the brain |
| Liquefactive | Liquid, from enzymes | Brain infarct, abscess, pancreatitis |
| Caseous | Soft, "cottage cheese" | Tuberculosis, fungal granulomas |
| Fat | Chalky white, from calcium soaps | Pancreatitis, breast trauma |
| Fibrinoid | Bright pink vessel wall | Malignant hypertension, vasculitis |
| Gangrenous | Dry, mummified; wet if infected | Lower limb and GI tract ischemia |

StatPearls adds that necrosis is usually inflamed and does not normally activate caspases. [StatPearls: Necrosis Pathology](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK557627/)

### Apoptosis (pp. 4-5)
- Uses ATP and caspases. The membrane stays intact, so there is little inflammation.
- DNA fragments form a "ladder" in multiples of 180 base pairs.
- **Intrinsic pathway:** runs through the mitochondria. BAX and BAK form pores, cytochrome c leaks out and caspases turn on. **Bcl-2 blocks this**. Too much Bcl-2 from t(14;18) drives follicular lymphoma.
- **Extrinsic pathway:** Fas ligand binds Fas, or TNF-α binds its receptor. Cytotoxic T cells use perforin and granzyme B. Faulty Fas signaling causes autoimmune lymphoproliferative syndrome.

### Calcification (p. 6)
| | Dystrophic | Metastatic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where | Dead or damaged tissue | Normal tissue, widespread |
| Serum calcium | Usually normal | Usually high |
| Examples | Calcific aortic stenosis, TB, fat necrosis, psammoma bodies | Primary hyperparathyroidism, sarcoidosis, vitamin D excess, kidney failure |
| Typical sites | At the injury | Kidney, lung, stomach lining |

### Ischemia and infarcts (pp. 8-9)
- **Most vulnerable sites:** brain border zones, left ventricular subendocardium, proximal tubule straight segment and thick ascending limb, liver zone III, and colon at the splenic flexure and rectum.
- **Most vulnerable neurons:** cerebellar Purkinje cells and hippocampal pyramidal cells.
- **Red infarct:** venous blockage, tissues with more than one blood supply (liver, lung, intestine, testis), and reperfusion.
- **Pale infarct:** solid organs with one arterial supply (heart, kidney).

### Inflammation (pp. 11-14)
- **Vasodilation (redness, warmth):** histamine, prostaglandins, bradykinin, nitric oxide.
- **More vascular permeability (swelling):** leukotrienes C4, D4, E4, histamine, serotonin.
- **Pain:** bradykinin, prostaglandin E2, histamine.
- **Fever:** IL-1 and TNF raise COX activity in the hypothalamus, so PGE2 raises the set point.
- **Leukocyte exit from blood (p. 14):** rolling (selectins, sialyl Lewis X), tight binding (ICAM-1 with integrins such as LFA-1), diapedesis (PECAM-1), then migration (C5a, IL-8, LTB4). Adhesion deficiency type 1 lacks the CD18 integrin subunit; type 2 lacks sialyl Lewis X.

### Acute-phase proteins and ESR (p. 12)
- **Up:** ferritin, fibrinogen, serum amyloid A, hepcidin, C-reactive protein.
- **Down:** albumin, transferrin.
- IL-6 is the main driver, and the liver makes them (p. 11). [StatPearls: Physiology, Acute Phase Reactants](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK519570/)
- **Hepcidin** lowers iron absorption and release, which causes anemia of chronic disease.
- **ESR up:** fibrinogen coats red cells, reducing their negative charge so they clump and fall faster. Seen with infection, inflammation, cancer, most anemias, kidney disease and pregnancy. [StatPearls: Erythrocyte Sedimentation Rate](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK557485/)
- **ESR down:** sickle cell anemia, polycythemia, heart failure, microcytosis, low fibrinogen.

### Exudate vs transudate (p. 13)
| | Exudate | Transudate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Look | Cloudy, cellular | Clear, few cells |
| Protein | Over 2.9 g/dL | Under 2.5 g/dL |
| Cause | Inflammation, infection, cancer, lymph blockage | High hydrostatic pressure (heart failure), low oncotic pressure (cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome) |

**Light criteria:** exudate if at least one is met: protein ratio over 0.5, LDH ratio over 0.6, or fluid LDH over 2/3 of the upper normal serum LDH. [MSD Manual: Pleural Effusion](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pulmonary-disorders/mediastinal-and-pleural-disorders/pleural-effusion)

### Wound healing and scars (pp. 15-16)
- **Inflammatory phase (up to day 3):** platelets, neutrophils, macrophages.
- **Proliferative phase (day 3 to weeks):** fibroblasts and myofibroblasts lay down granulation tissue and **type III collagen**. Vitamin C or copper deficiency delays it.
- **Remodeling phase (1 week to 6+ months):** type III is replaced by **type I collagen**. Collagenases need zinc, so zinc deficiency delays healing.
- Tensile strength returns to about 70-80% at 3 months, then improves very little.

| | Hypertrophic scar | Keloid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Collagen | Type III, parallel | Types I and III, disorganized |
| Extent | Stays within the wound | Grows beyond the wound |
| Recurrence | Infrequent | Frequent |
| Risk | None specific | Darker skin |

[StatPearls: Hypertrophic Scarring and Keloids](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK537058/) agrees on borders and adds that hypertrophic scars may regress.

### Lipofuscin (p. 16)
A yellow-brown "wear and tear" pigment from lipid breakdown, seen with normal aging, especially in the heart, colon, liver and kidney. It builds up in cells that no longer divide. [StatPearls: Histology, Lipofuscin](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK537358/)

### Amyloidosis (p. 17)
Misfolded proteins form β-pleated sheets that show **apple-green birefringence with Congo red** under polarized light. [MSD Manual: Amyloidosis](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/amyloidosis/amyloidosis)

| Type | Protein | Setting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AL | Immunoglobulin light chain | Multiple myeloma and other plasma cell disorders |
| AA | Serum amyloid A | Chronic inflammation such as rheumatoid arthritis, IBD, familial Mediterranean fever |
| Dialysis-related | β2-microglobulin | End-stage kidney disease, long-term dialysis; carpal tunnel syndrome |
| Alzheimer disease | β-amyloid | Brain |
| Type 2 diabetes | Islet amyloid polypeptide | Pancreatic islets |
| Medullary thyroid cancer | Calcitonin | Thyroid |
| Isolated atrial | Atrial natriuretic peptide | Aging atria |
| Wild-type (senile) | Normal transthyretin | Heart ventricles in older adults |
| Hereditary | Mutated transthyretin | Restrictive cardiomyopathy, polyneuropathy |

### Cell adaptation (p. 18)
- **Hypertrophy:** bigger cells. **Hyperplasia:** more cells. **Atrophy:** smaller or fewer cells.
- **Metaplasia:** one adult cell type replaces another, such as Barrett esophagus or smokers' bronchial squamous change. It can be reversible.
- **Dysplasia:** disordered, precancerous growth. It is not a true adaptation. Mild and moderate forms may regress, but severe dysplasia often progresses to carcinoma in situ.
- [National Cancer Institute: What Is Cancer?](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/understanding/what-is-cancer) separates hyperplasia (normal-looking cells) from dysplasia (abnormal-looking cells).

### Tumor naming and staging (p. 20)
| Cell type | Benign | Malignant |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Epithelium | Adenoma, papilloma | Adenocarcinoma, papillary carcinoma |
| Blood vessels | Hemangioma | Angiosarcoma |
| Smooth muscle | Leiomyoma | Leiomyosarcoma |
| Striated muscle | Rhabdomyoma | Rhabdomyosarcoma |
| Connective tissue | Fibroma | Fibrosarcoma |
| Bone | Osteoma | Osteosarcoma |
| Fat | Lipoma | Liposarcoma |
| Melanocyte | Nevus (mole) | Melanoma |

- **Hamartoma:** disorganized overgrowth of tissue in its normal place, such as a Peutz-Jeghers polyp.
- **Choristoma:** normal tissue in the wrong place, such as gastric tissue in a Meckel diverticulum.
- **Grade:** how abnormal the cells look under a microscope. **Stage:** how far it has spread (TNM). **Stage matters more for prognosis**, and the N and M parts usually matter most.

### Hallmarks of cancer (pp. 21-22)
| Hallmark | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Growth signal self-sufficiency | Mutated HER2/neu, RAS, MYC |
| Insensitivity to anti-growth signals | Rb mutation, loss of E-cadherin contact inhibition |

MSD covers telomerase, VEGF and immune evasion. [MSD Manual: Cellular and Molecular Basis of Cancer](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/hematology-and-oncology/overview-of-cancer/cellular-and-molecular-basis-of-cancer)

**Other source:** a 2024 review describes the Warburg effect as glucose converted to lactate despite oxygen **and functional mitochondria**. Do not assume that mitochondria are broken. [Barba et al., Int J Mol Sci 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10970388/)

### Immune checkpoints (p. 22)
- **PD-1 on T cells binds PD-L1 on tumor cells**, exhausting the T cell. Blocked by pembrolizumab and nivolumab (PD-1), and atezolizumab, durvalumab and avelumab (PD-L1).
- **CTLA-4 outcompetes CD28 for B7**, removing the T cell's costimulatory signal. Blocked by ipilimumab.
- [National Cancer Institute: Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/types/immunotherapy/checkpoint-inhibitors)

### Common metastases (p. 23)
- **Brain:** lung > breast > melanoma, colon, kidney. About half of brain tumors are metastases, often at the gray-white junction.
- **Liver:** colon >> stomach > pancreas. The liver and lung are the most common sites overall.
- **Bone:** prostate, breast > kidney, thyroid, lung. Bone metastases are far more common than primary bone tumors.
- **Lytic:** thyroid, kidney, non-small cell lung, multiple myeloma. **Blastic:** prostate, small cell lung. **Mixed:** breast.
- **Most carcinomas spread by lymphatics; most sarcomas spread by blood.** Your notes' exceptions, carcinomas that spread by blood: follicular thyroid, choriocarcinoma, renal cell and hepatocellular.

## Compare and contrast: necrosis vs apoptosis
| | Necrosis | Apoptosis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Energy | No ATP needed | ATP needed |
| Number of cells | Large groups | Single cells or small groups |
| Inflammation | Yes | Little or none |
| Membrane | Ruptures | Stays intact, blebs |
| Cell size | Swells | Shrinks |
| Physiologic? | Never | Can be |

Source: El-Husseiny, pp. 2, 4; [StatPearls: Necrosis Pathology](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK557627/).

## Clinical vignette
A 58-year-old woman on long-term dialysis has numbness in both hands at night. A tissue biopsy shows pink deposits with apple-green birefringence under polarized light after Congo red staining. What protein makes up the deposits?

**Answer:** β2-microglobulin.
**Why:** long-term dialysis is the setting, and the Congo red result shows amyloid. (El-Husseiny, p. 17; [MSD Manual: Amyloidosis](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/amyloidosis/amyloidosis))
**Trap:** light chains, which would need a plasma cell disorder.

## Common traps
- **Brain infarcts are liquefactive**, not coagulative.
- **Dystrophic calcification** has normal serum calcium.
- **Metaplasia can be reversible.** Carcinoma in situ has not crossed the basement membrane.
- **Grade is what it looks like; stage is where it has gone.**
- **Red infarcts:** lung, liver, intestine, testis. **Pale infarcts:** heart, kidney.
- **CRP and fibrinogen go up; albumin and transferrin go down.**
- **Sickle cell disease lowers the ESR.**
- **Type III collagen comes first; type I replaces it.**
- **Tumor markers track disease**; they do not diagnose it alone.

## Practice questions
1. A 62-year-old man dies 3 days after a heart attack. The heart shows firm tissue where muscle cell outlines remain, without nuclei. What type of cell death is this?
   - **Answer:** coagulative necrosis.
   - **Why:** ischemia in organs other than the brain gives coagulative necrosis. (El-Husseiny, p. 2)
   - **Trap:** liquefactive necrosis, which occurs in brain infarcts.
2. A 40-year-old has a lymph node biopsy showing a follicular lymphoma with t(14;18). What is the effect of the translocation?
   - **Answer:** too much Bcl-2 blocks intrinsic apoptosis.
   - **Why:** Bcl-2 keeps the mitochondrial membrane closed to cytochrome c. (El-Husseiny, p. 5)
   - **Trap:** c-MYC activation, which is t(8;14) in Burkitt lymphoma (see guide 41).
3. A 55-year-old woman with primary hyperparathyroidism has calcium deposits in normal kidney and lung tissue. What type of calcification is this?
   - **Answer:** metastatic calcification.
   - **Why:** high serum calcium drives deposits in normal tissue. (El-Husseiny, p. 6)
   - **Trap:** dystrophic calcification, which needs damaged tissue and has normal calcium.
4. A 60-year-old man with lung cancer has confusion, low sodium, concentrated urine and normal volume status. What is the tumor type?
   - **Answer:** small cell lung cancer, producing ADH.
   - **Why:** (El-Husseiny, p. 28; [MSD Manual: Paraneoplastic Syndromes](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/hematology-and-oncology/overview-of-cancer/paraneoplastic-syndromes))
   - **Trap:** squamous cell carcinoma, which is linked to PTHrP and high calcium.
5. A patient with recurrent infections, delayed umbilical cord separation and no pus has a defect in CD18. Which step of leukocyte movement fails?
   - **Answer:** tight binding (adhesion) to the endothelium.
   - **Why:** CD18 is the integrin subunit used for tight binding. (El-Husseiny, p. 14) Your Microbiology and Immunology notes give the delayed cord separation and lack of pus (p. 472).
   - **Trap:** rolling, which uses selectins and fails in adhesion deficiency type 2.
6. A shipyard worker who smokes develops a lung tumor. Which cancer is most likely?
   - **Answer:** bronchogenic carcinoma.
   - **Why:** your notes rank bronchogenic carcinoma above mesothelioma for asbestos (p. 25), and MSD lists both. [MSD Manual: Common Chemical Carcinogens](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/multimedia/table/common-chemical-carcinogens)
   - **Trap:** choosing mesothelioma because it is the most specific asbestos cancer.
